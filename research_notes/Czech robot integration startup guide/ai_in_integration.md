## 1. Breakdown of engineering hours in a typical robot cell project; which phases dominate cost and schedule

### Takeaway
No authoritative, phase-by-phase hour breakdown was found; published splits come from integrator and vendor guides and are inconsistent. The consistent picture is that the robot arm is a minority of cell cost (roughly a quarter to 40%), engineering/software/integration is roughly 20-50%, and on-site installation/commissioning is a further 5-25% of cost but a disproportionate share of schedule risk, because most control-software debugging happens there.

### Cited Findings
- Integrator guide (Unchained Robotics, Germany): hardware roughly 40-70% of a turnkey budget, engineering/software/integration 20-45%, installation/commissioning/training 10-25%; the engineering share shrinks for standardised cells and grows for novel processes; fixtures, image processing/sensors and integration/programming are separate budget items — [Unchained Robotics blog](https://blog.unchainedrobotics.de/en/how-much-does-a-turnkey-robotics-solution-cost)
- Another guide gives a cell-level table: robot arm and controller 30-40%, controls/integration/programming 25-35%, commissioning and training 5-8% (the same guide elsewhere lists installation and commissioning at 15-20%, so it is internally inconsistent). Attribution among the guide pages returned was not individually verified — [irecruit cost-per-cell guide](https://www.irecruit.co/guides/robot-integration-cost-per-cell); [GrabARobot integration cost guide 2026](https://grabarobot.com/blog/robot-integration-cost-guide-2026/)
- "Integration typically accounts for 30 to 50 percent of total project cost" (vendor statement) — [Inbolt, "What are integration costs?"](https://www.inbolt.com/resources/what-are-integration-costs); robot arm is only about 25-40% of the finished cell — [Standard Bots price breakdown](https://standardbots.com/articles/how-much-do-robots-cost)
- Rule of thumb reported from Engineering.com: integration including safety, tooling and programming costs a multiple of the robot's own price (search summary: "about four to six times"; verify against the article) — [Engineering.com](https://www.engineering.com/time-and-money-how-much-do-industrial-robots-cost/)
- Hours and rates quoted in integrator guides: 150-400 hours to design, program and commission a single cell at about $125-250/hour; SME integration projects "typically run 200-800 engineering hours"; application programming (paths, force-control tuning, vision integration) estimated at $15,000-80,000 — [GrabARobot](https://grabarobot.com/blog/robot-integration-cost-guide-2026/); [irecruit](https://www.irecruit.co/guides/robot-integration-cost-per-cell)
- One integrator timeline: engineering 6-10 weeks, build and assembly 8-14 weeks, installation and commissioning 4-6 weeks — [LANPDT Tech Talk episode 19](https://lanpdt.com/tech-talk-episode-19/)
- Commissioning "can take up to 15-20% of the total delivery time of an automation system project", and the most time-consuming part of commissioning and ramp-up is software implementation or redesign — [Visual Components blog](https://www.visualcomponents.com/blog/increasing-control-software-quality-with-virtual-commissioning/)
- Dassault blog (no source given): commissioning is about 25% of a project timeline and about 60% of control-software debugging happens during commissioning — [Dassault Systèmes DELMIA blog](https://blog.3ds.com/brands/delmia/virtual-commissioning-the-game-changer-for-industrial-equipment-manufacturing/)
- Virtual commissioning results (mostly vendor case studies): Wipro PARI cut on-site commissioning time by 70% — [Siemens case study](https://resources.sw.siemens.com/en-US/case-study-wipro-pari/); up to 40% — [Kalypso](https://kalypso.com/viewpoints/entry/reducing-commissioning-time-by-40-with-a-digital-twin); a special-machinery maker showed 21% shorter turnaround and 85% of surveyed firms named shorter commissioning as the main benefit — [machineering](https://www.machineering.com/en/virtual-commissioning-why-the-investment-often-pays-for-itself-in-just-three-months/); an academic test system (17 sensors, 10 actuators) had real commissioning time cut by 75% with requirement fulfilment rising from 37% to 84% — [Visual Components blog](https://www.visualcomponents.com/blog/increasing-control-software-quality-with-virtual-commissioning/)

### Inferences
- If engineering plus commissioning is roughly 30-50% of a cell's price and a single cell consumes roughly 150-800 engineering hours, then even a 30-50% reduction in desk-engineering hours (PLC, robot program, documentation, quoting) moves total project cost by perhaps 5-15%, not by half. The larger lever is schedule: fewer weeks of engineering before build, and fewer on-site days if software arrives pre-tested against a simulation.
- The phases where text-based AI can plausibly act (PLC code, robot code, documentation, quoting, electrical-design automation) are the ones done at a desk. Build, assembly, installation and on-site commissioning (roughly 12-20 of the 18-30 weeks in the timeline above) are physical and are not shortened by LLMs directly.
- Standardisation matters more than AI for the engineering share: the Unchained Robotics note that engineering share shrinks for standardised cells suggests that an AI-first integrator gets most leverage when AI is applied to a constrained, repeatable cell family rather than bespoke one-offs.

### Gaps
- No independent (VDMA, A3, IFR, academic) breakdown of hours by discipline (mechanical design vs electrical design vs PLC vs robot vs vision vs safety documentation) was found. The figures above are from marketing-oriented guides and should be validated against real integrator quotes or interviews.
- No Czech or Central European hourly-rate or hour-split data was found in this session.

## 2. LLMs for PLC code: research evidence, vendor copilots, measured gains, limitations, editable project formats

### Takeaway
By late 2026 every major PLC vendor ships or has announced an agentic assistant (Siemens Eigen Engineering Agent is generally available; CODESYS sells an MCP server; Beckhoff has announced TwinCAT CoAgent; Rockwell and Schneider have copilots), and third-party MCP bridges let general coding agents such as Claude Code drive TIA Portal. Structured Text generation for easy, well-specified tasks is good; ladder/FBD generation, hard tasks, behaviour under real plant dynamics and safety code remain weak or explicitly excluded, and all productivity numbers found are vendor claims rather than independent measurements.

### Cited Findings
Research and benchmarks
- LLM4PLC (ICSE 2024 SEIP; arXiv Jan 2024): a pipeline with grammar checkers, the MATIEC compiler and SMV model checking raised generation success from 47% to 72% and expert-rated code quality from 2.25/10 to 7.75/10. Dated: uses 2023-era models — [arXiv 2401.05443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05443v1); [ICSE 2024 listing](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icse-2024/icse-2024-software-engineering-in-practice/32/LLM4PLC-Harnessing-Large-Language-Models-for-Verifiable-Programming-of-PLCs-in-Indus)
- Agents4PLC (arXiv Oct 2024): multi-agent closed-loop generation plus formal verification with a 23-task benchmark from natural-language requirements to formal specs; search summary reports best pass rates of 50% (Easy) and 28.6% (Medium) with GPT-4o, where "pass" means at least 80% of specified properties hold. Dated: 2024 models — [arXiv 2410.14209](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.14209)
- AutoPLC (arXiv Dec 2024, revised): vendor-aware ST generation for CODESYS ST and Siemens SCL with a 914 requirement-code-pair benchmark (earlier benchmarks were as small as a 40-sample OSCAT subset); reported 78% formal-verification pass rate on the Agents4PLC benchmark — [arXiv 2412.02410](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.02410v2)
- Graphical languages are under-researched: "recent researchers have focused on Structured Text while overlooking the most commonly used LD, SFC and FBD" (Oct 2024) — [arXiv 2410.15200](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.15200v1)
- TU Dresden comparative study (AUTOMATION 2026 congress; not peer-reviewed per search summary): Gemini 2.5 Pro, DeepSeek V3 and Grok V3 generating ST and FBD in PLCopen XML for direct import, scored on import success, compilation and functional pass rate; FBD generation "remains more error-prone" — [TU Dresden publication record](https://fis.tu-dresden.de/portal/en/publications/generation-of-plc-code-using-large-language-models-a-comparative-study(73161f03-7513-46b1-a4b1-94ca2d14d188).html)
- FH Südwestfalen prompting benchmark: 21 prompting techniques on 25 real-world use cases; ST programs reached near-perfect rubric scores — [FH SWF publication](https://publikationen.fhb.fh-swf.de/receive/fhswf_mods_00002966)
- PLCWorld (arXiv Oct 2026): closed-loop benchmark that runs generated ST against simulated plant behaviour, scoring Task Success and Safety Violation separately; 100 tasks, 473 task-condition pairs, six LLMs and four generation/verification methods (LLM4PLC, Agents4PLC, AutoPLC among them). Headline: direct GPT-5.5 reaches 82.70% Task Success on Easy cases but 25.10% on Hard cases; the paper defines an "Execution Gap" between code that passes acceptance checks and code that later fails the task or violates safety — [arXiv 2610.02982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02982); community port: [AutoFactory-Bench](https://github.com/Koomook/autofactory-bench)
- SemaPLC (arXiv Aug 2026): a "project-grounded, verification-gated agent harness" that only declares a task complete when logged external checks (specification, integrated compilation, behaviour on a live runtime, per-scan recording) confirm it; evaluated on a 65-task project-context track — [arXiv 2608.18565](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.18565)
- Titles only (content not retrieved): "Compilation Is Not Correctness: Scan-by-Scan Evaluation of LLM-Generated PLC Programs" — [arXiv 2610.28675](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28675); PLCBench, a security-oriented benchmark on whether autonomous LLM agents can turn PLC access into physical impact — [arXiv 2608.26882](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26882v1)

### Inferences
- The research trajectory from 2024 to 2026 is consistent: raw generation was never the hard part; the gain comes from the harness (compile, static check, model-check, simulate, iterate). PLCWorld's 83% Easy vs 25% Hard result shows that current frontier models with no harness are not safe to trust on non-trivial sequences, and SemaPLC shows the research frontier has moved to exactly the kind of verification-gated agent loop a harness engineer would build.
- Vendors have converged on MCP as the integration surface (CODESYS product, Beckhoff CoAgent, third-party TIA servers). That lowers the barrier for an independent team to build its own agent workflow on top of standard engineering tools without waiting for vendor copilots, and CODESYS's own statement that Claude models perform best is a signal that general frontier models, not vendor-tuned ones, are doing the work.
- The most agent-friendly stacks in late 2026 appear to be those with text-first projects: CODESYS (ST plus MCP), Beckhoff TwinCAT (see gap below), and Siemens via SIMATIC AX or Openness export/import. Rockwell's stack looked the least open to agent editing in the material found.
- Safety PLC code is fenced off by design (Rockwell documents this explicitly), so the safety program and its validation stay manual regardless of tool quality.
- Because Siemens ships Eigen to every TIA user, "we use AI to write PLC code" is not a durable differentiator in itself; any advantage would have to come from a proprietary library of verified modules, simulation-based test harnesses, and project-level context that the generic vendor agent lacks.

### Gaps
- No independently measured productivity study (time-and-motion, RCT, or third-party audit) of any PLC copilot was found. All percentage gains are vendor pilot claims.
- Beckhoff detail could not be retrieved (search budget exhausted): TwinCAT Chat history, CoAgent release status, and confirmation that TwinCAT project files (.tsproj, .TcPOU XML) are plain text suitable for Git and agent editing. From the researcher's background knowledge (not sourced in this session) TwinCAT 3 stores POUs as XML text files and TwinCAT Chat was first shown in 2023; verify before relying on it.
- Per-model results for Claude and Gemini on PLCWorld were not visible in snippets; the full paper is needed.
- No pricing was found for Siemens Eigen, and no published statement of its limits on safety (F-) programs.
- No evidence was found on LLM handling of legacy binary formats (older Rockwell .ACD, Siemens STEP 7 classic); L5X/L5K export-based workflows were not covered by results.

## 3. LLMs for robot programming (URScript, RAPID, KRL, FANUC TP/Karel, ROS 2); vendor assistants; offline programming automation

### Takeaway
Academic and thesis-level work shows LLMs can write and modify vendor robot code when paired with documentation retrieval and simulation checks, but first-party vendor assistants are still mostly guidance tools (ABB) or data-collection platforms for learned policies (Universal Robots), and no measured production results for LLM-written robot programs were found. The practical route in 2026 is an agent driving an offline-programming API (RoboDK publishes agent skills for Claude Code) with simulation as the verifier.

### Cited Findings
- arXiv case study (Nov 2025): a general-purpose LLM modifying existing ABB RAPID routines through prompting alone (changing arguments, reversing movement routines) — [arXiv 2511.11125](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11125v1); follow-on (Feb 2026) — [arXiv 2602.23331](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.23331v1)
- Chalmers master's thesis (2026): agentic system that generates, validates and iteratively corrects RAPID from natural-language task descriptions using retrieval over documentation and code templates — [Chalmers ODR](https://odr.chalmers.se/items/6e297ced-7806-4a04-a648-1019500b2432)
- MDPI Applied Sciences (April 2026): digital-twin-based verification layer for LLM-generated code across RAPID, KRL and URScript fleets; frames proprietary languages as the core obstacle — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/16/8/3883)
- Fine-tuned LLMs for URScript using a synthetic dataset built from a small set of human examples (2025) — [EAI Endorsed Transactions](https://publications.eai.eu/index.php/airo/article/view/8983)
- Industry guide (vendor-style blog, unsourced figures): LLMs produce syntactically correct RAPID or TP code that can be kinematically invalid; claims 25-40% programming-time savings in early pilots; says code-generation and task-planning layers "remain at pilot stage" while perception and simulation layers are in production — [EVS guide, May 2026](https://www.evsint.com/generative-ai-industrial-robotics-foundation-models-code-generation-2026/)
- ABB: RobotStudio AI Assistant (Sept 2025) gives real-time step-by-step programming guidance in the cloud-hosted offline programming environment and is positioned partly as a training resource — [Robotics Tomorrow](https://www.roboticstomorrow.com/news/2025/09/26/abb-robotics-adds-generative-ai-assistant-to-robotstudio/25605)
- Universal Robots: with Scale AI launched the "UR AI Trainer" at NVIDIA GTC, March 2026, deployed on UR's AI Accelerator platform: leader-follower teleoperation to capture imitation-learning data on UR robots; a large industrial dataset promised for later in 2026; a partner demo used Generalist AI's foundation model on two UR7e robots for smartphone packaging — [Universal Robots press release](https://www.universal-robots.com/news-and-media/news-center/universal-robots-scale-ai-launch-imitation-learning-system-accelerate-ai-training-lab-to-factory/); [Robotics 24/7](https://www.robotics247.com/article/nvidia-gtc-2026-universal-robots-scale-ailaunch-imitation-learning-system-to-accelerate-ai-model-training)
- FANUC: AI-assisted programming for FANUC industrial robots arrives via Vention's MachineMotion AI platform (June 2026): operators define start and end targets instead of placing waypoints — [Vention / FANUC America release](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/vention-and-fanuc-america-join-forces-to-bring-industrial-robots-to-vention-s-ai-driven-hardware-and-software-platform-827509552.html)
- KUKA: has described an "Automation 2.0" strategy of intent-driven automation; no product specifics found — [pulse.bot summary](https://www.pulse.bot/robotics/news/kuka-outlines-automation-20-strategy-combining-ai-software-with-industrial-robotics-953e2342-4fe4-4cd6-8699-3867906b6829/)
- RoboDK: publishes agent skills that teach Claude Code and other agents to build cell components, write RoboDK API code and generate controller programs for ABB, FANUC, KUKA and UR — [RoboDK GitHub](https://github.com/RoboDK); RoboDK's Python API and CAD-to-path features are the automation surface — [RoboDK API docs](https://robodk.com/doc/en/RoboDK-API.html)
- Inbolt (Paris) launched vision-enabled robot programming at Automate 2026, "closing the loop from CAD to factory floor" — [pulse.bot summary](https://www.pulse.bot/manufacturing/news/inbolt-launches-vision-enabled-robot-programming-closing-the-loop-from-cad-to-factory-floor-84412565-f43a-483e-9452-6ec458799794/)
- Skild AI announced expanded work with ABB Robotics and Universal Robots in March 2026 — [Reuters via The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/03/17/skild-ai-nvidia-deploy-robot-brain-on-blackwell-assembly-lines)

### Inferences
- Robot program text is a small part of robot-programming effort; the expensive part is getting frames, reach, collisions, cycle time and gripper/part interaction right. That is why every credible research system pairs generation with a simulator or digital twin, and why offline-programming APIs (RoboDK, vendor simulators) are the natural tool surface for a coding agent.
- Robot OEMs are investing more visibly in learned-policy infrastructure (UR AI Trainer, Skild partnerships) than in "copilot for RAPID/KRL", which leaves LLM-driven conventional programming largely to third parties and integrators.
- Platform vendors (Vention, Wandelbots, Intrinsic) are attacking the same problem by replacing vendor languages with a common Python/skill layer; an integrator standardised on one such layer plus one or two robot brands would get far more from coding agents than one supporting every OEM dialect.

### Gaps
- No measured production results (hours saved, error rates) for LLM-generated robot programs from any integrator were found.
- No information was found on KUKA or FANUC first-party LLM assistants, on Karel/TP generation quality, or on ROS 2 code generation benchmarks specific to industrial cells.
- The UR "AI copilot for the UR+ ecosystem" item came only from an unofficial mirror site and is unconfirmed.

## 4. AI in simulation, layout and cell design; code-driven CAD for fixtures and grippers; automated electrical schematics

### Takeaway
Rule-based automation (EPLAN automated project generation, CAD-to-path, virtual commissioning) is mature and delivers real savings; generative AI layered on top is at the announcement and early-product stage, and frontier models remain poor at faithful parametric CAD (best frontier score about 0.40 on BenchCAD in May 2026). No production-proven generative tool for fixture or gripper design was found.

### Cited Findings
- EPLAN: "Eplan Copilot" AI assistant is integrated in the platform; the eBuild service is now the cloud "Automated Project Generation" (schematics generated from configuration without a local install), extended to 3D mounting-plate layout from bills of material; with Microsoft, EPLAN demonstrated AI-generated mounting-plate layouts at Hannover Messe 2026 using Azure OpenAI; EPLAN estimates up to 40% time savings in planning and design (vendor estimate); Platform 2027 is the upcoming release — [EPLAN press, SPS](https://www.eplan.com/de-en/about-us/press/eplan-and-rittal-at-sps-2025-in-nuremberg); [EPLAN Hannover Messe page](https://www.eplan.com/de-en/about-us/events/hannover-messe/); [EPLAN innovations page](https://eplan-software.com/company/innovations); [PresseBox, Platform 2027](https://www.pressebox.com/pressrelease/eplan-gmbh-co-kg/now-is-best-to-test-the-eplan-platform-2027/boxid/1299033)
- Vention (Montreal): at IMTS 2026 described an agentic AI operating at system level that uses natural language to help manufacturers design automation cells in its cloud CAD, alongside "physical AI" on its MachineMotion AI controller — [Vention IMTS 2026 release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/vention-facilitates-manufacturing-at-imts-2026-with-physical-ai-and-agentic-ai-in-one-platform-302874435.html)

- BenchCAD (arXiv May 2026): 17,900 execution-verified CadQuery programs over 106 industrial part families; across more than ten frontier models, systems "often recover coarse outer geometry but fail to produce faithful parametric CAD programs"; best frontier total about 0.397 (Gemini 3.1 Pro, thinking) versus 0.6014 for a specialist model (CADEvolve v3) — [BenchCAD site](https://benchcad.github.io/BenchCAD_webpage/); [author blog](https://haozhezhang6.github.io/research/2026/05/11/benchcad.html)

### Inferences
- Electrical design is the discipline closest to "configure, then generate": EPLAN's macro/configurator-based generation predates LLMs, and an integrator with a disciplined macro library can already generate most of a cabinet schematic. LLM value there is in the front end (turning a spec into configurator inputs) and in checking, not in drawing.
- Mechanical design of fixtures and grippers is the least AI-ready desk discipline: frontier models score under 0.4 on parametric CAD fidelity, and no commercial generative fixture tool surfaced. Code-driven CAD (CadQuery-style) works for parametric families a human has already designed, which again rewards standardised cell families.
- Simulation is the enabling layer for everything else: PLC verification (PLCWorld), robot code verification (MDPI digital-twin layer) and commissioning savings all depend on having a cell model. An AI-first integrator's real asset would be a fast path from concept to a simulation that agents can test against.

### Gaps
- Not searched (budget exhausted): actual adoption of NVIDIA Omniverse / Isaac Sim by small integrators, Siemens generative layout or "digital twin composer" tools, Visual Components AI features, automated reach and cycle-time study tools. No evidence either way was gathered.
- No evidence found for or against production use of text-to-CAD tools (for example Zoo) or vendor gripper configurators with AI in integrator workflows.
- The 40% EPLAN saving is a vendor estimate with no independent confirmation.

## 6. Robot foundation models / vision-language-action models for industrial tasks in 2026: reliability, cycle time, integrator deployment

### Takeaway
VLA models reached first paid or pilot factory deployments in 2026 (Skild at Foxconn Houston, Figure at BMW, a Siemens-internal pi0.5 packaging pilot), but every documented case needed on-site data collection and fine-tuning, and published evidence stresses brittleness under repetition rather than cycle-time parity. No evidence was found of ordinary systems integrators deploying VLAs in customer cells.

### Cited Findings
- Siemens factory case study (arXiv, 25 May 2026): a pretrained pi0.5 policy adapted to pick a transparent accessory bag from clutter and insert it into a carton at the Erlangen electronics plant; required repeated cycles of data collection, curation, fine-tuning, evaluation and targeted recovery-data collection, totalling 2,535 episodes (10 hours) of on-site data, with an on-floor deployment window of about two weeks; the paper's stated contribution is an account of recurring failure modes and lessons in teleoperation, data curation and runtime engineering — [arXiv 2605.27461](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27461)
- Trade-press visit to the same Siemens effort: Siemens is concentrating on fewer, more tightly defined use cases rather than general-purpose intelligence — [Machine Design](https://www.machinedesign.com/markets/robotics/article/55378521/siemens-flexible-robots-messy-worlds-inside-siemens-push-for-practical-industrial-ai)
- Habilis-beta paper (Feb 2026) argues single-trial success rate hides the problem: models "can achieve a high single-trial success rate, yet deteriorate rapidly under repetition"; proposes Tasks per Hour plus Mean Time Between Intervention as the industrial metrics — [arXiv 2602.18813](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.18813)
- Survey on transferring VLAs to industry (Sept 2025): limitations in operational precision, real-time responsiveness and system stability — [arXiv 2509.23121](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23121v1)
- Sereact's Cortex 2.0 paper (April 2026) itself states VLA models are brittle to compounding failures in long-horizon tasks — [papers.cool entry for arXiv 2604.20246](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2604.20246)
- Skild AI: its model is to run robots at Foxconn's Houston plant building NVIDIA Blackwell server racks, described by Reuters as an early commercial deployment (March 2026); Foxconn gave no line-speed effect; an aggregator's "60-80% efficiency within hours" figure is unverified — [Reuters via The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/03/17/skild-ai-nvidia-deploy-robot-brain-on-blackwell-assembly-lines); [NewsBytes (aggregator)](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/science/foxconn-to-use-skild-ais-robot-brain-in-production/tldr)

- Gemini Robotics: Boston Dynamics' 2026 Atlas units are committed to Hyundai and Google DeepMind with Gemini Robotics integration planned; no metrics — [Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/hyundai-deploys-atlas-robots-with-deepmind-integration-07e63c8b)

- Analyst opinion: factory buyers start with one narrow task such as palletising or welding, which slows adoption of a general-purpose model like Physical Intelligence's — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/b3c92bf0-1bf3-4daf-a85c-9f2e5efa1caf/)

### Inferences
- Deploying a VLA today swaps programming hours for data-collection and evaluation hours (the Siemens case needed 2,535 on-site episodes for one pick-and-insert task) and yields a system whose reliability must be characterised statistically. For a small integrator selling fixed-price cells with uptime commitments, that is a worse trade than classical programming for any task that can be fixtured.
- The tasks where VLAs are being tried are exactly those classical automation cannot do economically: deformable or transparent items, cluttered kitting, high-mix handling. They expand the addressable task set at the margin rather than reduce the cost of standard cells.
- Remote human assistance (Tutor) and RaaS pricing (Sereact, THEKER, Tutor) are how vendors absorb the reliability gap; that requires capital and a fleet, not an integrator's project-based model.

- No instance of a conventional systems integrator shipping a VLA-controlled cell to a customer was found; absence of evidence here reflects limited search, not proof.

## 7. AI for documentation and compliance: risk assessments (EN ISO 12100), CE technical files, manuals, translations; what the safety engineer must still sign off

### Takeaway
AI-assisted risk-assessment and manual-drafting tools exist in the German-speaking market (CE-Copilot is an explicit AI-first entrant; incumbents such as Safexpert are structured rather than generative), but they produce drafts: legal responsibility stays with the manufacturer, and specialists argue AI cannot deliver a complete, compliant EN ISO 12100 assessment on its own. The new EU Machinery Regulation (applicable 20 January 2027) widens what the risk assessment must cover.

### Cited Findings
- CE-Copilot (Germany) markets AI-supported hazard analysis per EN ISO 12100 with risk matrix and three-step method, plus AI drafts of protective measures and operating-manual chapters (vendor claims, not independently tested) — [CE-Copilot guide (EN)](https://www.ce-copilot.de/en/guides/risk-assessment-software); [CE-Copilot (DE)](https://www.ce-copilot.de/ratgeber/risikobeurteilung-software)
- Safety Software (safetysoftware.eu) offers structured machine risk assessment and stresses the system does not replace the standard or the engineer's responsibility — [Safety Software](https://safetysoftware.eu/en/p/machine-risk-assessment)
- Sceptical specialist view (technical-documentation firm): AI can support sub-tasks but cannot deliver a complete, legally compliant EN ISO 12100 risk assessment or a standalone CE conformity assessment; one reason given is that harmonised standards are paywalled, so models lack reliable knowledge of their content — [highdoc](https://www.highdoc.de/ce-kennzeichnung-und-risikobeurteilung-mit-ki/)
- Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 replaces Directive 2006/42/EC and applies from 20 January 2027; it explicitly addresses software, cybersecurity and AI-enabled or self-evolving behaviour, and risk assessment must cover the whole lifecycle including future software updates — [SGS](https://www.sgs.com/en/news/2025/06/get-ready-for-eu-machinery-regulation-2023-1230-what-you-need-to-know-and-how-sgs-can-help); Czech authority guidance for manufacturers — [ÚNMZ](https://unmz.gov.cz/en/narizeni-o-strojnich-zarizenich-informace-pro-vyrobce/)
- Test houses offer EN ISO 12100 risk-assessment support but state that "the final assessment and responsibility remain with the manufacturer" — [Intertek](https://www.intertek.com/industrial-equipment/machinery-regulation)
- ISO/TR 22100-5:2021 describes applying ISO 12100 to machinery with ML; it excludes AI used in safety functions (safety-related sensors and control parts) — [ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/80778.html)
- A revision of EN ISO 12100 is in progress and is expected to add cybersecurity and a reference to unintended self-evolving AI behaviour (status not confirmed) — [inmas](https://inmas.de/technische-dokumentation/neue-en-iso-12100-kommt)
- Vendor checklist claim (treat cautiously): AI systems acting as safety components in machinery are likely high-risk under the EU AI Act — [Presencis checklist](https://cdn.presencis.com/regulations/machinery-regulation/checklist/)

### Inferences
- Documentation is a strong fit for LLM agents working from the project's own artefacts (BOM, schematics, PLC code, layout): hazard lists, draft risk-assessment tables, operating manuals, translations and the technical-file index are text assembled from structured inputs. The human tasks that remain are judging the completeness of hazard identification on the physical machine, choosing and validating risk-reduction measures, verifying safety-function performance levels (EN ISO 13849 calculations and validation), physical validation tests, and signing the EU declaration of conformity.
- If an integrator uses AI or learned policies inside the machine's control (not just in its engineering process), the regulatory burden rises from 2027; keeping AI out of safety functions and in a supervised, non-safety role is the low-risk design choice.
- The paywalled-standards point is a practical constraint for a harness builder: the agent needs licensed standards text and the firm's own validated hazard library in context, not general model knowledge.

### Gaps
- No funded startup focused specifically on AI-generated CE technical files for machine builders was identified beyond CE-Copilot (funding unknown); no customer results or accuracy evaluations for any such tool were found.
- No source was found on AI translation quality or liability for machinery manuals specifically.
- Whether AI-enabled machinery always requires a notified body under the new regulation was asserted by one vendor source only; check the regulation text (Annex I list) directly.

An AI-first integrator that stays project-based will look like a services firm with better margins, not a venture-scale company, unless it productises a cell family or a software layer.
- The funded players cluster at two ends: standard simple cells sold direct (Vention, Standard Bots, RobCo) and hard perception-heavy handling (Sereact, THEKER, Tutor). The middle, customer-specific cells with PLC, safety, peripherals and process know-how, is still served by traditional integrators and is where LLM-agent engineering leverage (PLC, documentation, quoting, electrical generation) is least contested.
- No Czech or wider CEE AI-native integrator surfaced in these results; the EU-Startups CEE funding roundup appeared in results but its contents were not retrieved.

### Gaps
- No reported operating results (gross margin, deployment time, uptime) were found for any of these companies; all scale figures are company-reported.
- Formic, Bright Machines, Rapid Robotics, Launchpad and similar US automation-as-a-service firms were not covered by results (search budget exhausted).
- Valuation claims for RobCo ($500M+ at Series C per Bloomberg via secondary source; above $1B in an October 2026 secondary sale per one aggregator) are unconfirmed — [venturecapitaltracker.com](https://venturecapitaltracker.com/startup/robco)

## 10. What remains stubbornly manual; practitioner opinions on AI tools in controls engineering

### Takeaway
Practitioner sentiment is that AI helps with Structured Text drafts, explanations and documentation but does not touch the bulk of controls work, which is commissioning, troubleshooting, dealing with operators and legacy systems; trust is low (61% of surveyed control engineers report low or no trust in AI-driven controls). Ladder/graphical generation and safety code remain weak or prohibited in vendor tools.

### Cited Findings
- Control Engineering "AI in Control Systems" research, 2026 (107 respondents; covers AI in controls broadly, not only code generation): 27% use AI/ML in full production, 65% are piloting or evaluating, 61% report low or no trust in AI-driven controls, and about four in five see AI as complementing rather than replacing traditional approaches — [Control Engineering](https://www.controleng.com/research-how-industrial-ai-is-impacting-the-plant-floor/)
- Practitioner article (Sept 2026): clean-looking generated code can still miss a failed sensor, an operator intervention, a mode transition or a restart condition; AI belongs earlier in the workflow, in requirements and first drafts — [ManufacturingTomorrow](https://www.manufacturingtomorrow.com/article/2026/09/what-if-plc-programming-started-with-a-sentence/27993)
- "Most controls work is troubleshooting, commissioning, talking to operators and keeping old systems running"; AI "does not show up at the plant" (vendor blog, but a frank statement of limits) — [plccode.ai](https://plccode.ai/will-ai-replace-plc-programmers)
- Vendor demos of natural-language ladder generation called "mostly toys" whose output "rarely passes a real review" (blog of unclear authority) — [plcprogramming.io](https://plcprogramming.io/blog/ai-in-industrial-automation-2026)
- r/PLC user: ChatGPT and Claude are decent at explaining ladder or producing logic in text form, but cannot draw ladder that resembles real Studio 5000 output — [r/PLC thread mirror](https://redlib.cypherpunktools.com/r/PLC/comments/1jzt1s9/looking_for_an_ai_that_can_actually_draw_plc)
- Hands-on test (older, ChatGPT era): generated ladder used RSLogix 500-style addressing rather than Studio 5000 and the ST needed tag declarations fixed — [Control.com](https://control.com/technical-articles/can-openai-chatgpt-generative-ai-be-used-for-control-system-programming/)
- Practitioner knowledge-base article rates LLM ladder output as close to useless for production and recommends writing production ladder by hand (blog of unclear authority) — [Industrial Monitor Direct](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/using-llms-for-plc-programming-practical-use-cases-and-limitations)
- Vendor-documented limits: CODESYS MCP server cannot create or modify graphical-language objects — [CODESYS](https://www.codesys.com/ecosystem/release-lifecycle/releases-updates/development-system-mcp-server/); Rockwell's copilot cannot modify safety code or device configuration — [Rockwell documentation](https://www.rockwellautomation.com/en-us/docs/factorytalk-design-studio/current/contents-ditamap/factorytalk-design-studio-help/before-you-begin/factorytalk-design-studio-copilot.html)
- Third-party agent bridges treat download to a live PLC as destructive and gate it behind dry-run and explicit apply steps — [Czarnak/tia-portal-mcp](https://github.com/Czarnak/tia-portal-mcp); [note.com](https://note.com/totorotom/n/nc973555ee57f?hl=en)
- Benchmarks confirm the gap between "compiles" and "runs the plant correctly": 82.7% vs 25.1% task success between easy and hard closed-loop cases — [arXiv 2610.02982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02982)
- Installation and commissioning remain a 4-6 week on-site block in a typical cell timeline — [LANPDT](https://lanpdt.com/tech-talk-episode-19/)

### Inferences
- The stubbornly manual list, combining the evidence above: on-site installation and I/O checkout; commissioning against real mechanics, sensors and part tolerances; exception handling (faults, restarts, mode changes, operator interventions) which is most of real PLC code and worst-covered by generation; gripper and fixture iteration; safety validation and sign-off; cycle-time tuning on the real cell; customer change requests during acceptance; service and legacy maintenance. AI assistance on these is indirect (better-tested software arriving on site, faster diagnostics, faster documentation updates).
- Many customers, especially in North America and in maintenance-driven plants, require ladder for maintainability; an integrator that can deliver ST (common and accepted on CODESYS, Beckhoff and Siemens SCL in Europe) gets more from AI than one obliged to deliver ladder.
- Low trust among control engineers is both a hiring and a sales consideration: customers' maintenance staff must be able to read and own the delivered code, so AI-generated code needs to follow the customer's standards and be reviewable.

### Gaps
- No substantial r/PLC or PLCtalk thread from 2026 was retrievable through search; forum sentiment here rests on one Reddit post and several blogs, some vendor-authored. Direct reading of those communities is advised.
- No practitioner accounts of using Siemens Eigen or CODESYS MCP on a real delivered project (with hours saved or problems found at commissioning) were found.

## 11. Public claims and benchmarks on frontier model performance on hardware, mechanical, electrical or robotics engineering tasks in 2026

### Takeaway
Benchmarks published in 2025-2026 consistently show frontier models well below reliable performance on physical-engineering design tasks (parametric CAD fidelity about 0.4; hard closed-loop PLC tasks about 25%; "modest" scores on multi-domain engineering design), in contrast to their strength on conventional software.

### Cited Findings
- BenchCAD (May 2026): best frontier model total about 0.397 (Gemini 3.1 Pro, thinking) on execution-verified parametric CAD across 106 industrial part families; a specialist model reaches 0.6014 — [BenchCAD](https://benchcad.github.io/BenchCAD_webpage/)
- An author's LinkedIn post says Anthropic reported BenchCAD results in a system card (0.384 voxel IoU for a model named "Claude Mythos 5"); not verified from a primary source — [LinkedIn profile cited by search](https://www.linkedin.com/in/zbw)
- EngDesign (arXiv Sept 2025): engineering-design benchmark spanning control systems, mechanical systems, structural design, digital hardware, analog IC, robotics and signal processing; "even the most capable frontier models achieve only modest performance" — [arXiv 2509.16204](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16204v2)
- Frontier-Eng (arXiv April 2026): evaluates eight frontier models on open-ended engineering optimisation tasks with executable feedback — [arXiv 2604.12290](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.12290)
- PLCWorld (Oct 2026): direct GPT-5.5 at 82.70% task success on easy and 25.10% on hard closed-loop PLC control cases — [arXiv 2610.02982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02982)
- "Xplore AI" Real World Engineering Bench (reported October 2026): constrained mechanical, electrical and aerospace design tasks, with a headline of "GPT-6 Astra at 37%"; single trade-news source, not confirmed — [Runtime Wire](https://runtimewire.com/article/xplore-ai-rwe-bench-engineering-models)
- A vendor-run PCB design leaderboard (KiCadBench) exists; treat as vendor material — [D-Central](https://d-central.tech/llm-benchmarks/)
- CODESYS's internal tests found Claude models best for Structured Text among MCP-capable LLMs (vendor statement, no numbers) — [CODESYS](https://www.codesys.com/products/engineering/ai-supported-engineering/)

### Inferences
- The evidence supports a clear ranking of where an LLM-agent team has leverage in integration work: strongest on text artefacts with executable checks (ST/SCL code, robot scripts against a simulator, schematic generation through configurators, documents, quotes); weak on geometry-heavy mechanical design; unproven on anything requiring physical judgement.
- Benchmarks that add a verifier or harness (LLM4PLC, AutoPLC, SemaPLC) show large gains over direct prompting, so harness engineering skill is a real differentiator in this domain, more so than model choice.

### Gaps
- Per-model tables for the latest Claude, Gemini and GPT models on PLCWorld, EngDesign and Frontier-Eng were not retrievable; the "Xplore AI" benchmark and the system-card BenchCAD figure remain unverified because the confirming search could not be run.
- No benchmark was found for electrical schematic (EPLAN-style) generation, safety-function design, or robot-cell layout quality.
