## 1. Structure of Czech manufacturing (share of GDP/employment, SME counts, sectors, regions)

### Takeaway
Czechia is the most manufacturing-heavy labour market in the EU (manufacturing is about 30% of business-economy employment and about 20% of GDP), dominated by automotive and its supply chain, with the most industrial regions by employment being Zlin, Vysocina, Pardubice and Liberec. A clean count of manufacturing SMEs by size band could not be retrieved and remains a gap.

### Cited Findings
- Manufacturing value added was 20.04% of Czech GDP in 2024 (World Bank series). — [Trading Economics / World Bank](https://tradingeconomics.com/czech-republic/manufacturing-value-added-percent-of-gdp-wb-data.html)
- Manufacturing has stayed above one fifth of the Czech economy for about 30 years. — [Fortune, Jan 2025](https://fortune.com/europe/2025/01/21/czech-republic-quiet-automotive-giant-skoda-bucking-economic-downturn-unfolding-crucial-ally-germany/)
- Manufacturing accounted for 30.9% of business-economy employment in Czechia in 2023 (30.4% in 2022, the highest in the EU; range started at 8.7% in Luxembourg), and 14.6% of business-economy local units. — [Eurostat, Businesses in the manufacturing sector](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Businesses_in_the_manufacturing_sector); [Eurostat, SBS at regional level](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Structural_business_statistics_at_regional_level)
- Manufacturing averaged 27% of total Czech employment over 2008-2018, more than 10 percentage points above the EU-28 (older figure, 2008-2018). — [Trade-union study on industry employment](https://ipodpora.odbory.info/soubory/uploads/STUDIE_I_ES_ZAMESTNANCI_PRUMYSL_.pdf)
- Industry as a whole generated 29% of gross value added (Ceska sporitelna analysis, 2021 - older). — [Ceska sporitelna robot analysis, Feb 2021](https://cdn0.erstegroup.com/content/dam/cz/csas/www_csas_cz/Dokumenty-korporat/Dokumenty/Analytici/robot-analyza-2021-02.pdf)
- Czechia has held the EU lead in industry's share of total employment for more than two decades (CSU analysis, 2019 data). — [CSU analysis](https://www.czso.cz/documents/10180/151683470/320329-20a.pdf)
- CNB: in 2025 services contributed far more to growth than industry, which added only 0.3 percentage points; industrial employment has been falling since 2019 while services employment grew. — [CNB, Kondice ceskeho prumyslu](https://www.cnb.cz/cs/menova-politika/zpravy-o-menove-politice/boxy-a-clanky/Kondice-ceskeho-prumyslu)
- Czech plants built about 1.45 million passenger cars in 2025; car exports are close to 20% of goods exports. — [Central European Times](https://centraleuropeantimes.com/the-price-of-an-industrial-economy/)
- European Commission 2025 country report: Czech exports are about 81% industrial goods, automotive leads exports, and the industry share of GDP is above the EU average (which also explains high energy intensity). — [EC country report copy, Austrian Parliament](https://www.parlament.gv.at/dokument/XXVIII/EU/22946/imfname_11483158.pdf)
- H1 2026 passenger car output: 780,378 units, +4.4% year on year; 40% of vehicles had an electrified drive (130,356 BEV, 26,625 PHEV, 161,752 hybrids); 90.7% exported. — [AutoSAP, H1 2026](https://autosap.cz/en/aktualita/pres-tri-ctvrte-milionu-vyrobenych-vozidel-v-prvnim-pololeti-cesky-autoprumysl-drzi-silnou-vykonnost/)
- Industrial production was up 4.0% year on year in June 2026. — [kurzy.cz industry tracker](https://www.kurzy.cz/makroekonomika/prumysl/)
- All sectors: 1,341,014 economically active enterprises with 0-249 employees in 2024; SME share of all enterprises 99.87% (MPO report published January 2026; not manufacturing-specific). — [MPO, SME strategy evaluation report for 2024](https://mpo.gov.cz/assets/cz/podnikani/male-a-stredni-podnikani/studie-a-strategicke-dokumenty/2026/1/VZ-Strategie-MSP-za-rok-2024.pdf)
- Regions: the most industrial regions by employment are, long-term, Liberec, Zlin and Vysocina. — [CSU analysis](https://www.czso.cz/documents/10180/151683470/320329-20a.pdf)
- Share of employed residents working in industry (NACE B-E, not manufacturing only), Q4 2023: Zlin about 36.8%, Vysocina about 35.6%, Pardubice about 35.3%, Moravian-Silesian about 30.0%, South Moravian about 25.1%. Caution: the extract's table columns were misaligned, so verify against the original table. — [CSU regional labour table, Q4 2023](https://www.czso.cz/documents/10180/189468424/33010123q4e02.pdf)
- A plastics cluster (Plastikarsky klastr, "PLASTR") operates in the Zlin region and links to Slovak and Polish clusters. — [PLASTR presentation, UTB](https://businessdays.utb.cz/wp-content/uploads/pr2019/PLASTR.pdf)
- Named large engineering employers in Liberec region and Vysocina include Johnson Controls Ceska Lipa, Magna and Zdas (older teaching material). — [MUNI course material](https://is.muni.cz/el/sci/podzim2014/Z0047/um/50780270/GPAZ_06.pdf?lang=en)

### Inferences
- CSU innovation-survey tables for 2014-2016 show 6,039 manufacturing enterprises (51.7%) with innovations, which implies a survey frame of roughly 11,700 manufacturing enterprises with 10+ employees. This is a derived order-of-magnitude figure for the addressable base of small, medium and large manufacturers, not a published count, and it is about ten years old. — derived from [CSU innovation tables](https://www.czso.cz/documents/10180/61449086/2130031838B.pdf)
- The regional data point to Zlin, Vysocina, Pardubice and Liberec as the densest SME-manufacturing territories relative to population, while Moravian-Silesian and South Moravia (Brno) are larger in absolute terms but more diversified.
- Automotive output is not collapsing in volume terms (H1 2026 +4.4%), so the stress in the supplier base (see section 3) is about margins, powertrain mix and German-customer weakness rather than Czech OEM volumes.

## 2. Robot adoption (density, installations, concentration, SME adoption)

### Takeaway
Czech robot density reached 216 per 10,000 manufacturing employees in 2024 (16th globally, up 82% since 2017, above the world average of 132 but below Western Europe's 267), with 2,298 robots installed in 2025. Adoption is heavily skewed to automotive and large firms: over 60% of large manufacturers use industrial robots versus roughly 6-9% of small ones, and about 13% of SMEs overall.

### Cited Findings
- Robot density rose from 119 (2017) to 216 (2024) robots per 10,000 manufacturing employees, +82%; world average 132 in 2024. — [zakazka.cz](https://www.zakazka.cz/cesko-patri-v-robotizaci-k-nejrychleji-rostoucim-zemim-spicce-se-presto-vzdaluje/); [BusinessInfo](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/cesko-patri-v-robotizaci-k-rychleji-rostoucim-zemim-spicka-mu-presto-unika/)
- Czechia ranks 16th of 22 listed economies at 216, annual change +4.3% (IFR data published April 2026). Central European comparison: Slovenia 315, Czechia 216, Slovakia 210, Hungary 172. — [Visual Capitalist](https://www.visualcapitalist.com/ranked-countries-most-industrial-robots-per-worker-2024-v2/)
- Western Europe averaged 267 robots per 10,000 manufacturing employees in 2024 (North America 204, Asia 131); South Korea leads at 1,220. — [IFR press release, April 2026](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/robot-density-surges-in-europe-asia-and-americas)
- EU-27 density 231, world 132. — [EU Transition Pathways, IFR dataset note](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/adv-man/data/ifr-world-robotics-reference-dataset-robot-installations-and-density)
- Installations: 2,298 industrial robots were installed in Czechia in 2025, 7th in the EU, behind Poland and ahead of Sweden. — [Systemylogistiky.cz, 29 Sep 2026](https://www.systemylogistiky.cz/2026/09/29/ve-svetovych-tovarnach-pracuje-rekordnich-pet-milionu-prumyslovych-robotu/); [Euro.cz](https://www.euro.cz/clanky/cesko-patri-v-robotizaci-k-nejrychleji-rostoucim-zemim-svetove-spicce-se-i-presto-vzdaluje/)
- Commentary (Moore Czech Republic): Czechia is among faster-growing countries but the gap to the leaders is widening; South Korea added roughly five times the Czech increase over the same period. — [BusinessInfo](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/cesko-patri-v-robotizaci-k-rychleji-rostoucim-zemim-spicka-mu-presto-unika/)
- Global context from World Robotics 2026: about 603,000 installations in 2025 and about 5 million robots in operation; China took 59% of 2025 installations. — [Newstream](https://www.newstream.cz/nazory/robotu-ve-svete-opet-pribylo-vetsinu-novych-si-poridila-cina); [IFR World Robotics 2026 press conference deck](https://ifr.org/downloads/press_docs/Market_Presentation_WR_Press_Conference_2026.pdf)
- European automotive robot installations fell 25% in 2025 to 14,900 units. — [Tiscali.cz](https://www.tiscali.cz/v-tovarnach-uz-pracuje-5-milionu-robotu-cina-ovladla-vic-nez-polovinu-trhu-evropa-ztraci-tempo-a-instalaci-ubyva-779860)
- Automotive concentration: 58% of new robot installations in Czechia went to automotive (article roughly 1.5 years old; reference year not stated in the extract). — [Vse o prumyslu](https://www.vseoprumyslu.cz/inspirace/trendy/kvety-robotizace.html)
- Older data point: an undated Radio Prague report put Czechia 16th with 147 robots per 10,000 (world average 113) and said installations had fallen two years in a row. — [Radio Prague International](https://english.radio.cz/node/8699244)
- Firm-size concentration (CSU, 2020): 18% of manufacturing firms used industrial robots; more than 60% of large manufacturers; 7% of all firms with 10+ employees used industrial or service robots. — [CSU: Roboty vyuziva temer petina prumyslovych podniku](https://csu.gov.cz/produkty/roboty-vyuziva-temer-petina-prumyslovych-podniku)
- CSU 2020 as cited by AMSP: over 60% of large manufacturers versus only 9% of small ones use industrial robots. — [BusinessInfo on AMSP/Ipsos survey](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/robotu-by-se-ceskym-firmam-hodilo-dvakrat-vic-zjistil-pruzkum/)
- CSU 2018: more than half of large, almost a third of medium and only 6% of small manufacturing enterprises used industrial robots. — [CSU: Roboty vyuzivaji hlavne velke prumyslove firmy](https://csu.gov.cz/csu/czso/roboty-vyuzivaji-hlavne-velke-prumyslove-firmy-)
- CT24 (year not stated in extract): 16% of Czech firms use industrial robots; 53% of firms with 250+ employees but only 6% of small firms. — [CT24](https://ct24.ceskatelevize.cz/clanek/ekonomika/sestnact-procent-ceskych-firem-vyuziva-prumyslove-roboty-hlavne-ty-vetsi-69008)
- SME adoption (Ipsos for AMSP CR, March 2022, n=200): about 13% of SMEs used industrial robots, 7% mobile robotics, 5% collaborative robots; a typical SME had roughly 10% of production robotized; the survey judged the potential to be about double current use. — [BusinessInfo](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/robotu-by-se-ceskym-firmam-hodilo-dvakrat-vic-zjistil-pruzkum/); [AMSP/Ipsos PDF](https://amsp.cz/media/amsp/3821/ipsos-pro-amsprobotizace-v-mspfinal.pdf)

### Inferences
- With 2,298 units installed in 2025 and (as of the older data point) more than half going to automotive, the non-automotive Czech market is on the order of 1,000 robots per year across all firm sizes. This is a rough inference combining figures from different years.
- The small-firm adoption rate (6-9% of small manufacturers in 2018-2020) versus 60%+ for large firms is the clearest quantified evidence of an under-served small-shop segment.
- The 25% drop in European automotive robot installations in 2025 suggests established integrators that depend on automotive lines have spare capacity and may move down-market, raising competition for SME projects.

### Gaps
- The Czech country table from World Robotics 2026 (operational stock, 2024 vs 2025 installations, split by industry and application) was not retrieved; the 58% automotive share is from an older article.
- No CSU data on robot use by firm size more recent than 2020 was found (CSU surveys robots in its ICT-in-enterprises survey; a 2024/2025 wave should exist on csu.gov.cz).
- No Czech-specific cobot installation count or cobot share was found.

## 3. Demand drivers (labour market, wages, foreign workers, energy, German customers, automotive)

### Takeaway
The labour market is tight but looser than a year ago (unemployment 5.0% in September 2026 versus 4.5% a year earlier, 104,359 vacancies), wages are rising 6.4% nominally, and skilled production trades show 0.1-0.3 applicants per vacancy, so the automation case rests on skills mismatch and wage cost rather than absolute absence of people. Automotive volumes are growing in 2026, but suppliers are squeezed by German-customer weakness and the EV transition, and a German industrial power subsidy since January 2026 adds cost pressure.

### Cited Findings
Labour market
- September 2026: share of unemployed 5.0% (4.97%), unchanged from August, up from 4.5% in September 2025; 368,377 job seekers; 104,359 vacancies. Regional range from 3.93% (Prague) to 7.29% (Usti). — [Ceske noviny / CTK](https://www.ceskenoviny.cz/zpravy/nezamestnanost-v-cesku-zustala-v-zari-na-peti-procentech/2884375); [CSU Prague, Sep 2026](https://csu.gov.cz/pha/podil-nezamestnanych-v-praze-v-zari-2026-cinil-393)
- Regional: Liberec region 5.63% (fourth highest), Pardubice region 4.03% with 5,884 vacancies (September 2026). — [CSU Liberec](https://csu.gov.cz/lbk/v-zari-se-nezamestnanost-v-libereckem-kraji-lehce-snizila); [CSU Pardubice](https://csu.gov.cz/pak/nezamestnanost-v-pardubickem-kraji-k-30-zari-2026)
- Labour Office: shortage is sharpest in specialised engineering and production professions, with 0.1-0.3 applicants per vacancy. — [Labour Office press release, June 2026](https://up.gov.cz/cms/documents/dd7ee2a6-e540-3384-6b3c-0c8b29fba013/SCK_TZ_nezamestnanost_ČERVEN_26_fin.pdf)
- Labour Office employer survey 2026 (23,900 questionnaires from 17,000 employers, covering about 47% of employees): employers reported 46,400 missing positions; 36% expect to increase headcount; the main problem is described as mismatch between employer needs and available qualifications, especially technical specialists; the fall in manufacturing employment shows up mainly among agency workers. — [Labour Office, Predikce a realita pracovniho trhu 2026](https://up.gov.cz/cms/documents/3b6fbbeb-d080-daf6-127a-54ad77b3b0d3/UP_Predikce%20a%20realita%20pracovniho%20trhu-2026_fin.pdf)
- In the same survey family, only a minority of employers (about 21% in one reading of the extract) expect automation to solve their staffing gaps; internal retraining is named as a main response. — [MPSV data portal copy of the survey](https://data.mpsv.cz/portal/assets/UP_Predikce_a_realita_pracovniho_trhu-2026-Dny6INnR.pdf)
- ManpowerGroup: 66% of Czech employers report difficulty filling positions. — [BusinessInfo](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/nedostatek-talentu-se-prohlubuje-66-procent-zamestnavatelu-ma-problemy/)
- July 2026: labour shortage described as a "strategic problem"; manufacturing, construction and transport feel the most pressure, with shortages of production operators, welders and drivers; SP CR ranks labour shortage among the biggest barriers to growth. — [BusinessInfo](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/nedostatek-pracovniku-se-meni-ve-strategicky-problem-nejvetsi-tlak-citi-vyroba-stavebnictvi-a-doprava/)
- SP CR 2026 assembly at MSV Brno highlighted skilled-labour shortages and the need for robotics and automated-production specialists. — [Technika a trh](https://www.technikaatrh.cz/cesky-prumysl-potrebuje-prostor-pro-inovace-snem-svazu-prumyslu-otevrel-na-msv-klicova-temata-konkurenceschopnosti/)

### Inferences
- The "no people" narrative is weaker in late 2026 than in 2018-2023: unemployment has risen half a point in a year. A sales pitch built on wage inflation (6%+ a year), skills mismatch in welding/CNC/operator roles, and exposure to a possible Ukrainian return is better supported by the data than one built on headline labour scarcity.
- The OP TAK MAS grant ceiling (just under 3 million CZK eligible cost at 50%) maps closely onto a single cobot or small robot cell, so grant-sized projects of roughly 0.5-3 million CZK are a natural product size for a small integrator, and the cap sits below the typical project size of established line builders.
- Automotive tier 2/3 suppliers are simultaneously told to raise productivity and to diversify, yet are cutting capex; expect long sales cycles there in 2026 and better near-term traction with non-automotive metalworking, plastics, food and defence-related shops. This is an inference from AutoSAP's advice and the investment anecdotes, not a measured result.
- Firms in Czechia (many German-owned) still report clearly better investment intentions than firms in Germany, which supports Czechia-first sequencing over an early push into Germany.

### Gaps
- Average wage and wage growth specifically for manufacturing (CZ-NACE C) in 2026 were not retrieved; they are in the CSU Q2 2026 wage release tables.
- No current figure for the number or share of agency workers in manufacturing.
- No sourced industrial electricity price level (EUR/MWh) for Czech SMEs in 2026; the Klub financnich reditelu analysis (20 October 2026: Czechia below EU average but about twice China, emission allowances 18-50% of final price) appeared in a search summary without a resolvable URL and should be treated as unverified.
- The SP CR quarterly business survey for 2026 (share of firms planning automation investment, labour as a growth barrier) was not found.
- No direct evidence was found on explicit automation demands from German customers (e.g. audit or cost-down clauses); the German-pressure point rests on the supplier-crisis reporting above.

### Inferences
- With roughly 147 integrators for a market installing about 2,300 robots a year, the average integrator handles a small number of robots annually; the market is already long-tail, so a new entrant differentiates by niche (application, sector, region, project size) rather than by simply existing.
- The competitors most directly facing small shops appear to be (a) cobot distributors that also integrate (Exactec, AMTECH, ALL ROBOTS, Roboticall, Controltech, Fairino Europe), (b) small process specialists (welding: KSK, Goldmax, Valk Welding; machine tending/palletizing: ARC-Robotics), and (c) standardized cell sellers quoting 0.7-1.2 million CZK. Large line builders tied to automotive (Blumenbecker, DEL and similar) are less direct competitors.
- The evidence for a "too small for established integrators" gap is indirect: low small-firm adoption (section 2), AMSP's note that SMEs lack orientation and have "no one to consult" (section 8), and the low grant ceiling. It is plausible but not demonstrated.
- Low-cost Chinese cobots with a Czech legal entity (Fairino in Olomouc) are compressing hardware prices, shifting integrator margin toward engineering hours and service.

## 7. Cross-border selling: Germany, Austria, Slovakia, Poland; rate differentials

### Takeaway
The labour-cost gap is large: 2025 hourly labour cost was 19.8 EUR in Czechia versus 46.3 EUR in Austria (EU average 34.9), and German freelance PLC/robot programmers list at roughly 70-110 EUR per hour, while Czech PLC/robot programmers earn about 47,000-89,000 CZK gross per month. Posting staff to Germany requires A1 certificates and compliance with the German posting law, and German investment appetite is currently weak, so Slovakia and Czech-based German-owned plants look like easier first export steps.

### Cited Findings
Cost and rate differentials
- Eurostat 2025 hourly labour cost, whole economy: Czechia 19.8 EUR, Slovakia 19.8 EUR, Poland 19.1 EUR, Austria 46.3 EUR, EU average 34.9 EUR. — [Symsite.sk citing Eurostat](https://www.symsite.sk/single-post/slovak-labor-costs-are-11th-lowest-in-the-eu); [Eurostat news item, 2025 labour costs](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/product?code=ddn-20260331-2)

Posting workers and legal practicalities (sources mostly 2020-2023; verify current rules)
- Employees of an EU firm posted to Germany under a contract for work need no residence or work permit; an A1 certificate from the Czech social security administration and health-insurance proof are required. — [BusinessInfo, working in Germany](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/co-delat-kdyz-chcete-pracovat-v-nemecku/4/)

Market conditions and precedents
- German investment sentiment is weak: only 23% of firms in Germany plan higher investment in 2026 and the investment balance is -8 points, versus +11 in Czechia. — [GTAI on AHK survey](https://www.gtai.de/de/trade/tschechische-republik/wirtschaftsumfeld/stimmung-unter-investoren-truebt-sich-ein-1760090)
- Neighbour robot densities (2024): Slovakia 210, Hungary 172, Slovenia 315; Western Europe average 267. Poland installed more robots than Czechia in 2025. — [Visual Capitalist](https://www.visualcapitalist.com/ranked-countries-most-industrial-robots-per-worker-2024-v2/); [Systemylogistiky.cz](https://www.systemylogistiky.cz/2026/09/29/ve-svetovych-tovarnach-pracuje-rekordnich-pet-milionu-prumyslovych-robotu/)
- Precedents for cross-border operation: German-owned Blumenbecker runs Czech and Slovak subsidiaries; Olomouc-based Zeykan Robotics supplies customers across Europe; several cobot distributors (Roboticall, Controltech, Zlin Robotics) cover Czechia and Slovakia as one territory. — [Blumenbecker Prag](https://blumenbecker.com/cz-en/company/blumenbecker-group/blumenbecker-prag-sro); [Machineseeker, Zeykan Robotics](https://www.machineseeker.cz/Haendler/5732490/zeykan-robotics-s-r-o-olomouc); [Roboticall](https://roboticall.cz/)

### Inferences
- Rough rate arithmetic (assumptions flagged): a Czech robot programmer on about 75,600 CZK gross per month costs the employer roughly 100,000 CZK per month with statutory contributions (assumed about 34%, unverified), or about 600-650 CZK per productive hour before overhead, i.e. roughly 25-27 EUR at an assumed exchange rate near 24.5 CZK/EUR (unverified for late 2026). Against German freelance asking rates of 75-85 EUR per hour, a Czech integrator could bill 45-60 EUR per hour in Germany/Austria and still undercut local rates by 30-40% while roughly doubling its domestic cost base. Actual Czech integrator billing rates were not found, so this is an estimate.
- Slovakia is the lowest-friction export market: shared distributor territories, mutually intelligible language, similar labour cost and robot density, and an automotive-heavy base. Its cost level offers no price advantage, however, so the sale there rests on capability rather than price.
- Germany and Austria offer the price advantage but currently weak capex appetite; the most realistic entry is through German or Austrian parent companies of Czech plants already served, or subcontracting engineering hours to German integrators, rather than direct SME sales.
- Poland is a larger robot market than Czechia but has equal or lower labour costs and its own distributor base (e.g. Renex), so a Czech integrator has no obvious cost edge there.

### Gaps
- Germany's 2025 hourly labour cost level was not in the retrieved extracts (the Eurostat item gives the range 12-57 EUR; Germany's figure should be read from Eurostat table lc_lci_lev).
- No sourced billing rates for Czech integrators or Czech freelance PLC/robot programmers (CZK per hour); only salaries were found.
- No sourced day rates or project price comparisons between Czech and German integrators.
- Current (2026) posting rules for Germany and Austria, including Austrian LSD-BG notification (ZKO forms) and whether robot-cell installation and commissioning count as assembly under AEntG sector lists, were not verified.
- Nothing retrieved on CE-marking/conformity responsibilities, German-language documentation expectations, liability insurance, or VAT treatment of cross-border installation work.
- No specific information on Bavaria or Saxony demand, or on Polish market access.

## 8. Czech surveys on SME attitudes to robotization (barriers)

### Takeaway
The only robotization-specific SME survey found is Ipsos for AMSP CR from March 2022: cost and financing were the top barrier, compounded by lack of orientation and of anyone to consult, while stated potential was about double actual use. Later AMSP/Ipsos waves (late 2025, early 2026) shift the emphasis to regulation, bureaucracy and skilled-labour shortage, and trade press repeatedly cites the belief that robots only pay off for large series.

### Cited Findings
- Ipsos for AMSP CR, March 2022 (200 firms; partners Komercni banka and Skoda Auto): the biggest barriers to robotization/automation are financial, namely upfront entry costs and rising financing needs; AMSP commentary adds limited orientation in the field and no one to consult; over half of medium-sized industrial and trade firms expressed interest in investing in robotics; potential use estimated at roughly double the current level. — [BusinessInfo](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/robotu-by-se-ceskym-firmam-hodilo-dvakrat-vic-zjistil-pruzkum/); [AMSP/Ipsos commentary](https://www.amsp.cz/media/amsp/3823/ipsos-pro-amspautomatizace-52022-kom-amsp-cr.pdf)
- AMSP position: with more state support, more than the current roughly 25% of SME production could be automated (undated in extract). — [BusinessInfo](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/amsp-s-vetsi-podporou-statu-by-mohlo-byt-automatizovano-vice-nez-jen-25-produkce-malych-a-strednich-firem/)
- Ipsos for AMSP, 2021 (innovation in SMEs): the largest barriers to digitalization/automation were shortage of qualified employees (37%) and shortage of funds (33%). — [BusinessInfo](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/brzdou-inovaci-je-nedostatek-kvalifikovanych-zamestnancu-a-financi/); [AMSP/Ipsos 2021](https://amsp.cz/media/amsp/3819/pruzkum-inovace-v-msp-92021-koment-amsp-cr-2.pdf)
- Smaller firms often lack both resources and in-house experts for robotization and automation. — [BusinessInfo](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/mensim-firmam-casto-chybeji-zdroje-i-odbornici-na-robotizaci-a-automatizaci/)
- Ipsos for AMSP, Q4 2025 (digital/automation transformation): legal and regulatory constraints and uncertainty figure as more significant barriers than before; an overloaded regulatory environment and long-term skilled-worker shortage are named as the two main barriers. — [AMSP/Ipsos Q4 2025](https://www.amsp.cz/media/amsp/15949/vysledky-ipsos-pro-amsptransformace-iirtp-2025q4-final.pdf); [BusinessInfo](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/ceske-firmy-ztraceji-schopnost-reagovat-na-zmeny-msp-vsak-dokazuji-ze-i-pres-stale-narocnejsi-podminky-umi-byt-rychle/)
- Ipsos for AMSP, February 2026 (201 firms with 0-250 employees, AI-focused "Rok AI 2026"): most of the market is stagnating and worried mainly about rising costs and bureaucracy; only a small minority use real technological solutions. — [AMSP/Ipsos AI survey 2026](https://www.amsp.cz/media/amsp/16310/vysledky-pruzkumu-ipsos-ai-2026.pdf)
- Trade press on perceived barriers: high price and slow payback (described as an easily refuted myth), fear of installation complexity, and insufficiently trained operators; robotization is also credited with preserving the know-how of experienced workers. — [oneindustry](https://www.oneindustry.cz/automatizace-robotizace/robotizace-ma-i-prekvapive-benefity-pomaha-uchovat-know-how-zkusenych-pracovniku/)
- Firms commonly believe automation pays only for very large series, whereas cells can be set up per product type and changed over. — [DHL small-business advice](https://www.dhl.com/discover/cs-cz/poradenstvi-pro-male-firmy/business-innovation-trends/robots-in-small-business)
- Trade press lists "five most common unrealistic expectations of robotization" (title only retrieved). — [Svet prumyslu](https://svetprumyslu.cz/pet-nejcastejsich-nerealistickych-ocekavani-od-robotizace/)
- A 2026 trade article frames robotization as accelerating but not a universal fix, with Czech industry seeking the boundary between automation and manual work. — [oneindustry](https://www.oneindustry.cz/strojirenstvi/robotizace-zrychluje-univerzalnim-resenim-ale-neni-cesky-prumysl-hleda-hranici-mezi-automatizaci-a-lidskou-praci/)
- SP CR published an analysis of the impacts of work automation (2018, older). — [SP CR analysis 2018](https://www.spcr.cz/images/Dopady_automatizace_prace_analyza_320_2018.pdf)

### Inferences
- The barrier ranking maps onto an offer design: financing (leasing, grant-fit project sizes, robots-as-a-service), know-how (free or subsidised feasibility assessment, "someone to consult"), and small batches (fast-changeover cobot cells) address the three stated objections in order.
- "No one to consult" in the 2022 AMSP commentary is the most direct survey support for the hypothesis that small firms are under-served by existing integrators.
- By 2025-2026 SME owners report regulatory overload and stagnation, so decision capacity, not just money, is constrained; low-risk, small first projects with short commissioning are better matched to this mood than plant-wide proposals.

### Gaps
- No robotization-specific SME survey newer than March 2022 was found; the 2025-2026 AMSP/Ipsos waves cover digitalization/AI broadly.
- No Czech survey quantifying "small batch sizes / high mix" as a barrier was found; it appears only anecdotally in trade press and vendor material.
- Exact percentages from the March 2022 Ipsos robotization survey (barrier shares, intent by firm size) were not readable because the AMSP PDFs could not be opened.
- The Danish Embassy/HowToRobot 2025 feasibility study likely contains recent interview-based evidence on Czech manufacturers' automation barriers but was not read (links in section 4 gaps).
