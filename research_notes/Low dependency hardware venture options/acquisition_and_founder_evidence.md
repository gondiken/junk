# Acquisition of a small Czech technical firm, and evidence on software founders entering physical-world businesses (state as of November 2026)

Method note for the report writer: page fetching was blocked, so every figure below comes from search-result extracts, not from the full pages. Where the extract did not make clear which page carried a number, or where the date of the page is unknown, this is flagged "(unconfirmed)" or "(date unknown)". All 28 permitted searches were used. Sections A1–A6 cover acquisition; B1–B5 cover founder evidence.

## A1. The succession problem in Czech small and mid-sized firms: how many owners are retiring without successors, and how many firms come up for sale

### Takeaway
Czech surveys agree that the first post-1989 founder generation is now handing over and that most family firms lack a named successor (only 42% have one in a 2026 survey), but nobody publishes a reliable count of firms sold or closed each year, and no figure specific to manufacturing or industrial services was found. The best available orders of magnitude are advisory-firm estimates: about 18,000 family firms dealing with succession and over 40,000 firms with owners older than 55.

### Cited Findings
- 2026 survey by Helas and Talers: only 42% of Czech family firms have a chosen successor, although 60% see the handover as an opportunity. One third of founders are postponing the topic, against 22% across all owner generations. The article's own wording is "nearly two thirds have no clarity on who takes over", which does not match 42% exactly (58% without a successor) — [BusinessInfo, 2026](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/vetsina-ceskych-rodinnych-firem-nema-nastupce-ukazuje-pruzkum-2026-mezi-tuzemskymi-podniky/)
- AMSP CR survey (by IPSOS, date unknown, probably several years old): almost two thirds of family-firm owners intend to pass the firm to their children, yet only about one third of firms survive the handover to the next generation — [Novinky.cz](https://www.novinky.cz/clanek/veda-skoly-rodinne-firmy-prebira-mlada-generace-40004803)
- Another AMSP CR survey: 58% of owners are willing to sell the firm outside the family — [BusinessInfo, "Rodinné firmy na rozcestí"](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/rodinne-firmy-na-rozcesti-jak-resit-nastupnictvi/) (unconfirmed which of the AMSP pages carried this number)
- AMSP CR sample of 200 family firms: 56% are still owned by the founder; only 8% have been through more than one generational handover. A quarter of surveyed firms had been offered consulting on selling the firm, but only 8% used it — [AMSP CR press clipping, September 2024](https://www.amsp.cz/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/marketing-media-2024-09-16-generacni-obmena-prospiva-i-zn.pdf) (unconfirmed which AMSP document; see also [AMSP 32nd survey PDF](https://www.amsp.cz/media/amsp/3762/vysledky32pruzkumurodfirmyamspcrnaweb.pdf))
- EY Entrepreneurship Barometer 2025: almost a quarter of firms still have no concrete succession plan — [BusinessInfo, "Generační zlom: Třetinu firem čeká zásadní změna"](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/generacni-zlom-tretinu-firem-ceka-zasadni-zmena-jak-to-ovlivni-ekonomiku/) (unconfirmed which page)
- EY survey reported by Forbes Česko: one fifth of Czech owners plan to sell their firm in the coming months (headline only; date unknown) — [Forbes Česko](https://forbes.cz/petina-ceskych-majitelu-planuje-v-pristich-mesicich-prodat-firmu-vyplyva-z-pruzkumu-ey/)
- J&T Banka survey: 31% of surveyed entrepreneurs are considering leaving the business and selling the firm within five years. The sample was only 103 firms with revenue above CZK 500 million, so it describes large firms, not 5–30 employee firms — [Investiční web](https://www.investicniweb.cz/aktuality/328306-tretina-ceskych-podnikatelu-zvazuje-prodej-firmy-roste-take-zajem-o-akvizice)
- Advisory-firm estimates (commercially interested sources): about 18,000 family firms are dealing with succession today and more than 70% do not survive the move to the second generation; 36% of Czech family firms have a written succession plan; over 40,000 firms have owners older than 55 — [Kmošek advisory](https://www.kmosek.com/hromadny-odchod-podnikatelu-z-obdobi-baby-boomu/), [Freshtime, May 2026](https://www.freshtime.cz/2026/05/firmy-bez-dedicu-proc-tisice-ceskych-podniku-ceka-zbytecny-konec/)
- Czechia cannot reliably measure how many firms close for lack of a successor, because closure data do not record the reason — [Kmošek advisory](https://www.kmosek.com/firma-bez-nastupce/)
- e15 (2026) calls the exit of the first post-1989 founder generation a "quiet crisis". The only hard count in the article is German (KfW): owners of about 569,000 German firms plan to close without continuation by the end of 2029, about 114,000 a year. Česká spořitelna chief economist David Navrátil says the larger macro threat is falling investment and productivity in the years before an owner's planned exit — [e15](https://www.e15.cz/byznys/cesko-stoji-pred-tichou-krizi-odchod-prvni-generace-polistopadovych-podnikatelu-zmeni-ekonomiku-1435379)
- A PwC partner calls generational change the strongest driver of the M&A market in Czechia and the region — [e15, "Zrychlující Česko vyhlíží erupci akvizic"](https://www.e15.cz/byznys/zrychlujici-cesko-vyhlizi-erupci-akvizic-investori-se-pohrnou-do-technologii-a-realit-1422215) (unconfirmed which page)
- A lawyer quoted by Seznam Zprávy says only a third of firms manage the generational handover (headline only) — [Seznam Zprávy](https://www.seznamzpravy.cz/clanek/generacni-vymenu-zvladne-jen-tretina-firem-rika-pravnik-83237)

### Inferences
- The supply of sellers is real and probably at or near its peak in 2026, and a majority of owners say they would sell outside the family. A buyer's problem is therefore finding and vetting a good firm, not a shortage of firms.
- Navrátil's point matters for a buyer: a firm whose owner has been planning to leave is likely to be under-invested (old machines, no digitalisation, ageing staff). That is both the modernisation opportunity and a hidden capital need.
- Only 8% of AMSP respondents used sale advisory. Most small sales are therefore likely to happen off-market through personal contacts, accountants and suppliers, which favours direct outreach over waiting for listings.

### Gaps
- No figure was found for the number of Czech manufacturing or industrial-service firms offered for sale per year, and no Czech official statistic exists for firm closures due to succession.
- No survey by the Czech Family Business Association (Asociace rodinných firem) was surfaced.
- Sector split (machining, electrical, automation) of firms with owners over 55 was not found.

## A2. Where small Czech firms are bought and sold, asking prices and multiples, deal size for firms with 5–30 employees, and deal structures

### Takeaway
Published Czech guidance puts small manufacturing and engineering firms at roughly 3–6 times EBITDA (commonly 4–5 times), with wide spread driven by owner dependence and customer mix. No transaction database for firms with 5–30 employees was found, so deal size has to be derived by arithmetic. Channels are a handful of portals and brokers, law-firm and advisory boutiques, and bank acquisition desks; many deals are off-market.

### Cited Findings
- Fakturoid's guide gives 4–5 times EBITDA for manufacturing (engineering, light industry), and says larger, more stable firms earn higher multiples — [Fakturoid](https://www.fakturoid.cz/almanach/zacatky-podnikani/jak-prodat-firmu)
- ARROWS law firm: two similar plastics manufacturers can sell at 3.9 times and 5.8 times EBITDA; the difference lies in factors not visible in the accounts. Price is adjusted EBITDA times the multiple, plus or minus cash, inventory, debt and working capital; add-back adjustments can move the value by tens of percent — [ARROWS, "Jak prodat výrobní firmu"](https://arws.cz/novinky-v-arrows/jak-prodat-vyrobni-firmu)
- A range of 3–6 times EBITDA for manufacturing and engineering appears in another Czech advisory source (unconfirmed which page) — [ARROWS, "Jak najít kupce a určit cenu"](https://arws.cz/novinky-v-arrows/jak-najit-kupce-pro-vasi-firmu-a-urcit-jeji-cenu)
- e15 (old article, date unknown, probably early 2010s): most sectors trade around 5–7 times EBITDA, construction averages 2.5 times — [e15](https://www.e15.cz/domaci/nejdrazsi-jsou-zdravotnicke-firmy-nejlevnejsi-stavebni-934410)
- A bank may value the firm below the agreed price; the buyer then covers the difference from own funds, or the price is restructured, for example with an earn-out — [ARROWS, on bank valuation in acquisition financing](https://arws.cz/novinky-v-arrows/prodej-firmy-a-financovani-akvizice-jak-banka-ocenuje-predmet-koupe-a-na-co-se-diva-u-kupujiciho)
- Portals and brokers named in search results: TrhFirem.cz (run by TF Partners, on the market since 2004, about 50 transactions, online valuation tool) — [Podnikatel.cz](https://www.podnikatel.cz/clanky/podnikani-uz-vas-nebavi-ctete-jak-muzete-prodat-firmu-a-na-co-si-dat-pozor/) (unconfirmed which page); InBase in Brno, a database of operating firms for sale "from one million crowns" (2017 article) — [CzechCrunch, 2017](https://cc.cz/inbase-ceska-platforma-na-ktere-lze-koupit-ci-prodat-fungujici-firmu-jiz-za-jeden-milion-korun/)
- Succession advisory boutiques market directly to owners without successors — [Kmošek advisory](https://www.kmosek.com/podnik-na-prodej-nemam-nastupce-sve-firmy/); chamber-of-commerce press describes the sale as the owner's most important deal — [Komora Plus, September 2025](https://komoraplus.cz/2025/09/30/pro-majitele-je-prodej-firmy-nejdulezitejsim-obchodem/)
- Market temperature: e15 and Forbes Česko have reported record sales of Czech firms and prices rising "sometimes by half" (headlines only; dates unknown) — [e15](https://www.e15.cz/byznys/finance-a-bankovnictvi/prodej-ceskych-firem-je-letos-rekordni-1364452), [Forbes Česko](https://forbes.cz/ceny-firem-leti-nahoru-nekdy-az-o-polovinu-vyplati-se-je-neprodat/)
- Seller-side risks listed in Czech guidance include securing the deferred part of the purchase price and withdrawal rights, which confirms that deferred payment is a normal feature of small deals — [Podnikatel.cz](https://www.podnikatel.cz/clanky/podnikani-uz-vas-nebavi-ctete-jak-muzete-prodat-firmu-a-na-co-si-dat-pozor/)
- International practitioner sources list transition periods, employment agreements for the seller, earn-outs and holdbacks tied to customer retention as the standard mitigations for owner dependence and customer concentration — [Searchfunder, on pricing and structuring around customer concentration](https://searchfunder.com/post/how-to-price-structure-and-diligence-around-customer-concentration)

### Inferences
- Illustrative arithmetic only (the EBITDA figures are assumptions, not sourced): a 10–25 person machine shop or electrical firm earning CZK 3–10 million EBITDA, at 3–5 times, implies an enterprise value of roughly CZK 9–50 million (about EUR 0.4–2 million). Firms at the bottom of the size range with heavy owner dependence will often sell nearer asset value.
- These firms are too small for M&A boutiques and bank succession programmes, which focus on deals well above this size, so the realistic channels are portals, local accountants and lawyers, and direct letters to owners.
- The 3.9 versus 5.8 example shows that the multiple is mostly a measure of how replaceable the owner is. A buyer without technical background should expect to pay for owner independence or to accept a long paid transition.

### Gaps
- No Czech dataset of closed deals for firms with 5–30 employees, and no multiples specific to machining, electrical installation or automation integrators.
- No current listing counts from Czech portals, and no bank succession programme details beyond Komerční banka's general page.
- Frequency of seller financing and earn-outs in small Czech deals was not quantified.

## A3. Financing an acquisition in Czechia: bank loans, National Development Bank, search funds and minimum own capital

### Takeaway
Czech banks finance acquisitions with roughly 10–30% buyer equity, debt of about 1.5–3.5 times EBITDA and 5–7 year tenors (2018 teaching material, still the only quantified source found). Search-fund activity in Czechia exists but is tiny (one reported acquisition, a few searchers) and targets firms far larger than a 5–30 employee shop.

### Cited Findings
- Typical Czech acquisition loan structure: own funds usually 10–30% of transaction value, loan volume about 1.5–3.5 times EBITDA, tenor 5–7 years. Two models: dividends from the target repay the loan (fast, no merger, but structurally subordinated), or recapitalisation — [Masaryk University banking course presentation, 2018](https://is.muni.cz/el/econ/podzim2018/MPF_BAN2/um/Prezentace_Akvizicni_financovani.pdf?lang=en)
- Komerční banka offers acquisition and leveraged financing for buying a share or a business, buying out a partner, and explicitly for family firms at generational change — [Komerční banka](https://www.kb.cz/cs/korporace-a-instituce/uvery-a-financovani/strukturovane-financovani/akvizicni-a-dluhove-financovani)
- National Development Bank (NRB, formerly ČMZRB): a "GEN-záruka" guarantee was designed for purchase of a share in, or the business of, a family firm at generational change. The page describing it dates from the ČMZRB era, so whether it is open in 2026 is unconfirmed — [BusinessInfo, ČMZRB services](https://www.businessinfo.cz/navody/nabidka-sluzeb-ceskomoravske-zarucni-a/)
- NRB's "Národní záruka" programme is ending and being replaced by an InvestEU-backed guarantee that the client's commercial bank arranges directly — [e15, 2026](https://www.e15.cz/finexpert/banky-a-ucty/konec-obihani-uradu-statni-zaruku-za-uver-ted-vyridite-primo-ve-sve-bance-1434528), [Finmag topic page](https://www.finmag.cz/tema/narodni-rozvojova-banka)
- First reported Czech search-fund acquisition: Continuum Search Fund bought exhibition-stand makers Best Expo and IRE, with Kocián Šolc Balaštík and Havel & Partners advising (date unknown) — [CEE Legal Matters](https://www.ceelegalmatters.com/czech-republic/9092-kocian-solc-balastik-and-havel-partners-advise-on-first-search-fund-acquisition-in-czech-republic)
- Forbes Česko profiled a young Czech searcher (Martin Komínek) looking to buy one profitable, growing firm (date unknown) — [Forbes Česko](https://forbes.cz/at-je-ziskova-a-rostouci-mlady-investor-hleda-ke-koupi-jednu-jedinou-firmu/)
- A searcher from INSEAD fundraising for a "first partnered search fund in Czechoslovakia" targets at least EUR 1.5 million EBITDA and a minimum enterprise value of EUR 8 million (undated) — [Searchfunder](https://www.searchfunder.com/post/fundraising-for-a-first-partnered-search-fund-in-the-czechoslovakia)
- Poland: Icea's acquisition by Lynx Trail Capital is described as part of a growing search-fund trend — [Techleap finder](https://finder.techleap.nl/news/feed/icea-acquired-by-lynx-trail-capital); a Warsaw search fund founded in 2025 targets firms with PLN 15–150 million revenue and PLN 4–25 million EBITDA — [Aventis Advisors](https://aventis-advisors.com/de/search-fund-unternehmensnachfolge/)
- Europe and Latin America hold 88% of known international search funds and 93.5% of international acquisitions as of December 2025 — [IESE Insight, 2026 report](https://www.iese.edu/insight/articles/search-funds-record-expansion/)

### Inferences
- Applying the 10–30% rule to the illustrative CZK 9–50 million deal range gives own capital of roughly CZK 1–15 million, before transaction costs. A bank will also look at the buyer's sector experience, which a software founder lacks, so seller financing and a co-investor or an inside manager are likely to be needed.
- Classic search-fund investors will not back the purchase of a 10-person shop: their floor is around EUR 1.5 million EBITDA. A solo buyer at small scale is a "self-funded searcher" relying on own cash, a bank loan and a seller note.
- Debt of 1.5–3.5 times EBITDA against a 3–5 times price means a seller note or earn-out for 10–30% of the price is the natural gap filler.

### Gaps
- Current (2026) Czech bank terms, interest rates and minimum deal sizes for acquisition loans were not found.
- Whether any NRB guarantee currently covers purchase of a non-family firm by an outsider is unconfirmed.
- No count of Czech or Central European search funds, and no results data for them, was found. The IESE country tables would have this but could not be opened.

## A4. Evidence on outcomes: what goes wrong, search-fund success and failure rates, and how long search and closing take

### Takeaway
The best base rates come from search funds, which are larger and better resourced than a solo small-firm purchase: about 58% of North American searches end in an acquisition, after a median of about 20 months, and returns are concentrated in a minority of deals. International results were weaker in the 2024 study (18.1% internal rate of return) and stronger in the 2026 study (30%). Practitioner sources consistently name owner dependence, customer concentration and staff departures as the main causes of failure, but no rigorous study of small first-time acquirers was found.

### Cited Findings
- Stanford 2024 study (United States and Canada): 681 search funds, aggregate internal rate of return 35.1% (35.3% in 2022) and return on investment 4.5 times (down from 5.2 times); exited companies returned 42.9% — [Stanford GSB, highlights of the 2024 study](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/index%2Ephp/node/624774), [Searchfunder summary](https://searchfunder.com/post/stanford-2024-search-fund-study-highlights)
- A secondary summary of the 2024 study says nearly 7 in 10 acquired companies produced positive returns, which implies about 3 in 10 acquisitions lost money for investors — [Searchfunder summary](https://searchfunder.com/post/the-2024-search-fund-study-by-stanford-graduate-school-of-business) (unconfirmed against the primary PDF)
- Stanford 2026 study (data to 31 December 2025): aggregate acquisition rate since 1996 is 58%; acquiring a company typically takes around 20 months; aggregate internal rate of return 33.9%, return on investment 4.75 times, public market equivalent 2.88 — [Stanford GSB insight, 13 July 2026](https://gsb.stanford.edu/insights/search-funds-keep-offering-proven-path-ownership), [Stanford GSB, 2026 study selected observations](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/index%2Ephp/faculty-research/case-studies/2026-search-fund-study-selected-observations)
- Conflict between secondary sources on the non-acquisition rate: 58% acquiring implies 42% not acquiring, one blog headline says "52% never close a deal", and another primer says roughly one in three — [Papermark](https://www.papermark.com/blog/search-fund-guide), [Peony](https://www.peony.ink/blog/search-fund-guide). Use the Stanford 58% figure.
- Returns concentrate in a few outcomes — [Founder Institute commentary](https://fi.co/insight/why-search-fund-returns-concentrate-in-a-few-outcomes)
- IESE international study 2024 (outside the United States and Canada): 320 funds in 40 countries; return on investment 2.0 times, internal rate of return 18.1%; 79% of concluded international searches ended in an acquisition — [IESE Insight, 2024 report](https://www.iese.edu/insight/articles/search-funds-global-growth/)
- IESE international study 2026 (data to December 2025): return on investment 2.8 times, internal rate of return 30% — [IESE Insight, 2026 report](https://www.iese.edu/insight/articles/search-funds-record-expansion/)
- Poland: a "Polish Search Fund Study 2026" is cited on LinkedIn as reporting an average search of about 5,500 companies screened over 26 months (weak source) — [LinkedIn profile post](https://pl.linkedin.com/in/marekjakubow)
- Owner dependence: small companies run on one person's knowledge, relationships and judgment; a firm can look profitable because the owner works 60 hours a week and holds all key relationships — [Searchfunder, "How replaceable is that revenue without the seller in the room?"](https://searchfunder.com/post/how-replaceable-is-that-revenue-without-the-seller-in-the-room)
- Commonly listed causes of failed small acquisitions: weak diligence, unrealistic projections, customer concentration, owner dependence, poor financing, employee disruption, no transition plan, a key person leaving after closing — [Acquisition Stars](https://acquisitionstars.com/blog/reasons-business-acquisitions-fail), [Searchfunder, "top 10 reasons lower-middle-market deals fail"](https://www.searchfunder.com/post/i-researched-why-lmm-deals-actually-fail-here-are-the-top-10)
- Customer-concentration thresholds vary by adviser (red flag above 20% in one account in one source, lower in others). A vendor claims 67% of post-close earnings misses in its 340-deal dataset were tied to customer concentration; this is unverified marketing — [Buy and Build Advisors blog, March 2026](https://buyandbuildadvisors.blogspot.com/2026/03/the-5-hidden-risks-that-sink-business.html) (unconfirmed which page carried the 67% figure)
- Change-of-control and assignment clauses in customer contracts can be triggered by an ownership change — [Searchfunder, customer concentration](https://searchfunder.com/post/customer-concentration-2)

### Inferences
- Search-fund base rates are an upper bound for the reader's case. Searchers are typically backed by experienced investors, buy firms with EUR 1.5 million or more EBITDA and a management layer, and still fail to buy in about 4 of 10 cases and lose money in about 3 of 10 acquisitions. A 10-person technical shop with an owner who is also the chief engineer is riskier on owner dependence.
- Time to prove anything: about 20 months of search in North America and about 26 months in Poland, before any modernisation starts. Acquisition does not satisfy the reader's "test quickly at small scale" criterion unless a target is already identified. It gives revenue on day one after closing, but the path to closing is slower than the 6–24 months the reader rejected for the integrator start-up.
- A software founder buying a machining or electrical firm adds a second risk that search-fund data do not cover: the buyer cannot personally cover for a departing technical lead.

### Gaps
- No study of outcomes for small (under EUR 2 million) acquisitions by first-time individual buyers in Europe or Czechia.
- The share of search funds that return less than invested capital, by region, needs the primary Stanford and IESE PDFs.
- No Czech data on post-sale staff turnover or failure of acquired small firms.

## A5. The "AI roll-up" or "AI-enabled services" thesis: who is doing it, which sectors, results so far, and application to industrial or engineering services

### Takeaway
By late 2026 the AI roll-up is a heavily funded but unproven strategy concentrated in white-collar services (accounting, tax, IT services, call centres, property management, insurance-type back offices). Reported results are self-reported and unaudited. No roll-up aimed at industrial, automation or engineering services was found in Europe, and the nearest physical-trades precedent (HVAC roll-ups) is a cautionary one.

### Cited Findings
- Thrive Holdings raised about USD 2 billion at a USD 12 billion valuation from SoftBank, D1 Capital Partners and Altimeter (2026) — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/thrive-holdings-2bn-12bn-valuation-softbank-ai-rollup), [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=3926838)
- Thrive's accounting platform, Current, includes more than 50 firms and over 2,000 accounting professionals; its IT-services roll-up, Shield, passed USD 100 million annual revenue by the end of 2025; it is moving into "built-environment services" (construction certification, infrastructure approval). All figures come from Thrive, are unaudited and have no published baseline — [FourWeekMBA analysis](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-thrive-holdings-ai-owner-not-vendor/)
- General Catalyst has co-created at least ten companies that buy services firms, and earmarked USD 750 million for roll-up strategies in sectors such as call centres and property lettings — [Newcomer](https://www.newcomer.co/p/ai-roll-up-deals-accelerate-as-top), [PE Insights](https://pe-insights.com/thrive-and-general-catalyst-embrace-pe-style-roll-ups-to-build-ai-native-platforms/)
- Newcomer's assessment: it is still early to judge performance of roll-up investments as a category — [Newcomer, "Inside the VC roll-up craze"](https://www.newcomer.co/p/inside-the-vc-roll-up-craze-that)
- Multiplier, founded by a former Stripe executive, raised USD 27.5 million for an AI-powered accounting roll-up (June 2025) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/18/multiplier-founded-by-ex-stripe-exec-nabs-27-5m-to-fuel-ai-powered-accounting-roll-up)
- Europe: Tenet (Berlin) launched in February 2026 with an EUR 80 million first-fund target as an "AI roll-ups" investment firm; its first deal was EUR 5 million into Taxforce, a German tax advisory platform — [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/02/18/berlin-based-eur80m-ai-roll-up-investment-firm-launches/), [Berlin Partner](https://www.invest-in.berlin/n/tenet-europes-first-ai-roll-up-investment-firm-is-here/)
- Europe: Generation Tech Partners (Hamburg) launched a fund of over EUR 50 million in June 2026 to buy about 30 German owner-managed service companies and merge them — [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/06/10/hamburg-based-50m-ai-roll-up-fund-generation-tech-partners-launches)
- Europe: Integral (Berlin; accounting, tax and payroll) reached EUR 12 million total funding and acquired cleverlohn in November 2025 — [BeBeez](https://bebeez.eu/2025/11/19/berlins-integral-boosts-total-funding-to-e12-million-and-strengthens-capabilities-with-cleverlohn-acquisition/)
- Central Europe: Eilla AI reportedly completed "Europe's first M&A deal led by an AI-native advisory", buying two agencies in the region (April 2026 newsletter) — [CEE Startup & Tech Weekly via Pulse](https://www.pulse.bot/ma/news/cee-startup-tech-weekly-europes-first-ma-deal-led-by-an-ai-native-advisory-0e9851e4-b5e5-4432-a301-24c0d01162da/)
- Sceptical view: Tower Research describes the thesis (buy mature low-margin service firms, retrofit AI, end with a large high-margin firm), notes that buying companies is hard, and argues that building new AI-native service firms from zero is the better route. It also says many roll-up backers are motivated by fear that pure software margins will erode — [Tower Research](https://tower-research.com/the-ai-services-opportunity-starts-at-zero)
- Physical trades: HVAC contractors' margins are put at 5–12% by a software vendor — [Simpro](https://www.simprogroup.com/blog/protecting-revenue-and-margins-with-ai-in-2026-for-hvac-companies); NexCore reports 11 HVAC acquisitions since 2023 — [BriefGlance](https://briefglance.com/articles/nexcores-playbook-building-an-hvac-empire-one-acquisition-at-a-time)
- Historical warning: Comfort Systems USA, formed in the 1990s HVAC roll-up wave, saw its share price fall about 85% from its high by March 2000 and recovered by closing or selling weak acquisitions (unverified company-history page) — [company history page](https://emporv7.pages.dev/us/FIX)
- General risk statement: integrating dozens of acquired businesses is operationally brutal, debt can compound faster than synergies, and culture resists standardisation — [Catenaa](https://catenaa.com/markets/global-markets/ai-rollup-strategy-wall-street-deals/) (unconfirmed which page)

### Inferences
- The roll-up investors pick sectors where most labour is document and screen work (bookkeeping, tax filings, help desk). A machine shop or electrical contractor has only a thin layer of such work (quoting, scheduling, documentation, purchasing), so the automatable share of cost is far smaller and the thesis transfers only partly.
- For an integrator or engineering-services firm, the screen-work layer is larger (electrical design, PLC code, documentation, quoting). That is the part of the reader's skill set that could raise margin in an acquired firm, but no investor has published results from doing this.
- The absence of any industrial-services AI roll-up in Europe is a signal both ways: no competition for targets from venture-backed buyers, and no proof that the model works there.

### Gaps
- No audited or independent post-acquisition margin data for any AI roll-up platform.
- No example found of an AI roll-up in automation integration, machining or industrial maintenance in Europe or the United States.
- Thrive's "built-environment services" push is the closest to engineering services; details were not available in extracts.

## A6. Does owning an existing integrator or electrical firm solve the Czech qualification problem?

### Takeaway
Partly. The trade licence and the Technical Inspection (TIČR) authorisation belong to the company, so a share purchase carries them over without re-application. Both depend on named qualified individuals, so the qualification problem turns into a staff-retention problem: if the responsible representative or the qualified electrical staff leave, the firm must replace them or stop the regulated activity.

### Cited Findings
- "Montáž, opravy, revize a zkoušky elektrických zařízení" (installation, repair, inspection and testing of electrical equipment) is a craft trade (řemeslná živnost) that covers reserved electrical equipment — [BusinessInfo](https://www.businessinfo.cz/navody/montaz-opravy-revize-a-zkousky-elektrickych-zarizeni/)
- A Czech legal entity such as an s.r.o. must appoint a responsible representative (odpovědný zástupce) for a craft trade under section 11 of the Trade Licensing Act; preferably a qualified member of the statutory body, otherwise another qualified natural person. One person can hold the role for at most four businesses — [Firmin glossary](https://www.firmin.cz/slovnik/odpovedny-zastupce), [Firmin, electrical trade page](https://www.firmin.cz/zivnosti/montaz-opravy-revize-elektro)
- A change of responsible representative must be reported to the trade office within 15 days (secondary source; verify in the statute) — [Fakturoid, 2026](https://www.fakturoid.cz/almanach/zacatky-podnikani/odpovedny-zastupce-zivnost)
- Secondary sources conflict on whether a qualified managing director must still be formally appointed as responsible representative — [Fakturoid](https://www.fakturoid.cz/almanach/zacatky-podnikani/odpovedny-zastupce-zivnost) versus [Firmin](https://www.firmin.cz/slovnik/odpovedny-zastupce)
- Under Act 250/2021, the Technical Inspection of the Czech Republic issues authorisations to legal entities and self-employed persons for installation, repair and testing of reserved electrical equipment (sections 8 and 9). A sample decision shows the authorisation is valid for 10 years — [sample TIČR decision, hosted file](https://zealous-wren-476.eu-west-1.convex.cloud/api/storage/3276ead6-428d-4701-8584-4a098f8ab3d4) (unusual host; verify), [Act 250/2021 text](https://www.praceamzda.cz/predpisy/250-2021-sb)
- Government Regulation 190/2022: the "professionally competent person" for installation, repair, inspection and testing of reserved electrical equipment is the legal entity or self-employed person holding that authorisation, within the scope in annex 3 — [TZB-info, Regulation 190/2022](https://www.tzb-info.cz/pravni-predpisy/narizeni-c-190-2022-sb-o-vyhrazenych-technickych-elektrickych-zarizenich-a-pozadavcich-na-zajisteni-jejich-bezpecnosti)
- Government Regulation 194/2022 sets the qualification requirements for the natural persons who carry out and manage work on electrical equipment — [TZB-info, Regulation 194/2022](https://www.tzb-info.cz/pravni-predpisy/narizeni-c-194-2022-sb-o-pozadavcich-na-odbornou-zpusobilost-k-vykonu-cinnosti-na-elektrickych-zarizenich-a-na-odbornou-zpusobilost-v-elektrotechnice)
- Commentary on the 2022 recodification of the electrical rules — [Práce a mzda](https://www.praceamzda.cz/clanky/15514/nekolik-postrehu-k-rekodifikaci-elektro-upravy)

### Inferences
- In a share deal (buying the s.r.o.), the trade licence and the TIČR authorisation stay with the company. In an asset deal they do not, and the buyer's own entity would have to apply. A buyer who wants licences on day one should buy shares, which also means inheriting all past liabilities, including machine-manufacturer liability for machines the firm has already put on the market.
- The practical test in due diligence is: who is the responsible representative, who holds the Regulation 194/2022 qualifications (from the author's background knowledge, not confirmed in the search extracts: section 6 electrician, section 7 leading electrician, section 8 inspection technician), how old are they, and are they the seller. If the seller is the only qualified person, the licence leaves with the seller unless a long employment or contractor agreement is part of the deal.
- Acquisition removes the need to find outside partners (electrician, commissioning engineer) only as long as the firm's staff stay. It converts an external-dependency problem into an internal key-person problem, which is easier to manage (employment contracts, retention bonuses, earn-out) but is not eliminated.
- Machine-manufacturer liability under the machinery rules is unchanged by acquisition: the firm still signs the declaration of conformity. What changes is that the firm already has procedures, insurance and a track record.

### Gaps
- The statutory text of section 11 of the Trade Licensing Act, sections 8–9 of Act 250/2021 and the exact conditions under which a TIČR authorisation lapses when qualified staff leave were not confirmed from primary sources; a Czech lawyer or the trade office should confirm.
- Nothing was found on how professional liability insurance or safety-consultant arrangements transfer in a share deal.

## B2. Are physical-world businesses more defensible than software in an AI era? Arguments and data

### Takeaway
In 2026 "atoms over bits" is a mainstream investor narrative, supported by a sharp software-sector sell-off and by capital moving into hardware, defence and automation, but it is opinion and market sentiment, not evidence that small physical businesses earn better returns. The data that exist point the other way on ease: hardware start-ups fail at high rates, need more capital, and both hardware and service businesses run on gross margins well below software.

### Cited Findings
- By early February 2026 the United States software sector was down 22% year to date, a drawdown labelled the "SaaSpocalypse" — [Tema ETFs](https://temaetfs.com/insights/how-to-build-a-moat-ai-cant-disrupt) (unconfirmed which page)
- The core argument: real-world friction (land, regulation, capital intensity, logistics, physical scarcity) creates barriers that abundant intelligence cannot dissolve — [Tema ETFs](https://temaetfs.com/insights/how-to-build-a-moat-ai-cant-disrupt), [Citrini Research, "Atoms vs Bits"](https://www.citriniresearch.com/p/atoms-vs-bits)
- Venture investors are shifting: funding for AI hardware, defence and automation sees large rounds while enterprise software deals slow; a Quiet Capital partner's answer to "what's safe" was "anything touching the physical world", plus operationally intense businesses and hard assets — [VC Cafe](https://www.vccafe.com/how-ais-impact-on-software-is-pushing-vcs-from-bits-to-atoms/)
- Roll-up backers favour services partly because they fear software margins will erode with AI — [Tower Research](https://tower-research.com/the-ai-services-opportunity-starts-at-zero)
- Failure base rates (CB Insights, reported 2017, so dated): about 70% of hardware start-ups die or become "zombies" after raising outside funding; first seed rounds are easy, later rounds are not — [CNBC, 2017](https://www.cnbc.com/2017/09/27/juicero-and-jawbone-havent-put-early-stage-investors-off-hardware.html)
- Only 46% of seed-funded tech start-ups (hardware and software combined) raise another round — [Screw the Valley](https://screwthevalley.substack.com/p/chart-of-the-week-why-do-so-many)
- A "97% fail" figure circulates for hardware; it traces to seed-stage crowdfunded companies, not hardware start-ups in general. CB Insights' top causes across about 400 failed hardware start-ups: lack of consumer demand, high burn rate, loss of interest after crowdfunding — [Wevolver](https://www.wevolver.com/article/why-do-hardware-startups-fail), [MacroFab](https://macrofab.com/blog/why-hardware-startups-fail)
- Gross-margin ranges by business model (blog-level sources, mutually inconsistent): software as a service about 71–74% median (KeyBanc 2025) to 82% for pure software; hardware about 20–35% or 30–50%; hardware with a software layer 35–48%; hardware-enabled software 40–60%; professional services 18–28% and agencies 30–50%; managed services 40–60% with a 46% median — [Stealth Agents benchmarks 2026](https://stealthagents.com/research/startup-gross-margin-benchmarks-2026), [Knowledgelib benchmarks 2026](https://knowledgelib.io/finance/saas-benchmarks/saas-gross-margin-benchmarks/2026), [RaiseReady](https://raisereadybook.pages.dev/blog/gross-margin-by-business-model-whats-good-whats-bad-whats-a-problem)
- Robots-as-a-service margins are below hardware margins, which are below software margins (aggregator commentary) — [IdeaProof](https://ideaproof.io/failure/locus-robotics)
- Contractor margins in a physical trade (HVAC) are 5–12% — [Simpro](https://www.simprogroup.com/blog/protecting-revenue-and-margins-with-ai-in-2026-for-hvac-companies)

### Inferences
- "Defensible" and "profitable" are different claims. Physical businesses are harder to copy because they are harder to build, and the same friction applies to the founder entering them. The moat argument is strongest for businesses that already hold licences, staff, customer relationships and assets, which supports acquisition over a fresh start, and weakest for a new entrant without those.
- The investor narrative mostly concerns large, capital-heavy plays (chips, energy, defence, data-centre supply). It says little about a solo founder's 10-person firm, where defensibility comes from local reputation and qualified people.
- The failure data are old (2017) and skewed to consumer gadgets; they should be quoted as dated and not as a 2026 base rate.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 dataset comparing survival or returns of hardware versus software start-ups was found.
- No margin benchmarks specific to machine shops, automation integrators or Czech electrical firms were found.
- No rigorous study of whether AI has in fact reduced software margins or pricing by 2026 was surfaced; the evidence is share prices and commentary.

## B3. Speed of validation: which physical-world business types reach a paid pilot fastest, sales cycles when selling to small manufacturers, and fast-validation tactics

### Takeaway
Hard data are thin. Vendor sources put manufacturing sales cycles at 4–6 months or more with several decision makers, against roughly 3 months for small businesses generally. The fastest validation documented is direct online pre-selling of a niche product; for business customers, the tools are paid pilots, deposits and option-style pre-orders.

### Cited Findings
- Average sales cycle for manufacturing and capital equipment: 130–180 days, longer above USD 100,000; a single-contact approach fails because several decision makers are involved; pilots lower perceived risk (vendor blog) — [Danish Lead Co](https://danishleadco.io/blog/7-outbound-tactics-for-manufacturers-with-12-month-cycles)
- A job posting at a procurement-software company selling to German mid-sized manufacturers asks for experience with sales cycles of 6 months or more and multiple senior stakeholders — [Tacto posting via Index Ventures](https://www.indexventures.com/startup-jobs/tacto/junior-account-executive/) (unconfirmed which of the listed postings carried the phrase)
- General benchmark: small-business sales cycles about three months, enterprise about seven (not manufacturing-specific) — [B2B Rocket](https://www.b2brocket.ai/blog-posts/smb-vs-enterprise-sales-cycles-5-key-differences)
- A second-hand claim attributed to MIT: mid-market firms scale a successful AI pilot in about 90 days, large enterprises in about nine months (not verified) — [Dev.to article on the German Mittelstand](https://dev.to/digitalcolliers/ai-implementation-for-the-german-mittelstand-where-it-works-where-it-doesnx27t-and-how-to-2m8m)
- Manufacturing buyers do not respond to urgency or limited-time offers (vendor advice) — [Salesmotion](https://salesmotion.io/blog/how-to-sell-to-manufacturing)
- "Sell first, build second": pre-orders both validate demand and fund production; Formlabs paid for its first products with pre-orders — [Predictable Designs](https://predictabledesigns.com/sell-your-product-before-you-make-it), [Predictable Designs, bootstrapped hardware cases](https://predictabledesigns.com/successful-hardware-startups-that-were-bootstrapped/)
- Common validation mistake: asking people whether they would buy, instead of starting from the problem and taking money — [StartupRise](https://startuprise.co.uk/how-to-validate-your-hardware-idea-before-spending-on-manufacturing/)
- Business-customer pre-orders as "B2B Kickstarter": structure the pre-order as an option agreement that expires only after a visible milestone (Boom Supersonic playbook; large-company example) — [Blake Scholl's Substack](https://bscholl.substack.com/p/boom-virgin-and-the-pre-order-playbook)
- Anecdotes: a founder claims EUR 988,000 of product pre-sold in 90 days (headline only) — [Product Hunt post](https://www.producthunt.com/p/self-promotion/how-i-pre-sold-988k-of-product-in-90-days); a solo founder reached USD 50,000 a month with about USD 25,000 of savings by messaging followers individually for pre-orders (secondary summary) — [Weaving Words notes, May 2026](https://weavingwords.pages.dev/notes/2026-05-21_how-i-brought-my-first-product-to-market-idea-to-small-busin/)
- For comparison, the acquisition route takes a median of about 20 months to close in North America and about 26 months in Poland — [Stanford GSB, 2026](https://gsb.stanford.edu/insights/search-funds-keep-offering-proven-path-ownership), [LinkedIn post on the Polish study](https://pl.linkedin.com/in/marekjakubow)

### Inferences
- Ranking by time to first paid evidence, from the cases found: (1) a niche product or kit sold online to individuals or technicians, weeks to a few months; (2) a paid service or feasibility study for a manufacturer using the customer's existing equipment, a few months; (3) capital equipment or installed automation sold to a manufacturer, 4–6 months of selling plus delivery; (4) acquisition, 1–2 years to close.
- Anything sold into a factory inherits the factory's buying pace, whatever the founder's building speed. The reader's AI-driven development speed shortens the build, not the sale.
- A paid feasibility study or audit is the manufacturing equivalent of a pre-order: it tests willingness to pay within one sales conversation and needs no licence.

### Gaps
- No European or Czech data on sales cycles for selling to small manufacturers, pilot pricing or pilot-to-contract conversion.
- No comparative study of time to first revenue by type of physical business.
- Evidence on marketplaces and concierge pilots for industrial offers was not found.

## B4. Frameworks investors and operators use to judge "solo-startable" hardware businesses

### Takeaway
No formal published framework for judging solo-startable hardware or physical businesses was found; what exists is practitioner advice whose common criteria can be assembled into one. The assembled criteria below are an inference from the cases and failure accounts, not an established tool.

### Cited Findings
- Capital and time: hardware needs roughly three times the funding and twice the development cycle of software (aggregator summary of the K-Scale post-mortem) — [IdeaProof](https://ideaproof.io/failure/k-scale-labs)
- Sequencing: start with software because it is easier to iterate, deploy, hire for and fund, and add hardware once customers exist (Cents) — [Founderpath interview](https://founderpath.com/resources/alex-jekowsky-cents-60m-laundry-software-hardware-payments/watch)
- Demand first: the most capital-efficient hardware start-ups sell first and build second — [StartupRise](https://startuprise.co.uk/how-to-validate-your-hardware-idea-before-spending-on-manufacturing/)
- Operations: for a bootstrapped device business, supply chain, support and packaging tooling mattered more than shipping the device — [summary of Michael Lynch's retrospective](https://engineered.at/articles/my-fifth-year-as-a-bootstrapped-founder)
- Differentiation: hardware start-ups in mature markets must solve problems incumbents structurally cannot, not compete on feature parity — [IdeaProof, BuilderX entry](https://ideaproof.io/failure/builderx-robotics-china)
- Margin structure: service-heavy delivery models (robots as a service) carry lower gross margins than hardware sales, which are lower than software — [IdeaProof](https://ideaproof.io/failure/locus-robotics)
- Service businesses: for AI-enabled services, building a new firm from zero is argued to beat buying and retrofitting — [Tower Research](https://tower-research.com/the-ai-services-opportunity-starts-at-zero)
- Top failure causes to screen against: no demand, high burn, reliance on crowdfunding interest — [Wevolver](https://www.wevolver.com/article/why-do-hardware-startups-fail)

### Inferences
- Assembled screening criteria (inference): (1) cash needed before the first paid unit or job; (2) length of one build-test-learn loop, days versus months; (3) regulatory load: does the offer create machine-manufacturer liability, need a licensed trade, or need certification beyond self-declared conformity; (4) how much of the product is bought-in modules versus custom parts; (5) service intensity: on-site installation, commissioning and warranty visits per sale; (6) inventory and working capital per unit of revenue; (7) share of the value that is software the founder can build alone; (8) length and committee size of the customer's buying process; (9) dependence on named outside individuals.
- Scored on these, the documented solo successes (TinyPilot, Jamcorder) are low on 1, 3, 5, 8 and 9 and high on 7. A robot-cell integrator is high on 1, 3, 5, 8 and 9, which matches the reader's reasons for rejecting it. An acquired firm scores well on 3 and 9 only while staff stay, and poorly on 1 and on time to start.

### Gaps
- No investor-published scoring model for solo or bootstrapped hardware was found; accelerator material (for example from hardware-focused investors) was not surfaced in the searches used.
- No data linking these criteria to survival rates.

## B5. 2026 data on how frontier AI models change hardware or mechanical engineering productivity, and scepticism

### Takeaway
Benchmarks published in 2026 show frontier models improving quickly at producing parametric CAD for simple to moderate parts, but the same benchmarks show weak parametric integrity, missing fine detail, and no testing of tolerances or manufacturability. No study measuring real productivity gains for mechanical or hardware engineers was found.

### Cited Findings
- A vendor guide reports an OpenAI self-reported 95.9% geometric-overlap score on an image-to-CAD benchmark for its newest model when tools are available, against 83.3% for the previous model. The model names in the extract ("GPT-6 Astra", "GPT-5.6 Sol") could not be independently verified — [CoLab guide for mechanical engineers, 2026](https://www.colabsoftware.com/guides/chatgpt-for-mechanical-engineers-a-practical-playbook)
- On 100 FreeCAD tasks in a parametric CAD benchmark, mean reward rose from 70.34% to 84.78% between the same two model generations; the survey notes that no benchmark yet tests tolerance specifications or manufacturability of physical parts — [MIT survey on frontier models for 3D modelling, computational design and robotics](https://mit-cdfg.github.io/Survey-AI-for-3D-modeling-Robotics/)
- BenchCAD: models often recover the coarse outer shape while struggling with the parametric construction; fine details go missing, and sweeps, lofts and twisted extrusions get replaced by simpler look-alike constructions. Specialist CAD models outperform general frontier models on its leaderboard — [Klu.ai BenchCAD page](https://klu.ai/benchmarks/benchcad)
- CADEngBench (ICML 2026): current evaluations reward executable geometry more than reusable, physics-valid parametric models; parametric integrity is the main failure point and under-specified prompts are "catastrophic" — [ICML 2026 listing](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/73498)
- RealCADBench (September 2026, built from real industrial design intents): among nine frontier models none leads on all four metrics, and there is a wide gap between code that executes and geometry that is accurate — [arXiv 2609.03773](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.03773v2)
- Selling AI to machine shops is an active commercial field: CloudNC's AI tool-path software is used in over 1,000 shops — [CloudNC](https://www.cloudnc.com/news-room/cloudnc-raises-20m-to-expand-ai-tools-for-precision-machining); a start-up builds AI agents to automate CNC machining — [Manufacturing.net](https://www.manufacturing.net/oracle/news/22936263/startup-develops-ai-agents-to-automate-cnc-machining)

### Inferences
- For a founder with little hardware experience, models in late 2026 can draft brackets, enclosures and fixtures and write the surrounding code, but cannot yet be trusted on tolerances, manufacturability or safety-relevant design without a human who knows the domain. The AI advantage is therefore largest in the screen-work layer (quoting, documentation, PLC and CNC programming assistance, drawings for simple parts) and smallest in the physical verification layer.
- The benchmark trend (about 14 points gained in one model generation on FreeCAD tasks) suggests the gap is closing in geometry but says nothing about physical validation, which remains bounded by build-and-test time.
- Machine-shop AI adoption at over 1,000 shops for one vendor suggests that an acquired shop could buy this capability off the shelf; it is not a proprietary edge for the buyer.

### Gaps
- No controlled study of engineer productivity with AI in mechanical, electrical or controls design was found.
- No 2026 evidence on AI for PLC programming, electrical schematics or machine-safety documentation was searched for, owing to the search cap.
- Benchmark scores are partly self-reported by model vendors and cross-benchmark comparisons are unreliable.
