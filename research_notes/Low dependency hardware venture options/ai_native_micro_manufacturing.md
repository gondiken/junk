Method limits that apply to every section: page fetching was blocked, so all findings come from search-result extracts (28 searches, the full budget). I could not open any page. Each figure is linked to the page the extract appeared to come from, but where one search returned several similar pages the claim-to-URL match is my best reading of the extract. ## 1. Marketplaces that feed orders to small suppliers in Europe (Xometry, Protolabs Network, Laserhub, Craftcloud, Treatstock, FACTUREE, Spanflug, Fractory; Etsy/Amazon/Allegro)

### Takeaway
Xometry Europe is the only network found whose public partner pages describe an open, free, test-part-based entry with no stated ISO requirement; Spanflug, FACTUREE and Protolabs Network describe audited, ISO-9001-centred networks that a brand-new solo shop is unlikely to pass. Supplier-side reports (mostly US forums) say job-board payouts are a fraction of the customer price and falling, so marketplaces look usable as a way to get first paid parts and feedback within weeks, but not as a margin source.

### Cited Findings
- Xometry Europe: partner registration is free, with no subscription fees, no transaction costs and no minimum order requirement; the partner chooses which jobs to accept from a job board matched to declared capabilities — [Xometry EU "Get orders with Xometry"](https://xometry.eu/en/?p=41920); [Xometry EU "Become a Manufacturing Partner"](https://xometry.eu/en/?p=21350)
- Xometry Europe onboarding starts with a test order that is paid like a regular order; after five real orders delivered with good quality and timing the supplier becomes a "trusted partner" with access to the largest orders — [Xometry EU "Become a Manufacturing Partner"](https://xometry.eu/en/?p=21350)
- Xometry pays partners by bank transfer in euro against the partner's own invoice; no payment term in days is stated on the pages found — [Xometry EU "Get orders with Xometry"](https://xometry.eu/en/?p=41920)
- A Xometry partner page (Turkish site) tells sole traders and small businesses that are not VAT payers to put "The supplier is not VAT payer" / "Small business – VAT not applicable" on invoices, which implies non-VAT micro-businesses are accepted there; not confirmed for the EU site — [Xometry TR partner page](https://xometry.com.tr/en/partner-ol/)
- Xometry Europe's network size is stated inconsistently: "over 5000 CNC machines and 3D printers in qualified and audited workshops" on one page, "more than 2000 manufacturing workshops" on another — [Wevolver profile of Xometry Europe](https://www.wevolver.com/profile/xometry.europe); [Xometry EU](https://xometry.eu/en/?p=42720)
- Supplier complaint (US forum, unverified individual post): after job-board changes, offers to partners are "typically 40%–30% or less of the customer price"; the poster no longer expects significant revenue from Xometry — [Practical Machinist thread "Xometry"](https://practicalmachinist.com/forum/threads/xometry.403500/post-3951391)
- Supplier complaint (US forum): a partner who re-quoted job-board parts through the customer tool found Xometry "charging the customer over twice what they were offering for the job" — [Practical Machinist thread "Xometry"](https://practicalmachinist.com/forum/threads/xometry.403500/post-3956708)
- Supplier opinion (Reddit r/CNC, 2025): shops taking Xometry jobs at break-even or below to keep a spindle turning "destroys the actual market" — [r/CNC "Who else hates working with Xometry" (mirror)](https://lr.eu.psf.lt/r/CNC/comments/1jwrlz8/who_else_hates_working_with_xometry_give_me_your)
- Buyer-side use pattern reported on the same forums: Xometry is used for one-off prototype parts that local shops are too busy for; production work goes out for normal quotes — [Practical Machinist "Xometry – who is doing it?"](https://practicalmachinist.com/forum/threads/xometry-who-is-doing-it.351883/)
- Xometry said in a September 2026 release that a rebuilt Job Board cut partners' job review time by more than 60% and raised acceptance rates by about 15% in early testing; the release does not address payout levels (company-reported; the URL is my best match for the syndicated release) — [Taiwan News syndicated release](https://www.taiwannews.com.tw/news/6436131)
- Protolabs Network (formerly Hubs): describes a strict eight-step selection and onboarding sequence per supplier, including a check against qualification criteria, a factory audit and a signed Manufacturing Partner Agreement; processes and certifications are re-audited every two years — [Protolabs "Quality Assurance at Protolabs Network"](https://protolabs.com/about-us/quality-assurance-at-network); [Hubs partner network page](https://www.hubs.com/partner-network)
- Protolabs Network lists the certifications held across its network as ISO 9001, AS 9100, ISO 13485, ISO 14001 and ISO/TS 16949 — [Protolabs "What is Protolabs Network?"](https://www.protolabs.com/about-us/what-is-protolabs-network/)
- Spanflug (Germany, CNC): states that all manufacturing partners in its network are ISO 9001:2015 certified, with targeted selection and continuous auditing — [IndustryArena Spanflug page](https://en.industryarena.com/spanflug/products)
- FACTUREE (Germany): partner companies pass a multi-stage audit that records the production spectrum and each company's strengths; the network operates under FACTUREE's ISO 9001 quality system — [FACTUREE ISO 9001 page](https://www.facturee.de/en/iso-9001-certification/)
- Laserhub (Stuttgart, sheet metal): Laserhub is the customer's sole contracting party, sets the prices and issues the invoices; an algorithm assigns each order to a manufacturer in its network, so the supplier works behind Laserhub's price — [IO+ "Start-up of the day: Laserhub"](https://ioplus.nl/archive/en/start-up-of-the-day-intelligent-acquisition-of-sheet-metal-parts-with-laserhub/); [Acton Capital portfolio page](https://www.actoncapital.com/portfolio/laserhub)
- Fractory: has a "Put Your Machines to Work – Become a Partner" page; a job advert for its partner-network manager describes partner selection on process capability "rather than price alone" and partner files holding onboarding documents, credit agreements, NDAs, SLAs and audit records — [Fractory partner page](https://fractory.com/?p=12180); [job advert copy](https://huntukvisasponsors.com/job/partner-network-manager-uk-region-a15lfmth016b)
- Fractory's platform chooses the production partner by price, location, materials and manufacturing method — [Fractory company page](https://fractory.com/?p=8140)
- Craftcloud (All3DP's 3D-printing marketplace): acts as the customer's contractual service partner, responsible for quality and delivery; partners are "vetted and selected"; Craftcloud takes "a fixed percentage from each order as a commission" (percentage not stated) — [All3DP on Craftcloud](https://all3dp.com/1/craftcloud-cheaper-without-compromising-quality/)
- Etsy: fees take about 11–15% of each sale (2026 seller-guide figure); Etsy enforcement can remove listings or a whole shop — [StackSheriff 3D printing side hustle guide 2026](https://stacksheriff.com/3d-printing/3d-printing-side-hustle-guide/)

### Inferences
- For a solo newcomer the realistic "demand on tap" list is short: Xometry Europe (CNC, 3D printing, sheet metal) and 3D-print marketplaces such as Craftcloud. Spanflug requires ISO 9001; FACTUREE and Protolabs Network audit the factory. ISO 9001 certification for a one-person shop is possible but costs money and months, and it is an outside dependency (certification body).
- Because Xometry, Laserhub and Craftcloud are the contracting party and set the customer price, the supplier never owns the customer. Marketplace volume therefore builds process experience and a quality record on that platform, but not a customer base.
- If the 30–40%-of-customer-price figure from US forums holds in Europe, a job-board part must be made at well under half the direct-market price. That favours suppliers with paid-off machines and low labour cost; a Czech cost base helps, a beginner's slow setup time hurts.

### Gaps
- Xometry Europe partner payment term in days, any minimum machine list, and whether ISO 9001 is required for some job classes: not on the pages returned; needs the partner portal.
- Treatstock: no result at all on supplier terms or commission. Unverified background: it is an open-signup marketplace for print services with a commission on orders.
- Craftcloud commission percentage and whether new one-person farms are being admitted in 2026: not found. One German directory entry (dated May 2025) appeared to describe partners in Germany, Austria and Switzerland, which I could not confirm — [3Dnatives directory](https://www.3dnatives.com/de/3d-druck-branchenbuch/craftcloud/).
- Laserhub and Fractory supplier requirements (machine list, certifications), supplier margins and payment terms: not published in the pages found.
- European supplier-side price reports: all payout complaints found are from US-centred forums (Practical Machinist, r/CNC). I found no European supplier review of Xometry Europe payouts.
- Amazon and Allegro seller economics for manufactured products were not searched (budget).
- Unverified background: Hubs (then 3D Hubs) closed its open peer-to-peer hobbyist printing network in 2018 and moved to vetted professional partners, which is why it no longer suits a small print farm.

## 2. 3D print farms in 2025–2026: economics per printer, farm software, products, solo examples, price competition, Czech specifics

### Takeaway
A print farm is the cheapest and fastest variant to start and the one where slicing, scheduling and order intake are already software, but published per-printer revenue figures are vendor models (about USD 1,000 per printer per month at 60% utilisation) and independent worked examples show near-zero profit for a small generic Etsy farm once labour is counted. Generic products are saturated (tens of thousands of listings per item); the reported successes are own-design niche products, not contract printing.

### Cited Findings
- Vendor model (Flashforge, US dollars): 40 printers at a 60% operating rate give about USD 42,000 monthly revenue, roughly USD 1,000 per printer per month; its 20-printer case assumes 70% utilisation — [Flashforge print farm page](https://enterprise.flashforge.com/pages/3d-print-farm)
- Blog estimate (farm-software vendor): a 6-printer farm at full utilisation could bring in USD 8,000–15,000 a month depending on product mix — [Manuflo blog "How to scale a 3D printing business"](https://manuflo-9y7.pages.dev/blog/post-17-how-to-scale-3d-printing-business)
- Worked example from the same vendor: a 6-printer Etsy-style farm with USD 2,700 monthly revenue leaves USD 44 profit after labour, overhead and machine cost — [Manuflo blog "Print farm profitability"](https://manuflo-9y7.pages.dev/blog/post-14-print-farm-profitability)
- Utilisation: a "good" rate for a farm is put at 70–90%, with 100% rarely achievable — [Print farm throughput calculator page](https://www.ultimatefinancecalculator.com/calculators/print-farm-throughput-calculator)
- Utilisation at the start: SimplyPrint's guide says most farms begin near 15% of capacity and take months to climb — [SimplyPrint "How to start a 3D print farm"](https://simplyprint.io/articles/how-to-start-a-3d-print-farm.md)
- Failure cost: a 10% failure rate raises effective unit cost by about 11% (cost divided by one minus the failure rate) — [Beancount.io on-demand 3D printing bookkeeping, July 2026](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/08/on-demand-3d-printing-service-bookkeeping)
- Bambu Lab Farm Manager: free, runs on the local network without Bambu's cloud, manages an unlimited number of Bambu printers only; Bambu says the current version stays free but paid advanced features may come — [3D Printing Industry](https://3dprintingindustry.com/news/bambu-lab-launches-new-local-print-farm-manager-241739/); [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/3d-printing/bambu-lab-introduces-free-software-to-manage-an-unlimited-number-of-3d-printers-simultaneously-cloud-free-lan-mode-print-farm-manager-program-simplifies-mass-3d-printing)
- One operator in the Netherlands reportedly runs about 35–45 Bambu printers with Farm Manager — [3DPrint.com](https://3dprint.com/319200/bambu-lab-launches-software-to-manage-3d-printer-fleets-no-cloud-needed/)
- Farm Manager is device control only; order-to-print automation for Bambu farms comes from third parties such as Printago (order and commerce automation) and SimplyPrint — [Printago comparison](https://printago.io/blog/bambu-farm-manager-vs-printago); [SimplyPrint comparison](https://simplyprint.io/alternatives/bambu-farm-manager.md)
- Automatic bed clearing on Bambu printers exists as a third-party method (FarmLoop, marketed with SimplyPrint AutoPrint) — [SimplyPrint AutoPrint / FarmLoop](https://simplyprint.io/features/autoprint/farmloop)
- Prusa Pro AFS (Automated Farm System, rack with automatic print collection, managed through Prusa Connect): launch price about EUR 45,000 / USD 50,000 excluding VAT, rising with the number of printers; figure dates from 2022 and no 2026 update was found — [Prusa Research article on AFS pricing](https://prusa3d.com/article/prusa-research-reveals-the-pricing-of-its-afs-automated-print-farm-and-publishes-the-names-of-its-first-customers_233067); [3Printr](https://www.3printr.com/prusa-research-announces-price-for-its-automated-3d-printing-farm-1659973/)
- Saturation: a generic phone stand can have 40,000–70,000 competing Etsy listings — [MeshMinds "Sell 3D prints on Etsy"](https://meshminds.vercel.app/blog/sell-3d-prints-on-etsy)
- Price erosion, first-hand: a seller who sold 15,000 printed products writes that when large print farms enter a saturated market prices erode, competitors priced 20–30% below them, and a seller who does not know their cost floor slides into loss — [Write-up "What I learned from selling 15,000 3D printed products", May 2026](https://weavingwords.pages.dev/notes/2026-05-21_what-i-learned-from-selling-15000-3d-printed-products-4-less/)
- Etsy revenue ranges (2026 guides, unverified, US dollars): top 3D-printing shops USD 5,000–20,000+ a month, median active shop USD 1,000–3,000 a month — [ADP Industries Etsy guide 2026](https://adp-industries.pages.dev/blog/sell-3d-prints-etsy-2026/)
- Part-time sellers with one or two printers: USD 500–2,000 a month on Etsy after 6–12 months of building the shop; top miniature sellers USD 2,000–10,000 a month — [Closo market analysis](https://closo.co/blogs/beginner-guides-how-tos/how-to-sell-3d-prints-2)
- Own-product example (headline only, not opened): "CruiseCup brought in USD 808,000 with a Bambu Lab A1 print farm, and it still couldn't keep up" — [Makers101](https://makers101.com/cruisecup-3d-print-farm/)
- Farm example (headline only, US, tariff context): "How 40 3D printers generated USD 30,000 a month" (about USD 750 per printer per month) — [Webull news](https://www.webull.com/news/12779786262324224)
- Czech pricing method: Prusa's own pricing guide splits cost into material, labour and printer running cost; its example values labour at CZK 210 per hour (2020 Czech average wage) and adds CZK 4.6 per print hour for an MK3S and CZK 2.3 for a MINI; electricity is "tens of haléř to single crowns" per print — [Prusa blog "Jak nacenit 3D tisk na zakázku"](https://blog.prusa3d.com/cs/jak-nacenit-3d-tisk-na-zakazku_38650/)
- Czech market rate (2020 forum, individual posts): one commercial user charges about CZK 100 per print hour; PLA costs just under CZK 0.5 per gram — [Prusa forum "Kolik si říct za tisk"](https://forum.prusa3d.com/forum/original-prusa-i3-mk3-czech-cesky-jak-to-vytisknu-pomoc-s-tiskem/kolik-si-rict-za-tisk/)
- Czech local competition: Prusa Research itself sells professional custom 3D printing to companies — [Prusa "Zakázkový 3D tisk na míru"](https://www.prusa3d.com/cs/stranka/zakazkovy-3d-tisk-na-miru_240720/)
- Czech start-up budget guidance: CZK 50,000–250,000 for one or more mid-range printers — [Shopify CZ "Jak podnikat v 3D tisku"](https://www.shopify.com/cz/blog/jak-podnikat-v-3d-tisku)
- Czech electricity 2026: average energy component about CZK 2.24 per kWh excluding VAT in April 2026 (CZK 2,119 per MWh in January) — [Ušetřeno.cz](https://www.usetreno.cz/energie-elektrina/prumerna-cena-elektriny/)
- Czech electricity 2026: the regulated component for low-voltage customers (households and businesses) falls 15.1% from CZK 2,772 to CZK 2,352 per MWh from January 2026 — [BusinessInfo.cz on the ERÚ price decision](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/od-ledna-2026-nizsi-ceny-elektriny-pro-domacnosti-a-firmy-eru-vydal-zmenovy-cenovy-vymer/)

### Inferences
- Adding the two electricity components gives roughly CZK 4.6 per kWh before VAT and taxes for a small low-voltage business in 2026. With a desktop FDM printer averaging on the order of 0.1–0.15 kW (unverified background figure), power costs well under CZK 1 per print hour. Electricity is not a deciding cost for a print farm; labour and failed prints are.
- A Czech contract-print rate near CZK 100 per print hour (2020) is about EUR 4. A printer at 60% utilisation runs about 430 hours a month, so contract printing at that rate grosses about CZK 43,000 (about EUR 1,700) per printer-month before material and labour only if the hours are sold, and the 15% starting utilisation figure says they mostly are not at first. This is my arithmetic on a dated forum rate, not a measured figure.
- The gap between the vendor model (USD 1,000 per printer-month) and the worked Etsy example (USD 450 per printer-month and no profit) suggests the outcome is decided by product choice and sales channel, not by the machines.
- Software leverage is high and already commoditised: slicing, queueing, fleet monitoring and order routing are sold as cheap or free tools. A founder's own agent harness adds value mainly in product design generation, listing and customer-message handling, and demand testing across many product ideas, not in farm control.
- Prusa being Czech is a mixed factor: local parts, service and community, but also a strong local contract-printing competitor and a very high density of printer owners who can print for themselves or undercut.

### Gaps
- No independent, audited figure for revenue per printer, utilisation or FDM failure rate was found; every number above is a vendor model, calculator or blog.
- No reliable solo print-farm case with verified revenue beyond two headlines I could not open (CruiseCup, the 40-printer farm).
- Current printer prices were not searched. Unverified background: Bambu Lab A1 / P1-class printers cost roughly EUR 300–700 and Prusa MK4S / Core One roughly EUR 800–1,200, so ten printers cost about EUR 3,000–12,000.
- Resin printing economics, belt printers and Prusa's 2026 AFS status were not covered by any result.
- Czech contract-printing price lists for 2025–2026 and the number and size of Czech print-service competitors: not found; the only rate is a 2020 forum post.
- Reddit supplier-side threads (r/3Dprinting, r/BambuLab, r/3DprintEntrepreneurs) did not return usable content.

## 3. AI-native machining: CAM automation tools, venture-backed "AI-native factories", machine and tooling cost, beginner learning time, Czech sector and hourly rates

### Takeaway
CAM and quoting assistance is now cheap and widely deployed (CloudNC reports more than 1,000 shops; Toolpath is priced at about USD 125–160 a month), which means software leverage in machining is available to every incumbent shop and is not a newcomer's advantage. The capital-rich AI-native factories (Hadrian, Isembard, Daedalus) aim at certified aerospace and defence work that a solo shop cannot serve; the solo entrant instead competes with thousands of established small job shops, and Czech hourly rates found (about CZK 700–1,800, roughly EUR 28–72) are well below the German guide range of EUR 70–120 for 3-axis milling.

A Czech solo shop therefore has a cost edge selling into Germany, but the same edge is held by the many existing Czech job shops with skilled operators earning CZK 150–220 per hour.
- The AI-native factory companies show investors' thesis that software can replace scarce machinist skill, but they build it inside vertically integrated, certified plants for defence and aerospace. Their existence does not open a path for a solo entrant; the tools that do (CAM Assist, Toolpath) are sold to every shop, so they raise the floor for all rather than giving a newcomer an edge.
- Isembard's franchise model is the one item that points at small operators, but it is UK-based and no terms were found.
- What CAM automation does not remove is the manual part: workholding, tool setting, first-article proving, deburring and inspection. Those are exactly where a person with no machine experience is slowest, and where scrap and crashes cost money.
- A realistic entry budget for CNC is far above a print farm: a usable used industrial mill is EUR 9,000–49,000 before transport, three-phase power, tooling, workholding and measuring equipment; a benchtop machine is about USD 5,000–30,000 but limits materials, tolerances and marketplace eligibility.

## 4. Laser cutting and sheet metal on demand: entry-level fibre laser prices, online instant-quote players, margins

### Takeaway
A new entry-level fibre laser from an Asian brand is quoted at roughly USD 15,000–35,000 for 1–1.5 kW and USD 35,000–72,000 for 3 kW before chiller, gas, extraction and installation, so capital is comparable to a used CNC mill. The European on-demand channel (Laserhub, Fractory, Xometry) sets the customer price itself and I found no published supplier margins, so demand evidence for a solo entrant is the weakest of the four variants.

### Cited Findings

- Sheet-metal customers usually want cut, bent and often welded or coated parts. A laser alone covers only flat blanks; a press brake and finishing add capital and skill. A solo laser-only shop is therefore a narrow supplier to these platforms.

## 5. Small-batch PCB assembly: entry cost of a desktop pick-and-place and reflow line, competition from JLCPCB/PCBWay and European assemblers, quick-turn local niche

### Takeaway
A desktop assembly line is cheap to enter (an open-source pick-and-place kit cost about USD 1,150 in 2022; faster desktop machines are a few thousand dollars), but the machines are slow (about 500 to 3,500 components per hour) and the market is squeezed between very cheap Chinese assembly and established European quick-turn houses (Eurocircuits offers assembled prototypes in 6 working days). The only niche the sources support is speed and local handling for small runs, and I found no marketplace that feeds such orders to small assemblers.

## 7. Regulatory and practical burden in Czechia/EU: trade licence category, workshop rules, GPSR, product liability, machinery obligations for own-use machines

### Takeaway
Printing plastic products fits a free trade (notification only, no qualification), while CNC machining ("Obráběčství") is a craft trade that requires a vocational certificate or years of documented practice, or else a qualified responsible representative, which is an outside dependency for a founder with no machining background. Buying CE-marked machines and using them does not make the founder a machine manufacturer, but building or substantially modifying a machine for own use does; own products sold to consumers carry GPSR manufacturer duties with no small-seller exemption.

### Cited Findings
- The free trade field "Výroba plastových a pryžových výrobků" covers production of semi-finished and finished plastic products, plastic packaging, household goods, plastic toys, sports equipment and more; it excludes vulcanisation of rubber compounds and production of plastics in primary form — [BusinessInfo.cz, content of the free trade](https://www.businessinfo.cz/navody/vyroba-plastovych-a-pryzovych-vyrobku/)
- "Obráběčství" (machining) is a craft trade covering production and repair of semi-finished products, parts and products by all kinds of machine chip removal, such as turning, milling and grinding — [BusinessInfo.cz "Obráběčství – živnostenské oprávnění krok za krokem"](https://www.businessinfo.cz/navody/obrabecstvi/)
- A craft trade requires proof of professional competence: a vocational certificate in the field (sources differ on added practice: three years' practice in one document, one year in older ministry material), or substitutes such as a certificate in a related field, a retraining certificate, or several years of practice in the field (four years in one source, six in another) — [Dostupný advokát "Řemeslná živnost – podmínky a obory"](https://dostupnyadvokat.cz/blog/remeslna-zivnost); [BusinessInfo.cz craft trade guide](https://www.businessinfo.cz/navody/remeslna-zivnost-po-sidlo-na-uzemi-cr/)
- A trade can be run through a responsible representative who meets the competence conditions; the entrepreneur chooses the person, the relationship is contractual, the representative answers for proper operation, and a replacement must be appointed within 15 days if the person stops qualifying — [Dostupný advokát "Řemeslná živnost"](https://dostupnyadvokat.cz/blog/remeslna-zivnost)
- A business premises (provozovna) must be notified to the trade licensing office no later than three days before activity starts — [Dostupný advokát "Provozovna"](https://dostupnyadvokat.cz/blog/provozovna)
- A family house is normally approved as a building for housing; setting up a workshop or premises in a house or garage usually needs a change of use handled with the building authority, and the zoning plan must allow it — [MT Projekt "Jak vybudovat provozovnu v rodinném domě"](https://mtprojekt.cz/clanky/jak_vybudovat_provozovnu_v_rodinnem_dome/); [Generali Česká "Jak na umístění provozovny a podnikání z domova"](https://www.generaliceskaprofi.cz/-/jak-na-umisteni-provozovny-a-podnikani-z-domova)
- The premises must meet hygiene limits including noise, plus lighting, heating and ventilation requirements; a disruptive activity may not be approved, and fines for breaches can reach hundreds of thousands to millions of crowns (commercial advice article; the statute number it cited looked wrong to me) — [Dostupný advokát "Provozovna v bytě či rodinném domě"](https://dostupnyadvokat.cz/blog/provozovna-v-byte-rodinnem-dome)
- The Czech Ombudsman's collected opinions note that an operator within noise limits can still disturb neighbours beyond what is reasonable (civil nuisance) — [Veřejný ochránce práv, "Hluková zátěž"](https://www.ochrance.cz/vystupy/edice-stanoviska/Hlukova_zatez.pdf)
- EU General Product Safety Regulation: a seller who places a product on the market under their own name or trademark is the manufacturer; guides state there is no exemption for small, handmade or low-volume sellers — [Craftybase "What is GPSR"](https://craftybase.com/blog/what-is-gpsr); [Chartered Institute of Export "GPSR essentials"](https://www.export.org.uk/insights/how-to/gpsr-essentials-a-practical-guide-for-small-businesses/)
- GPSR manufacturer duties (Article 9, per a compliance guide) cover labelling, a safety/risk assessment and technical documentation; for simple products the guide calls the requirements "manageable"; manufacturer details and safety information must appear in listings — [EU Seller Ready GPSR guide for Etsy sellers](https://eusellerready.pages.dev/gpsr-compliance-for-etsy-sellers/); [EUverify guide](https://euverify.com/resource/gpsr-compliance-for-etsy-listings/)
- Enforcement in practice: Etsy does not fine but removes or hides listings or suspends shops; national market-surveillance authorities can fine; the most common outcome for a small seller is listing removal — [StallScore Etsy GPSR guide](https://stallscore.pages.dev/learn/etsy-gpsr)
- Machinery rules: the obligations that fall on machine manufacturers also apply to anyone producing a machine or safety component for their own use — [Belgian FPS Economy, machine safety](https://economie.fgov.be/en/themes/quality-and-safety/safety-products-and-services/specific-regulations/machines-and-rented-products/safety-machines)
- Under Regulation (EU) 2023/1230, whoever makes a "substantial modification" to machinery is treated as its manufacturer (risk assessment, technical file, conformity assessment, declaration, marking); the regulation applies from 20 January 2027, replacing Directive 2006/42/EC — [LexisNexis practice note](https://www.lexisnexis.com/en-gb/legal/guidance/the-eu-machinery-regulation); [Fabrico blog](https://www.fabrico.io/blog/eu-machinery-regulation-2023-1230/)

### Inferences
- Variant (a), a print farm, has the lightest licensing: one free-trade notification covers making plastic products. Variant (b) is the only one where the licence itself blocks a solo beginner: without a vocational certificate or years of practice the founder needs a responsible representative or a retraining certificate first.
- For own-use equipment the dividing line is clear enough for planning: buying CE-marked printers, mills, lasers or pick-and-place machines and operating them makes the founder a user, not a manufacturer. Building custom automation around them (a home-made bed-clearing robot, a self-built machine enclosure with interlocks, linking machines into one cell) can cross into manufacturer duties for own-use machinery. This matters for a software person tempted to automate the farm with self-built hardware.
- Selling contract parts made to a customer's drawing keeps product-safety responsibility mostly with the customer who places the final product on the market; selling own-brand products makes the founder the GPSR manufacturer. So the "own products" route trades marketplace dependence for product-compliance work.
- A garage or house workshop is the main practical risk for the noisy variants (CNC, laser with extraction and compressor): change of use, zoning and neighbour noise. A rented light-industrial unit avoids this at a monthly cost. Print farms and desktop PCB lines are quiet, though fumes (resin, soldering flux) still need ventilation.

### Gaps
- Exact current wording of the Trade Licensing Act on proof of competence for craft trades (sections 21–22): the sources conflict on years of practice; needs the consolidated statute. Unverified background: alternatives include a related-field certificate plus one year's practice, a retraining or professional-qualification certificate plus one year, or six years' practice.
- Whether 3D printing as a service is officially classed under the plastic-products free trade: no source addressed it directly; it is the obvious fit but unconfirmed.
- Licence category for laser cutting of sheet metal (possibly the craft trade "Zámečnictví, nástrojářství" or a free-trade metal-products field) and for PCB assembly (unverified background: a free-trade field covers electronic components and equipment working at low voltage, while a craft trade covers electrical machines and apparatus generally): not confirmed.
- Whether simple desktop CNC routing or engraving in non-metals falls outside "Obráběčství": not found.
- Czech fire-safety rules for a small workshop, and occupational-safety duties of a self-employed person without employees using work equipment: no source returned. Unverified background: the user-side rules come from the EU Work Equipment Directive 2009/104/EC and Czech government regulation 378/2001 Sb., with some duties extended to self-employed persons by Act 309/2006 Sb.
- Product liability: not covered by any result. Unverified background: the new Product Liability Directive (EU) 2024/2853 must be applied by member states from 9 December 2026, keeps strict liability for defective products and extends it to software; product liability insurance is the usual mitigation.
- The statute number for noise limits cited by one source (205/2020 Sb.) looks wrong; unverified background says the Public Health Protection Act is 258/2000 Sb. with noise limits in government regulation 272/2011 Sb.
- No source addressed GPSR specifically for 3D-printed goods, or CE marking duties (toys, electronics, food-contact items) for printed or assembled own products.

## 8. Failure modes: commodity pricing, machine downtime, quality claims, post-processing time, capital tied in machines, marketplace dependence

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced failure modes are price erosion in commodity work (Etsy generic prints; marketplace job boards paying 30–40% of customer price), under-utilisation (farms start near 15% of capacity), and unpaid labour swallowing the margin (a 6-printer farm with USD 2,700 revenue and USD 44 profit). Evidence on downtime, quality claims and capital losses was not found and remains judgement.

### Cited Findings
- Commodity pricing, products: competitors undercut by 20–30% once large farms enter a saturated category; sellers without a known cost floor slide into loss — [Seller write-up, May 2026](https://weavingwords.pages.dev/notes/2026-05-21_what-i-learned-from-selling-15000-3d-printed-products-4-less/)
- Commodity pricing, contract work: job-board offers at 30–40% or less of customer price (US forum post) — [Practical Machinist](https://practicalmachinist.com/forum/threads/xometry.403500/post-3951391)

- Under-utilisation: most farms start near 15% of capacity — [SimplyPrint](https://simplyprint.io/articles/how-to-start-a-3d-print-farm.md)

- Durable advantage in every variant comes from what the marketplace cannot take back: own product designs and brand, direct repeat customers, and process data for a narrow part family. Pure job-board work accumulates only a supplier rating on someone else's platform.
- The variants where machines are cheap (a, d) fail through price competition and labour; the variants where machines are expensive (b, c) fail through idle capital and the learning curve. No variant found avoids both.
- For a software-skilled founder with no machine experience, the evidence points to (a) with own-design products in a narrow niche as the only variant that meets "alone, within weeks", with (d) second if a first customer is already known. Variant (b) conflicts with the constraints on both licence and time to competence, even though it has the clearest marketplace on-ramp and a Czech-versus-German rate advantage.
