## 1. What consultancies and tool vendors charge for Cyber Resilience Act help

### Takeaway
Almost nobody publishes a price. Four searches found exactly two figures: a TÜV NORD CRA workshop seminar at 1,470 EUR net per participant, and a small SaaS tool ("CRA-Ready") at 99 USD per month. Every consultancy and every firmware or SBOM platform quotes on request, which means a visible fixed price would itself be a differentiator, but also that the price level a small manufacturer accepts is unproven.

### Inferences
- The market is priced like classic consulting (quote after a call), not like a productised service. That fits buyers who are large enough to run a tender; it is a poor fit for a 30-person machine builder who wants to know the cost before the first call. A published fixed price for a narrow first step is an open position, at least in what search engines surface.
- Researcher's price hypothesis, built only from the anchors above and not from any observed CRA quote: (1) scoping and gap assessment for one product family, 2–5 working days, about 2,000–5,000 EUR; (2) SBOM generation plus vulnerability-monitoring set-up for one product, about 1,500–4,000 EUR; (3) monitoring and triage retainer, a few hundred EUR per product per month; (4) full technical-documentation pack (risk assessment, SBOM, vulnerability-handling policy, user security information, draft declaration) in the range the CE market already accepts, 5,000–20,000 EUR per product family. Treat all four as numbers to test in customer conversations.
- The floor is set by tools: 99 USD/month for SBOM plus monitoring on three repositories, and 119–399 EUR/month for AI-drafted CE files. A service has to sell what the tool does not do: deciding scope and class, getting a usable component inventory out of firmware and PLC projects, and writing documents the manufacturer can sign.
- The TÜV figure is useful in another way: a manufacturer already pays about 1,500 EUR per head for a generic seminar. A product-specific half-day review at a similar price is an easy first sale to justify.

### Gaps
- No day rate, package price or quote for CRA consulting in Germany, Austria or Czechia was found. No Czech-language price of any kind.
- No price for ONEKEY, Cybellum, Finite State, Anchore or comparable firmware and SBOM platforms. Background, unverified: these are typically sold as annual enterprise subscriptions in the five- to six-figure EUR range, which would put them out of reach for small makers; this needs a quote to confirm.
- The Commission's own estimate of compliance cost per manufacturer (impact assessment) was not retrieved.
- Whether the valantic tool, the chamber checks and the 28digital workshop are free was not visible.

### Inferences
- Background, unverified (check against EUR-Lex before use), on what a typical machine builder must do under the three regimes:
  - CRA: most machines, PLC-based controls and industrial devices fall in the default class, so the manufacturer self-assesses (no notified body). Industrial control systems and robot controllers were on the "important products" list in the 2022 proposal but are not in the final Annex III; industrial firewalls and intrusion-detection products, routers, operating systems and some microcontrollers are. Duties: cyber risk assessment, the essential requirements of Annex I, an SBOM in the technical file, a vulnerability-handling process and free security updates for a support period of at least five years in most cases, user security information, EU declaration of conformity, and reporting through the SRP.
  - Machinery Regulation from 20 January 2027: two cyber-related essential requirements (protection against corruption, and safety and reliability of control systems against malicious third-party interference) that apply only where an attack could lead to a hazardous situation. A dedicated harmonised standard was in preparation; its status in late 2026 was not checked.
  - RED: the delegated act on cybersecurity for radio equipment has applied since 1 August 2025 with the EN 18031 series as harmonised standards, and is expected to be replaced by the CRA once that applies fully. A machine with a built-in radio module mostly relies on the module maker's conformity, but a maker of its own wireless device is already under these rules today.
- The practical picture for a small machine builder: three overlapping sets of cyber clauses within 13 months (MR January 2027, RED already, CRA December 2027) and one technical file that has to answer all of them. A single combined "cyber annex to the technical file" is a coherent thing to sell.
- Background, unverified, on population size: Germany is usually said to have around 6,500 machine-building firms with 20 or more staff; Eurostat structural business statistics put NACE 28 in the EU at several tens of thousands of enterprises, most of them small; Czechia has on the order of a few thousand NACE 28 enterprises including micro firms. Makers of electronics (NACE 26) and electrical equipment (NACE 27) add to this. None of these numbers was retrieved in this session.
- Many small "machine builders" are integrators who combine bought-in CE-marked controllers. Their CRA exposure depends on whether they place a product with digital elements on the market under their own name (the machine with its control software counts) and, in practice, on what their customers demand in purchase contracts. The second driver may arrive earlier than the legal date.

### Gaps
- No count of machine builders or makers of products with digital elements for Czechia, Slovakia, Germany or Austria, and no share of small firms. ## 4. Readiness and urgency: surveys, standards, relief for small firms, delays, reporting in practice

### Takeaway
The December 2027 application date stands as far as any extract shows; what slipped is the standards work, with a Commission draft pushing the 2026 deadlines for harmonised standards back by two months. The reporting platform went live on 11 September 2026 and reached version 1.2.1 on 20 October 2026, but no account of how reporting works in practice, and no 2026 preparedness survey beyond the Bitkom figure, was found.

### Cited Findings
- State of play (independent guide site, undated page): the remaining horizontal standards are still due and the product-specific (vertical) standards are still expected; the European standards bodies are drafting them. — [cyberresilienceact.eu, state of play](https://cyberresilienceact.eu/state-of-play.html)

### Inferences
- Three version bumps of the platform within six weeks of launch (to 1.2.1 by 20 October) and a FAQ revision on 15 October suggest a system still being stabilised. Combined with the earlier finding that it started without automated interfaces, reporting in late 2026 is a manual, form-based process.
- For a small machine builder the reporting duty is real but rarely triggered: it bites only when a vulnerability in its own product is actively exploited or a severe incident occurs. The work that can be sold now is the preparation (who watches for vulnerabilities in the components, who files within 24 hours, registration on the platform), not the reporting itself.
- Standards being late cuts both ways for a consultant. Clients can argue "wait until the standards are out"; a provider can argue that the essential requirements and the documentation duty do not depend on the standards, and that default-class products self-assess anyway. Expect the first argument to win with many small firms until customers or large OEM buyers start demanding evidence.
- Background, unverified, on relief for small firms: the CRA tells the Commission to provide a simplified technical-documentation format for micro and small enterprises and asks member states for support measures such as awareness, training and test environments; micro and small enterprises are shielded from fines for missing the 24-hour early-warning deadline. Whether the simplified format had been published by November 2026 was not found. If it exists, it lowers the document workload but not the SBOM, risk-assessment or update duties.
- Background, unverified: the "June 2028" tail date is the end of validity for certain certificates issued under other EU rules before the CRA applies.

### Inferences
- Buyers in this field choose on proof of similar work and a recognisable badge, in that order. The badges that recur are IEC 62443 training certificates, ISO 27001 lead auditor and CISSP. - The founder's software background is a real asset for the half of the CRA that incumbents from the machine-safety world find hard (SBOM from build systems and binaries, vulnerability monitoring, update mechanisms, secure-development process). Contract terms should define deliverables as drafts for the manufacturer's approval, cap liability at the fee or the insurance sum, and professional indemnity cover for IT consulting should be in place before the first paid job. Cost and availability of such cover for a Czech sole trader were not researched.
- A limited company (s.r.o.) instead of a sole trader is the usual way to contain this risk in Czechia; not researched here.

## 8. Overall judgement against the objective (is this a real, fast-to-prove opening for one person; what to sell, at what price, against whom; where it leads)

### Takeaway
The opening is real in law and in timing but unproven in willingness to pay: duties are fixed, 13 months remain, incumbents sell upmarket on request-only pricing, and no one visibly serves small machine and device makers with a fixed-price, product-level offer. What desk research could not establish is the one thing that decides it: whether small makers will pay now, before standards are final and before their customers force the issue. That can only be settled by selling.

### Cited Findings
- Demand side: duties apply (reporting since 11 September 2026, platform live), full application December 2027, standards late, companies want clarity, small firms at risk of falling behind. — [ENISA SRP FAQ](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/product-security/single-reporting-platform-srp/frequently-asked-questions); [cyberresilienceact.eu news](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/news/cra-standardisation-deadlines-pushed-back-two-months.html); [produktion.de](https://www.produktion.de/technik/cyber-resilience-act-warum-firmen-investieren-muessen/2637153)
- Supply side: many providers, none with public prices for consulting; a seminar at 1,470 EUR net; tools from 99 USD/month; a funded startup generating CRA documentation. — [TÜV NORD](https://www.tuev-nord.de/de/weiterbildung/seminare/cra-workshop-cyber-resilience-act-erfolgreich-umsetzen); [Capterra, CRA-Ready](https://www.capterra.com/p/10045966/CRA-Ready/); [pulse.bot, CRACI](https://www.pulse.bot/venture-capital/news/craci-raises-14m-for-eu-cybersecurity-compliance-platform-ce73ef96-5bbe-4e60-90c4-ee3571148feb/)
- Credibility side: visible independents carry CISSP, ISO 27001 lead auditor and IEC 62443 references; adverts ask for five years of industrial security. — [freelancermap profile](https://www.freelancermap.de/profil/david-kleinz); [freelancermap project](https://www.freelancermap.de/projekt/ot-security-and-ai-compliance-iec-62443-eu-ai-act-manufacturing-remote)
- Czech side: attention is on the NIS2 law; the Chamber of Commerce has begun CRA awareness; local CRA providers are large firms. — [Advokátní deník](https://advokatnidenik.cz/2025/11/03/novy-zakon-o-kyberneticke-bezpecnosti-vstoupil-1-listopadu-v-ucinnost/); [BusinessInfo.cz](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/hospodarska-komora-upozornuje-firmy-na-nove-povinnosti-podle-cyber-resilience-act/)

### Inferences
- What exactly would be sold (researcher's proposal, to be tested): a fixed-price "CRA starter file" for one product or machine type from a small maker, consisting of a scope and class decision, a component inventory and SBOM for the control software and firmware, a first cyber risk assessment, a vulnerability-handling and reporting procedure including SRP registration, and a gap list against Annex I; followed by a per-product monitoring subscription. For machine builders, add the Machinery Regulation cyber clauses that apply from 20 January 2027 as the near-term hook.
- At what price: unverified hypothesis of 2,000–5,000 EUR for the starter step, 5,000–20,000 EUR for a full file, and a few hundred EUR per product per month for monitoring (see section 1 for the anchors). No observed CRA quote supports these.
- Against whom: test houses and consultancies above (stronger brand, higher price, generic method), tools below (cheap, but built for software repositories), supplier declarations and free guidance beside (may persuade small firms they need nothing more), and experienced freelance industrial-security consultants at the same level (stronger references, limited capacity).
- Fast proof: a two- to four-week test is feasible without partners: a published fixed price, a worked example file, and direct outreach to 50–100 makers of connected devices and machines in Czechia, Germany and Austria, plus listings where chambers and associations point members. A reasonable pass mark is two or three paid starter engagements. Electronic-device makers with their own firmware are probably easier first clients for a software founder than machine builders, because the product is software-shaped and the RED cybersecurity rules already apply to wireless devices; this ordering is a judgement, not a finding.
- Where it leads: the per-product monitoring duty over a support period of years gives recurring revenue, and the extraction and drafting pipeline can become a vertical tool. It is unlikely to become a venture-scale company from this starting point; it can become a small product business or an acquisition target for a CE-tool or test-house buyer (no precedent found).
- Main reasons it could fail: small makers defer until late 2027 or until standards are final; buyers insist on industrial-security references; tools and supplier-provided SBOMs make the service look unnecessary; a rush in the second half of 2027 arrives when a one-person firm cannot scale to meet it.

### Gaps
