# Quick screen of four uncovered physical-world ideas (Czechia, as of November 2026)

Method limits, which apply to every section: 12 web searches in total (the hard cap), no page fetching. Every figure below comes from a search-result extract, not from the page itself. Several extracts did not show the article date or which article carried which number; those are flagged. Statements marked "background, unverified" are from the researcher's general knowledge and were not checked in this session. Searches spent: energy 4, OEM modules 3, 3D scanning 3, printed tooling 2. All four ideas got some coverage; ideas 3 and 4 returned almost no Czech price evidence.

## 1. Energy management hardware in Czechia (controllers for solar, batteries, heat pumps, chargers against spot prices, sold through installers)

### Takeaway
The installed base is large (about 157,000 batteries, nearly 2 GWh, date unconfirmed), but the household market is shrinking after subsidies ended, and the control layer is already supplied free or bundled: by a funded Czech aggregator that is also the electricity supplier (Delta Green, Proteus), by Loxone, and by free Home Assistant integrations. On this evidence it does not displace the options already found; the only one-person version is a software or configuration service, and the money per household is a few thousand crowns a year.

### Cited Findings

Installed base and market direction
- 36,246 solar installations with battery storage were connected in the reporting year, with 506 MWh of capacity; households were 84% of them and the average home battery was 11.7 kWh. In total 157,507 battery storage units with almost 2 GWh are connected in Czechia. The extract did not show the reporting year (my reading: full-year 2024, unconfirmed) or which of two oEnergetika articles carries the figures. — [oEnergetika, corporate solar grows, home installs slow](https://oenergetice.cz/elektrarny-cr/firemnich-fotovoltaik-v-cr-pribyva-rozvoj-domacich-instalaci-zpomalil); [oEnergetika, nine in ten new plants have storage](https://oenergetice.cz/obnovitelne-zdroje-energie/devet-z-deseti-novych-solarnich-elektraren-ma-system-na-akumulaci-energie)
- The Solar Association says almost 95% of home solar installations have a battery, and its representative Jan Fousek calls Czechia the European record holder for batteries in home solar. Date not shown. — [oEnergetika](https://oenergetice.cz/obnovitelne-zdroje-energie/devet-z-deseti-novych-solarnich-elektraren-ma-system-na-akumulaci-energie)
- A Seznam Zprávy article headlined "The end of subsidies hit the home solar market, a quarter fewer are being built" reports that new home batteries fell year on year and only 41% of solar plants in the first half were built with a battery. - Headlines across several years say the same thing: the home solar boom "hit a ceiling" and "the time of companies" is coming; large plants are being added while the household market slows. — [Seznam Zprávy](https://www.seznamzpravy.cz/clanek/ekonomika-firmy-boom-domacich-solarnich-elektraren-narazil-na-strop-prichazi-cas-firem-255900); [oEnergetika](https://oenergetice.cz/obnovitelne-zdroje-energie/v-cr-loni-zacaly-pribyvat-velke-solarni-elektrarny-trh-domacnosti-zpomalil)

Delta Green and Proteus (the main Czech incumbent)
- Delta Green is a Prague company that aggregates household flexibility into a virtual power plant from home solar, batteries and other devices. It is an electricity supplier: Proteus users must be Delta Green supply customers. — [Balkan Green Energy News, April 2025](https://balkangreenenergynews.com/delta-green-launches-platform-in-cee-for-households-to-provide-grid-balancing/); [Ekonomický deník](https://ekonomickydenik.cz/cesi-maji-novou-moznost-jak-usetrit-zapojenim-baterie-do-stabilizace-site/)
- Proteus launched in spring 2024. It plans household consumption ahead, discharges batteries or sells solar surplus when electricity is expensive or the grid is overloaded. Conditions: a smart interval meter, rooftop solar, a battery, a spot tariff, a Delta Green supply contract and the Proteus app. The grid-balancing service was open to only a smaller part of households at the time of writing. — [Ekonomický deník](https://ekonomickydenik.cz/cesi-maji-novou-moznost-jak-usetrit-zapojenim-baterie-do-stabilizace-site/); [Kurzy.cz](https://zpravy.kurzy.cz/796321-nenechavejte-baterii-a-fve-lezet-ladem-vyuzivejte-je-k-vyrovnavani-site/)
- Reward to the household: E.ON, Delta Green's partner in Czechia, says customers can earn "up to several thousand crowns a year". Indicative, from a partner, not guaranteed. — [Ekonomický deník](https://ekonomickydenik.cz/cesi-maji-novou-moznost-jak-usetrit-zapojenim-baterie-do-stabilizace-site/) (the extract did not show which of the Czech articles carries this line)
- Scale: 200 households used the balancing service at the start of one December with over 100 waiting; in that December Delta Green certified a 1 MW aggregation block made only of households with the grid operator ČEPS and Nano Energies; about 200 households make 1 MW. Later reports say it supplies about 5,550 households and aims for 10,000 by year-end. Years not shown in the extracts (my reading: December 2024, then 2025 or 2026). — [České noviny/ČTK](https://www.ceskenoviny.cz/zpravy/2473083); [České noviny/ČTK](https://www.ceskenoviny.cz/zpravy/2652530); [CzechCrunch](https://cc.cz/z-prahy-propojuji-evropske-domacnosti-a-tvori-z-nich-obri-elektrarnu-jejich-cena-je-ve-stamilionech/)
- Funding: Credo Ventures, Tilia Impact Ventures and Purple Ventures invested €2 million in October 2025; another database records the round as $2.4 million. CzechCrunch's headline puts the company's value "in the hundreds of millions" of crowns. — [Techleap finder](https://finder.techleap.nl/news/feed/delta-green-secures-2m-for-expansion); [Seedtable](https://seedtable.com/companies/delta-green/funding-rounds/seed-2025-10); [CzechCrunch](https://cc.cz/domacnostem-umoznuji-vydelavat-na-elektrine-vyrovnavanim-site-investuje-do-nich-tilia-i-credo/)
- Delta Green is hiring trading data analysts and a go-to-market engineer in 2026 and is pitching the Romanian market. — [rejobs.org](https://rejobs.org/cs/prace-v-obnovitelne-energetice/137751-senior-trading-data-analyst-delta-green); [Energynomics](https://www.energynomics.ro/en/romanias-imbalance-problem-is-becoming-a-household-opportunity-how-can-delta-green-help)

Other existing products
- Loxone sells a "spot price optimiser" that downloads exchange prices and shifts appliances to the cheapest hours, as part of its home energy management product. — [Loxone, energy management](https://www.loxone.com/cscz/produkty/energie/energeticky-management-domacnosti/); [Loxone SK, spot prices](https://www.loxone.com/sksk/blog/spotovy-trh-spotove-ceny/)
- Loxone advertises that a subsidy of up to CZK 140,000 is available when the solar system includes "smart control" and joins energy sharing. Programme and current validity not confirmed. — [Loxone blog](https://www.loxone.com/cscz/blog/dotace-chytra-fve/)
- Free open-source alternatives exist: a Brno University of Technology thesis describes "photoptimizer", a Home Assistant integration for GoodWe and Growatt inverters that combines spot prices, production and consumption forecasts and battery state; another thesis does the same with Home Assistant automations and blueprints for spot and fixed tariffs. — [VUT thesis, Home Assistant control](https://dspace.vut.cz/items/c98c04a8-aed4-414b-a02d-6ae40010a502); [thesis record](https://invenio.nusl.cz/record/775803)
- One measured saving: a Czech Technical University thesis (2024) reports a client who bought electricity at an average CZK 1.09/kWh and saved about CZK 32,000 (period and system not shown in the extract). — [ČVUT thesis, Slezák 2024](https://dspace.cvut.cz/bitstream/handle/10467/113301/F3-DP-2024-Slezak-Ondrej-DP-Slezak-Ondrej-2024.pdf)

### Inferences
- What one person can do alone: write optimisation software that talks to inverters over the local network or vendor cloud. A physical controller is a different matter: it must carry CE marking (background, unverified: low-voltage, electromagnetic compatibility and, with radio, the Radio Equipment Directive with its cybersecurity requirements), and anything wired into the switchboard or switching a heat pump or charger must be installed by a person with Czech electrical qualification (background, unverified: Act 250/2021 and Government Regulation 194/2022). So the founder is the manufacturer of the controller and depends on installers for every sale. This repeats the partner dependence the reader rejected, in a milder form.
- The strongest incumbent does not sell a box. Delta Green earns from electricity supply and from selling aggregated flexibility to the grid operator, and gives the optimiser away. A solo founder cannot match that without becoming a licensed electricity trader or partnering with one.
- The saving available to pay for a controller is small: "several thousand crowns a year" from balancing, and one case of about CZK 32,000 total. A controller priced in the low tens of thousands of crowns plus installation has a multi-year payback, and competes with free software on hardware the household already owns.
- Residential demand is falling with the subsidies; growth has moved to commercial sites and larger batteries. If anything here is open, it is small-business sites (several loads, 15-minute pricing, no vendor cloud that covers the mix), not homes. This was not researched.
- Software share is high (forecasting, optimisation, device integrations) and AI coding help applies fully. But the part that is hard is not the optimiser; it is integrating dozens of inverter, heat pump and charger models and supporting them in the field.
- What accumulates: device integrations and an installer network. Neither is protected; inverter vendors can close or change interfaces at any time.
- Main failure modes: inverter vendors and energy suppliers bundle the function; support load from hundreds of device combinations; installer channel is slow and wants margin; subsidy rules change demand overnight.
- Rough cost and time to a first paid job (estimate, no source): a software-only setup service on Home Assistant could be sold within weeks for a few thousand crowns per home; a certified hardware product is 6–12 months and tens of thousands of euros.

### Gaps
- No figures found for the heat pump installed base, home car chargers, or the share of households on spot tariffs in 2026.
- Nothing found on Woltair, on Nano Energies' own products (only its role in the 1 MW certification), or on inverter vendors' own optimisers. Not researched for lack of budget.
- No prices found for Loxone hardware or any Czech controller product; no installer margins.
- State of flexibility regulation in 2026 not confirmed. Background, unverified: independent aggregation and storage were written into the Energy Act by the 2025 amendment known as Lex OZE III, and the national energy data centre (EDC) was due to reach full operation in mid-2026.
- Dates of most installed-base figures are unconfirmed (see flags above).

## 2. AI or vision modules supplied to machine builders (founder is component and software supplier; the builder stays the manufacturer)

### Takeaway
The commercial model is well established and suits the liability constraint: vision software is sold to machine builders as a development licence plus a runtime licence per machine or station, and the Czech firm Pekat Vision shows a small supplier can win (about €500 per line per month, sold for €16 million in 2022). No evidence was found on design-in time or on small and mid-sized builders buying from one-person suppliers, and the incumbents are many; this is better treated as a sales channel for the vision work already covered than as a new option.

### Cited Findings
- Licensing pattern: Zebra's Aurora line (formerly Matrox) needs separate development licences and a runtime licence for each production system; optional modules (deep learning, 3D, OCR) add licence cost per machine; pricing is quote-based. Source is a comparison site, not the vendor. — [rfp.wiki, Matrox vs Robovision](https://www.rfp.wiki/specialty-industries/manufacturing/machine-vision-software/matrox-imaging/robovision)
- Adaptive Vision (also Zebra) licenses per station with dongles or PC binding, which reviewers say surprises buyers and creates replacement risk when a machine fails. — [rfp.wiki, Adaptive Vision](https://www.rfp.wiki/vendors/adaptive-vision)
- NI requires a runtime (deployment) licence for each deployed system; an integrator states that each executable shipped to a customer is bundled with a runtime licence. — [NI forum](https://forums.ni.com/t5/Machine-Vision/Vision-Development-Module-License/td-p/4062358); [NI forum, 2017](https://forums.ni.com/t5/Machine-Vision/Run-time-License/m-p/3576773)
- MVTec describes the device-maker lifecycle as: buy the software, develop the application, implement it in the device, roll out the device bundled with the software. — [MVTec embedded vision flyer](https://www.mvtec.com/fileadmin/Redaktion/mvtec.com/products/embedded_vision/MVTec_embedded_vision_flyer.pdf)
- Pekat Vision (Brno) splits development and runtime licences and offers a cheaper "runtime without training" licence for additional lines. — [Pekat documentation](https://pekatvision.atlassian.net/wiki/x/pIESF)
- Pekat Vision's business rests on system integrators (for example Techno Horizon in Japan); the software is sold as a subscription at roughly €500 per line per month. Article date not shown, probably before 2022. — [Forbes.cz](https://forbes.cz/vzdal-se-dobre-prace-a-zacal-podnikat-ted-petr-smid-miri-se-svym-detektivem-do-celeho-sveta/)
- Datalogic bought Pekat Vision in March 2022 for €16 million (Czech press: about CZK 400 million), to build its algorithms into Datalogic hardware. — [Imaging and Machine Vision Europe](https://www.imveurope.com/news/datalogic-buys-ai-firm-pekat-vision-16m); [Forbes.cz](https://forbes.cz/italsky-nakup-za-400-milionu-brnensky-startup-pekat-vision-meni-majitele/); [Datalogic press release](https://www.datalogic.com/eng/media-center/press-releases/datalogic-acquires-pekat-vision-nd-8048.html)
- Pekat reached builders through distributors: UK distributor Multipix presented it as helping "OEMs and vision solution providers"; Swedish distributor OEM Automatic ran webinars. — [Automation Magazine UK](https://www.automationmagazine.co.uk/pekat-vision-software-for-industrial-visual-inspection/)
- A Czech state business portal article is titled "Pekat Vision knows AI, and is not the only one in Czechia" (other Czech suppliers exist; names not in the extract). — [BusinessInfo.cz](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/pekat-vision-umi-s-umelou-inteligenci-a-neni-v-cr-jediny/)
- Vendor ecosystems act as a channel: camera maker IDS runs an AI marketplace that connects suppliers of finished vision solutions with end customers. — [Computer & Automation](https://www.computer-automation.de/bildverarbeitung/ki-marktplatz-gelauncht.htm)
- A product called AI_Control, a complete AI vision solution for real-time detection and control in machines, is listed in the Siemens ecosystem. The page address suggests it comes from a small engineering office (Ingenieurbüro Roth), not from Siemens itself; not confirmed. — [Siemens ecosystem listing](https://www.siemens.com/de-de/ecosystem/ingenieurburo-roth/)
- Packaging-machine builder Viking Masek and sensor maker SICK announced a "strategic OEM partnership" (headline only). — [Plant Automation Technology](https://plantautomation-technology.com/news/viking-masek-robotics-automation-and-sick-forge-strategic-oem-partnership)
- A vision start-up is advertising for a "Director OEM Partnerships" to drive adoption by machine builders and structure hardware-centred deals (company not named in the extract). — [job advert](https://mynejo.com/de-de/job/director-oem-partnerships~d85a90a3-54b4-42c6-bd4d-a089b6d82f32)
- Demand signal: 43% of surveyed German automotive executives already use some form of AI such as deep learning in machine vision (survey sponsor and year not shown). — [it-daily.net](https://www.it-daily.net/shortnews/ki-machine-vision)

### Inferences
- Liability: this is the cleanest of the four on the reader's main objection. The machine builder signs the declaration of conformity and is the manufacturer; the supplier's exposure is contractual, provided the module performs no safety function. Background, unverified: the new EU Machinery Regulation (2023/1230, applying from 20 January 2027) treats software that performs a safety function and is sold separately as a safety component, and the new Product Liability Directive (2024/2853, applying to products placed on the market from 9 December 2026) names software as a product and lets claimants reach component makers. Keep the module out of safety functions and cap liability in the contract.
- No licences or partners are needed to start; the work is almost entirely software, so AI coding help applies fully. Hardware is a camera and an industrial PC bought off the shelf.
- Time is the problem. No source gave design-in times, but the lifecycle MVTec describes (develop, implement, roll out) runs on the builder's machine-generation schedule. A paid proof-of-concept or development contract within weeks is plausible; recurring per-machine licence income is likely a year or more away (estimate, no source).
- Pekat's path is the relevant precedent and a caution: it sold mostly through integrators and distributors at about €500 per line per month, needed a team and several years, and ended inside a hardware vendor. Datalogic, Zebra, MVTec, Cognex-class vendors and camera makers with on-camera AI are what a builder compares a solo supplier against. A builder also worries whether a one-person supplier will exist in ten years, since it must support its machines that long.
- What accumulates: per-machine-type models and training data, and being designed in (switching costs are high once the builder has shipped machines with the module). That is a real advantage, but only after the first design win.
- Main failure modes: long sales cycle with a few dozen possible customers; builder takes the work in-house after the proof-of-concept; price pressure from bundled runtime licences; dependence on one or two customers.
- Overlap: technically this is the same work as the "AI visual inspection stations" option already covered. The difference is who the customer is and who carries the liability.

### Gaps
- No published per-machine licence prices or royalty rates for OEM deals; no data on design-in time.
- No direct evidence that small and mid-sized builders in Czechia or Germany buy from one- or few-person suppliers, and no named Czech builders that do.
- No examples found of small suppliers other than Pekat (pre-acquisition) and the unconfirmed Siemens ecosystem listing.
- AI Act obligations for AI embedded in machinery in 2026–2027 were not checked.

## 3. 3D scanning, metrology and reverse-engineering services

### Takeaway
A single person can plainly start this within weeks with one scanner, but the searches returned no commercial Czech prices, no count of providers and no evidence of AI doing the scan-to-CAD step, so there is no basis for ranking it above the options already found. The little that was found points to an existing field of small providers and universities, and to hardware getting cheap, which lowers both the entry cost and the barrier for everyone else.

### Cited Findings
- The only Czech price list found is a university one: Masaryk University's anthropology institute (2021) charges CZK 600 per hour for digitisation work, plus 35% overhead and 21% VAT; its example job (digitising and reconstructing a skull) came to CZK 78,000 before VAT. Not an industrial rate. — [Masaryk University price list, 2021](https://cdn.muni.cz/media/3329545/cenik-celkovy_ua-2021.pdf)
- Universities are equipping their own labs: Jan Evangelista Purkyně University ran a 2025 tender for a reverse-engineering laboratory, including a handheld optical scanner with laser projection and photogrammetry. — [UJEP public contract 2025/0176](https://ezak.ujep.cz/vz00002757)
- Small Czech providers that advertise scanning plus reverse engineering include VZKS (Velké Meziříčí; scanning, reverse engineering, design of machine parts, dimension control), 3D Tisk Čechy, and Alvipek. Directory listing only; no prices. — [Firmy.cz listing](https://www.firmy.cz/stitek/3d-modely/kraj-vysocina/zdar-nad-sazavou/5281-bory)
- The standard deliverable chain is scan, refined polygon mesh (STL), then conversion to a surface or solid CAD model (STEP, IGES). — [Technical University of Liberec presentation](https://new.fm.tul.cz/files/pages/other/MTI/obr19/TUL-Mendricky.pdf)
- Scanner accuracy claims in 2026: FARO Edge ScanArm HD about 0.025 mm and Scanology KSCAN-E 0.020 mm, per a January 2026 buyer's guide that reads like a paid press release; no prices given. — [buyer's guide, January 2026](https://business.mammothtimes.com/mammothtimes/article/getnews-2026-1-22-top-industrial-3d-scannersbuyers-guide-for-metrology-and-qc-2026)
- Low-cost scanners now claim the same class: a 2026 blog review says the Creality CR-Scan Raptor claims 0.02 mm with blue laser; Revopoint's MetroX line is marketed as metrology-grade at low price. Weak sources, no prices. — [Raptor review blog, April 2026](https://fulbarri.blogspot.com/2026/04/creality-cr-scan-raptor-review.html); [MetroX Pro review](https://www.3dtechvalley.com/revopoint-metrox-pro-review/)
- The only price seen: Shining 3D FreeScan UE Pro at roughly $8,000–9,300 on Alibaba marketplace listings (not an official price; configuration unknown). — [Alibaba listing](https://electronics.alibaba.com/product/30-scanner)
- Creaform's HandySCAN Silver series is described as the established metrology-grade handheld at an "accessible price" (no figure). — [cadcam.org](https://www.cadcam.org/creaform/creaform-handyscan-3d-silver-series)
- AI in scanning software, as far as found, is limited to marker-free tracking and automatic gap-filling in meshes; nothing found on AI scan-to-CAD. — [Barchart, 2026 industrial scanner market release](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/1407126/2026-industrial-3d-scanner-market-insights-moving-beyond-parameter-chasing-to-realworld-scenarios)

### Inferences
- What one person can do alone: everything. No licence is needed to scan and model. Liability is that of a service provider for the report or model, not of a manufacturer. The limit is inspection reports that a customer uses for acceptance: automotive customers commonly expect calibrated, traceable equipment and sometimes an accredited laboratory (background, unverified), which a cheap scanner and a sole trader do not provide. Reverse engineering of legacy parts has no such requirement.
- Money and time (estimate; scanner prices are background, unverified): about €1,000–3,000 for a blue-laser prosumer scanner, €8,000–25,000 for an entry metrology-grade handheld, €30,000 or more for the Creaform/Scantech class with certified accuracy, plus €2,000–10,000 a year for inspection and reverse-engineering software. A first paid reverse-engineering job within two to four weeks is realistic.
- Software share: scanning is manual and on site; the CAD reconstruction is skilled manual modelling. The reader's software skill gives little advantage today. An AI scan-to-CAD tool would be the software opportunity here, but no evidence was found that it works or that anyone sells it.
- What accumulates: little. Reputation and a library of part models for repeat customers. Hours are sold one at a time.
- Main failure modes: hourly-rate ceiling; customers with regular need buy their own scanner as prices fall; inspection work goes to accredited labs; irregular demand.
- Possible value as an add-on: a scanner makes idea 4 (printed tooling and legacy spare parts) and any fixture work easier, since most shop-floor parts have no CAD model.

### Gaps
- No commercial Czech prices per hour or per part were found in two targeted searches; results were mostly theses. Providers appear not to publish price lists.
- No count of Czech providers; no demand data.
- Scanner prices in 2026 not confirmed from vendors or resellers.
- AI scan-to-CAD tools not found; the search did not cover dedicated products, so absence here is weak evidence.

## 4. On-demand printed tooling and spare parts for local factories (jigs, fixtures, gripper fingers, replacement parts)

### Takeaway
The evidence found is that factories do use printed jigs and fixtures widely, but every case study is about bringing the printing in-house to replace outside suppliers, which is the opposite of what this business needs. No Czech supplier prices or case studies were found. Cheap and fast to test, but with no sign of a durable advantage; it does not displace the earlier options on this evidence.

### Cited Findings
- 57% of respondents in Jabil's 2021 survey said their company uses 3D printing for tooling, jigs and fixtures, up from 30% in 2017 and 37% in 2019. Jabil is a vendor. — [Jabil](https://jabil.com:443/blog/3d-printing-jigs-fixtures-tooling.html)
- Jabil reports average savings of $1,500–2,500 from printed tools in its plants (unit of saving not shown in the extract). — [Jabil](https://jabil.com:443/blog/3d-printing-jigs-fixtures-tooling.html)
- Volkswagen Autoeuropa cut tool purchasing cost by 91% compared with external suppliers and implementation time by 95% by printing in-house. Source is printer vendor material. — [Ultimaker](https://ultimaker.com/de/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/6251)
- Musashi: outsourced jigs took 30 days; in-house printing cut that to 7 days. — [Raise3D](https://raise3d.com/fr/application-jigs-and-fixtures/) (vendor; the extract did not show which vendor page carries this case)
- Pankl Racing Systems found in-house printing 48 times faster and 12 times cheaper than outsourcing milled jigs. Vendor material. — [Formlabs white paper](https://formlabs.com/eu/white-papers/how-to-3d-print-in-house-jigs-fixtures-and-other-manufacturing-aids/)
- Durability: a Polish study of printed flexible grippers found at least several thousand cycles achievable, with sintered nylon (SLS) parts performing best because of their uniform structure. — [MATEC Web of Conferences, 2019](https://www.matec-conferences.org/10.1051/matecconf/201925402020)
- Costing model: a Brno University of Technology presentation notes that printing cost scales with part mass while machining cost scales with shape complexity, and names one-off parts needed within 24 hours as the typical case for printing. — [VUT presentation](https://dspace.vut.cz/bitstreams/f7f761e7-7831-4b7a-87a4-d6352dce2a2d/download)
- Czech firms advertising printing together with scanning and reverse engineering: 3D Tisk Čechy, Alvipek. Directory listing only. — [Firmy.cz listing](https://www.firmy.cz/stitek/3d-modely/kraj-vysocina/zdar-nad-sazavou/5281-bory)

### Inferences
- What one person can do alone: design and print to order with no licence. Liability is that of a parts supplier; it is light for jigs and fixtures and heavier for spare parts that go into a machine (the printed part may change the machine's conformity; a part that fails and causes damage is the supplier's product). Avoid load-bearing and safety-related spares.
- Money and time (estimate; prices are background, unverified): €1,500–5,000 for one or two enclosed printers able to run carbon-fibre nylon and similar engineering filaments; sintered nylon needs €30,000 or more or can be bought in from a bureau. A first paid fixture within two to three weeks is realistic if the founder can get onto a shop floor.
- The value is in the design, not the print. Factories that pay an outsider are paying for someone to look at the station, design the fixture and iterate. That is engineering time sold by the hour, and the customer can replace the supplier with a €2,000 printer and a technician once the use is proven; the vendor case studies above describe exactly that move.
- Software share: moderate. Fixture design from a part model can be partly automated (generating nests, clamps and gripper fingers from CAD), and that is where the reader's skills would count; no evidence was found of customers paying for it as a product.
- What accumulates: a library of fixture designs per customer and familiarity with their parts; weak protection.
- Main failure modes: customers bring it in-house; online print bureaus undercut on price per part; small order values (tens to low hundreds of euros) with high visit and design time; no CAD for legacy parts, which then needs scanning (idea 3).
- Ideas 3 and 4 fit together as one small local service (scan, model, print), but the combination is still hours for money.
