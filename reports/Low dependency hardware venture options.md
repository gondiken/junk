# Start with test stations, not robots

Build and sell functional test stations to small electronics makers, and offer the same companies a fixed-price cybersecurity compliance file. Of the 25 options researched, these two best meet your two tests, and a third, camera inspection of parts that are sorted by hand today, earns a cheap check but not yet a purchase. A functional test station is a bench fixture plus software that powers up each finished circuit board, loads its firmware, measures it and records a pass or fail. All three can be started by one person with no licensed partner and without becoming a machine manufacturer, and each can produce a paying customer or a clear refusal in two to eight weeks, against 6–24 months on the integrator route. What you give up is size and robots: a test fixture sells for roughly USD 2,000–20,000 where a Czech cobot cell sells for CZK 0.7–1.2 million, and none of the three involves a robot. The research could not show that customers will pay, because nobody in these trades publishes prices, and every finding rests on search-result extracts because page fetching was blocked. So the first step is not to found a company. It is to spend four weeks and under about EUR 2,000 asking 30 electronics firms, 60 device and machine makers and a handful of automotive suppliers for money, with the pass marks and decision rule set out at the end.

**How far to trust this.** All ten research streams were limited to search-result extracts and a fixed number of searches; no page was opened in full. Prices, dates and company facts are leads to verify. The ratings in the comparison table are the researchers' judgement built on those extracts, and time and capital figures are estimates unless a source is linked. Items the researchers knew from memory but could not confirm were left out of this report or are flagged as unverified.

| Term | Meaning here |
|---|---|
| Integrator route | The earlier recommendation: build cobot cells (a small robot arm plus gripper, frame and controls) for small factories. It needs an electrically qualified person, a machinery-safety consultant and a commissioning engineer; whoever signs the cell's declaration of conformity is legally its manufacturer; the earlier report estimated 6–9 months to a supervised first cell and 12–24 months to independent delivery. |
| Self-declaration | The manufacturer itself declares that a product meets EU rules (the CE mark) without a third-party test body. |
| EMS | Contract electronics manufacturer: a firm that assembles circuit boards designed by others. |
| Test station, fixture | A fixture holds a finished board against spring-loaded probes; the station's software runs the checks. |
| CRA | Cyber Resilience Act, the EU law that makes cybersecurity a condition for selling any product with software in it. |
| SBOM | Software bill of materials: the list of software components inside a product, which the CRA requires. |
| Sorting, containment | When an automotive supplier ships defective parts, people check every part by hand until the cause is fixed. Specialist firms sell this labour. |
| Signal-only | An inspection station that shows pass or fail on a light, screen or signal wire and never moves, rejects or stops anything itself. |

Three patterns explain the table. Options with moving machines or licensed trades bring the partner back under another name: an installer, a responsible representative for the trade licence, or a key employee. Options that are quick and solo are mostly quick and solo for everyone, so an incumbent has already published the price. And several options that sound physical are software or hourly services once you look at where the work is done. The three that survive share a shape: the customer is a business, the delivery is a one-off judged against a written acceptance test or a document the customer signs, nothing moves or runs on mains voltage, and mistakes are caught on your own bench or desk before they reach the customer.

## 3. The ranked three, and what each costs you against the integrator route

### First: functional test stations for small electronics makers

Every circuit board that goes into production needs a tester built for that board, so the purchase recurs with each new product. Vendor sources put a fixture at **USD 2,000–20,000 and two to six weeks** of lead time ([AllPCB](https://www.allpcb.com/allelectrohub/flying-probe-vs-fixture-testing-cost), [Bester PCBA](https://www.besterpcba.com/fixture-service-midvolume-test)), and one assembler contrasts a USD 2,000 functional setup with "the $40,000 paperweight" bought by low-volume makers who over-specify ([Bester PCBA](https://www.besterpcba.com/low-volume-hardware-flexible-testing)). The offer the research points to is a fixed-price station for low-voltage boards: a manual fixture on a bought-in kit, firmware loading, power and communication checks, serial numbers and results logged to a database, and the source code handed over. Exclude mains-powered products and pneumatic presses, which bring equipment-safety duties that were not researched.

This is the option where your skills do most of the work. The core is Python; Google's open-source OpenHTF framework structures it ([GitHub](https://github.com/google/openhtf)), and one practitioner describes a production tester made of OpenHTF, a laptop, a USB cable and about 150 lines of prompted Python ([EmbeddedRelated](https://www.embeddedrelated.com/showabstract/632.php)). Errors surface on your bench against known-good and deliberately broken boards, which is a far kinder loop than AI board design, where the best model passed 8.15% of tasks ([HWE-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18102)). No mature tool yet turns a schematic into test code; the nearest product describes itself as early ([TestFlow AI](https://www.hunted.space/product/testflow-ai)). The asset that builds up is a reusable station platform and each client's production data. FixturFab in the US began exactly this way, with a contractor who kept needing fixtures for clients ([CircuitHub](https://circuithub.com/post/ep-41-how-to-develop-a-pcba-functional-test-system-w-fixturfab)).

The evidence on local demand cuts both ways. Six searches in Czech, Slovak and English found no local firm advertising small, fast, fixed-price fixtures; the local suppliers that surfaced sell their own tester platforms ([UNITES Systems](https://columbia.etesters.com/catalog/B6741A88-1422-08DF-AA5D-3808DB1BA9B1/unites-systems-a-s/)). Small makers visibly have the need: one Czech product company's subsidy record describes manual board testing and a plan to buy automated testers ([RISY](https://www.risy.cz/en/vyhledavace/projekty-eu?page=1435)), and radio-modem maker RACOM had its tester built as a master's thesis ([CTU](https://dspace.cvut.cz/entities/publication/93677dba-c3d0-4809-9797-409052a1b066)). That same thesis shows the substitute: firms are used to getting testers almost free from students, or building them in-house as Ryston does ([DPS Elektronika](https://www.dps-az.cz/clanky/id:71917/vyvoj-nove-generace-ate-automatickeho-testovaciho-systemu-pro-osazene-desky-plosnych-spoju)). Three limits follow. The home market is small, about **61 Czech contract assemblers in 2017 against 424 in Germany** ([iConnect007](https://iconnect007.com/article/106217/the-past-and-the-future-of-the-european-ems-industry/106220/smt)), so product companies and German-speaking customers must be in range from the start. An employed test engineer in Brno is advertised at CZK 50,000–80,000 gross a month ([Pracomat](https://www.pracomat.cz/prace/4_2016977-test-engineer-hw-fw-50-80-000-kc)), so the buyer is a firm with no such person, or one whose engineer is overloaded, and the price has to stay near one or two months of that cost. And Chinese factories build a simple jig for "a few hundred to a few thousand dollars" ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/china-sourcing-agents/functional-test-jigs-end-of-line-testing-at-china-factories-51cn)), so you win on software quality, data and ownership of the code, never on price.

### Second: fixed-price cybersecurity compliance files

The CRA's duty to report exploited vulnerabilities has applied since 11 September 2026, and its full requirements, including conformity assessment and the duty to supply security updates, apply from **11 December 2027** ([ENISA](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/product-security/single-reporting-platform-srp/frequently-asked-questions), [CMS](https://cms.law/en/bel/legal-updates/ready-respond-report-the-cyber-resilience-act-s-reporting-regime-is-here)), with fines up to EUR 15 million or 2.5% of turnover ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/07/eu-cyber-resilience-act-24hour-reporting-duties-start-september-11-2026)). Manufacturers must hold an SBOM and a cyber risk assessment for each product ([BearingPoint](https://bearingpoint.services/foss/en/newsblogs/eu-cyber-resilience-act-how-sboms-move-from-nice-to-have-to-must-have-by-2027/)). In a Bitkom survey of 1,003 companies only **29% knew what the law means for them** ([it-daily](https://www.it-daily.net/?p=186948)), and trade press says small firms risk falling behind while standards run late ([produktion.de](https://www.produktion.de/technik/cyber-resilience-act-warum-firmen-investieren-muessen/2637153), [cyberresilienceact.eu](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/news/cra-standardisation-deadlines-pushed-back-two-months.html)). The thing to sell is a starter file for one product: which class it falls in, an SBOM for its firmware and control software, a first risk assessment, a procedure for handling and reporting vulnerabilities, and a gap list, followed by a monthly monitoring subscription.

On your two tests alone this option scores highest. It needs no capital, no licence and no partner, the client signs the declaration, and the software half of the law (component inventories, vulnerability matching, update mechanisms) is the half that advisers from the machine-safety world find hard. The supply side leaves room: test houses and consultancies sell workshops and gap analyses priced on request, the visible Czech providers are TÜV SÜD, Deloitte and ESET ([TÜV SÜD](https://www.tuvsud.com/cs-cz/cinnosti/kyberneticka-bezpecnost/akt-o-kyberneticke-odolnosti), [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/cz-sk/cs/services/consulting/services/cyber-risk/cyber-resilience-act.html?icid=toggle_cz-sk_cs), [ESET](https://www.eset.com/cz/firmy/eset-private/advisory/)), and four searches found only two public prices anywhere: a EUR 1,470 seminar ([TÜV NORD](https://www.tuev-nord.de/de/weiterbildung/seminare/cra-workshop-cyber-resilience-act-erfolgreich-umsetzen)) and a USD 99-a-month tool for software repositories ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10045966/CRA-Ready/)). A published fixed price would itself stand out.

It ranks second because it is not physical work. It is consulting about physical products, done at a desk, and it only becomes more than hours if the monitoring subscription and your own extraction tooling take hold. Three risks are specific. Visible freelancers in this field carry security certificates and industrial references, and adverts ask for five years of industrial security experience ([freelancermap](https://www.freelancermap.de/profil/david-kleinz), [freelancermap](https://www.freelancermap.de/projekt/ot-security-and-ai-compliance-iec-62443-eu-ai-act-manufacturing-remote)). A startup has already raised about EUR 1.4 million to generate CRA documentation ([pulse.bot](https://www.pulse.bot/venture-capital/news/craci-raises-14m-for-eu-cybersecurity-compliance-platform-ce73ef96-5bbe-4e60-90c4-ee3571148feb/)). And Czech attention is still on the separate cybersecurity law that took effect on 1 November 2025, with the Chamber of Commerce only now warning firms about the CRA ([Advokátní deník](https://advokatnidenik.cz/2025/11/03/novy-zakon-o-kyberneticke-bezpecnosti-vstoupil-1-listopadu-v-ucinnost/), [BusinessInfo](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/hospodarska-komora-upozornuje-firmy-na-nove-povinnosti-podle-cyber-resilience-act/)), so many small makers will wait. Advisers are treated as answerable for their advice ([IBF Solutions](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/fachbeitraege/verantwortung-haftung-einfluss-von-funktion-position)); deliver drafts for the client's approval, cap liability in the contract and hold professional indemnity insurance before the first paid job.

### Third: signal-only camera inspection for parts sorted by hand

This is the most physical of the three and has the clearest budget to sell against. Czech sorting firms recruit inspectors continuously at CZK 150–180 an hour ([JenPráce](https://www.jenprace.cz/nabidka/3ggqg9/kontrolor-ka-kvality-novy-jicin-nastup-mozny-ihned-od-150-kc-hod-2930), [Pracomat](https://www.pracomat.cz/firma/03680134-enter-prise-sorting-s-r-o)), the supplier pays the bill ([Law Insider](https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/controlled-shipping)), and the global leader TRIGO turned over EUR 530 million in 2024 ([TRIGO](https://www.trigo-group.com/trigo-continues-its-strong-growth/)). A camera, a light and a small computer that only signal pass or fail have no drive system or moving parts, which is what the legal definition of machinery turns on ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/LexUriServ/LexUriServ.do?uri=OJ:L:2006:157:0024:0086:en:PDF)); no source states this for vision stations, so treat it as the researchers' reading. The moment the station drives a reject mechanism, whoever fits it takes CE responsibility for the assembly ([GT Engineering](https://www.gt-engineering.it/en/insights/machinery-directive/datoredilavoro-marcatura-ce/)) and the integrator problem returns.

It ranks third because the deeper look weakened it. Six searches in four languages found no price that any sorting firm charges, so the budget you would sell against is still a guess. TRIGO already owns an AI inspection kit whose revenue "more than tripled" in a year ([TRIGO](https://www.trigo-group.com/scortex/), [TRIGO](https://www.trigo-group.com/trigo-continues-its-strong-growth/)). Under the stricter form of containment the customer chooses the sorter, which shuts out an unlisted newcomer; only sorting that the supplier arranges itself is open. Self-contained AI cameras cost an estimated GBP 2,000–8,000 ([detectdefects](https://detectdefectsjekyll.pages.dev/blog/2026/02/23/keyence-vs-cognex-machine-vision-comparison)) and Brno's Sanezoo has raised about CZK 140 million with Bosch, Škoda and ZF as references ([CzechCrunch](https://cc.cz/uci-stroje-vnimat-pro-lidi-neviditelne-brnensky-startup-uz-pomohl-i-ve-skodovce-ted-chce-do-ameriky/)). The technology shortens the demo, not the acceptance: one method scores 96.6% at spotting defective parts from a single reference image on the standard benchmark, while leading methods stay below 60% at locating defects on a harder benchmark with transparent parts and shifting light ([AnomalyDINO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14529v3), [MVTec AD 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21622v1)), lighting causes more failures than models ([Edge AI and Vision Alliance](https://www.edge-ai-vision.com/2026/06/top-challenges-when-implementing-ai-inspection-and-how-to-outcome-them/)), and only 9% of firms saw most of their AI trials reach production ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/11/13/survey_ai_projects_failure_no_prepare/)). What you lack here, optics and illumination, takes months of hands-on trials that software skill does not shorten.

### Run the first two as one offer to one list

The first two options share a buyer. The researchers judged that product companies with their own firmware are better first customers for test stations than contract assemblers, because they write the test specification and carry the cost of field failures, and that the same kind of company is the easier first CRA client for a software person. Both are judgements, not findings, but they mean one call can test two offers. The sequence is: four weeks of demand testing now; a first station delivered at cost for a reference in December and January; CRA starter files sold to the same and neighbouring firms through 2027 as the deadline approaches; inspection added only if the sorting check below passes or a customer asks. Test stations supply the physical work and the customer lock-in, compliance files supply deadline pressure and recurring income.

|---|---|---|---|
| Integrator route (for reference) | Cobot cell at CZK 0.7–1.2 million ([Svět robotů](https://www.svet-robotu.cz/blogs/novinky/dostupny-paletizacni-robot-konec-driny-i-pro-male-firmy)) | n/a | Robots, large tickets; three outside dependencies, manufacturer liability, 6–24 months |

## 4. The open questions are about buyers, and search could not answer them

The research settled most questions about law, technology and competitors well enough to rank the options. It did not settle whether anyone will pay, and for the top three that is the only question left that matters. These trades quote privately, so search engines return wages and marketing pages, not invoices.

| Option | The question that decides it | What the research found | What would settle it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Test stations | Do small Czech and Slovak electronics makers pay outsiders to develop production tests, and how much? | No local supplier with a price; evidence of in-house and student-built testers; no statement from any assembler | Calls to the makers themselves; one quote each from a fixture-kit distributor and a Polish fixture house |
| CRA files | Will small makers pay now, before standards are final and before their customers demand evidence? | No observed quote, day rate or deal anywhere; no count of affected manufacturers | Offering a fixed price and counting orders |
| Inspection | What do sorting firms bill per hour or per part, how long do jobs run, and who chooses the method? | Wages only; one German manufacturer's internal costing rate; contract clauses | Two or three real quotes or invoices; filed accounts of Czech sorting firms; two conversations with regional sorting firms |

Four further unknowns apply across the three. No insurance premium for a Czech sole trader or company was found for any option. The safety rules for custom test equipment supplied to a factory were not researched. No documented case was found of a solo operator starting any of the three, so the timings are estimates. And a vendor source puts manufacturing sales cycles at 130–180 days ([Danish Lead Co](https://danishleadco.io/blog/7-outbound-tactics-for-manufacturers-with-12-month-cycles)), so four weeks will show intent and a first order at best, not a business.

## Conclusion

The dividing line among these 25 options is not hardware against software. It is where the error is caught and who signs. Options pass your tests when a mistake shows up on your own bench or desk and the customer remains the legal manufacturer of what leaves the factory; they fail when the mistake shows up on a customer's running machine or the signature is yours. That rule is worth keeping for any idea that arrives later.

Your two tests and your wish for physical work pull in opposite directions: the quicker and more solo an option is, the more it turns out to be software or advice, and the options with robots in them bring the partners back. Test stations are the point where the two meet, which is why they lead despite the smallest tickets. They also leave a road back toward automation: Czech trade press notes that in higher-end testers operators are replaced by robots and single-purpose machines ([DPS Elektronika](https://www.dps-az.cz/clanky/id:42798/testovani-pri-vyrobe-aneb-dft-3-dil)). That step would bring machinery rules back, but you would take it with paying customers, production data and a record of delivered equipment, which is the position the integrator route asked you to start without.

## The four-week test (9 November to 4 December 2026)

The goal is to settle the three demand questions with one person's time and little money, and to finish before the December slowdown. Nothing is built for a customer in this window. The same open-source circuit board serves as the demonstration piece for both the test station and the compliance file.

| Week | Actions | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (9–13 Nov) | Build a demonstration test station for an open-source board: firmware loading, power and communication checks, logged results, pass or fail, written with OpenHTF; film it. Write a complete CRA starter file for the same board. Publish one page with two fixed prices to test: a station at EUR 3,000–8,000 depending on complexity with the first at cost, and a CRA starter file at EUR 2,000–5,000; both are the researchers' untested hypotheses. Build three lists: 30 electronics makers, starting with the named Czech assemblers and product companies ([SAFIRAL](https://www.safiral.cz/ems), [ELO+](https://www.elo.cz/osazovani-dps), [ADDAT](https://www.addat.cz/zakazkova-vyroba-elektroniky), [ELITRONIC](https://www.elitronic.cz/), [ATM Elektro](https://www.atmelektro.cz/), [MARPOS](https://www.khkmsk.cz/clenove/marpos-s-r-o/), [Hokami CZ](https://a-leads.co/company/927674_0e7ec3cd), NAM system, RACOM) plus trade-fair exhibitor lists and subsidy records that mention testers; 60 makers of connected devices and machines in Czechia, Germany and Austria; 10 quality managers at automotive parts suppliers and 3 regional sorting firms. | Demo video, worked file, price page, three lists |
| 2–3 (16–27 Nov) | Call the 30 electronics makers. Ask how each product is tested today, who built the tester, what it cost, and which board enters production next; offer to send a draft test plan for that board within a day. Send the CRA offer to the 60 and ask for a 20-minute call. Request a quote for a sample board from a fixture-kit distributor ([Ingun](https://ingun.com/en/products/test-fixture-kits)) and a Polish fixture house ([Fitech](https://www.etesters.com/catalog/59690B12-DC20-E3E7-8BDE-AD2C85B97DEC/fitech-ltd)) to fix the price ceiling. For inspection, ask the quality managers for a recent sorting quote or invoice and how long the job ran; read the filed accounts of five Czech sorting firms in the public register to work out revenue per employee; ask two sorting firms whether they would trial a camera on a long job. Ask an insurance broker for a professional indemnity quote. | Call notes in one sheet; two fixture quotes; two or three sorting quotes; an insurance quote |
| 4 (30 Nov–4 Dec) | Ask for money. Offer the best electronics prospect a first station at cost against a written acceptance test. Ask the best CRA prospects to order a starter file. Ask one supplier for a free day photographing a part that is being sorted. Score the results against the table below and decide. | Orders or refusals; a written decision |

Budget, as planning caps and not researched prices: parts for the demonstration station (boards, probes, a simple manual fixture, a USB power supply and meter) EUR 900; price page and domain EUR 60; travel for six to eight visits EUR 350; documents, postage and phone EUR 150; contingency EUR 300. The total is about **EUR 1,750, roughly CZK 44,000**. Fixture-kit prices are not published, which is why a quote is part of the test. Inspection hardware is deliberately excluded; if that option passes, a bench rig built from the component prices found (camera EUR 450–1,629, lens EUR 250–900, a Jetson-class computer about USD 635) costs a few thousand euros ([Enao Vision](https://enaovision.com/blog/real-cost-machine-vision), [detectdefects](https://detectdefectsjekyll.pages.dev/blog/2026/03/20/machine-vision-system-cost-pricing-guide), [Waveshare](https://www.waveshare.com/product/ai/boards-kits.htm?mode=list&p=4)). Insurance is bound only before a first paid job.

| Option | Pass by 4 December | Fail |
|---|---|---|
| Test stations | Of 30 firms, at least five name a board due for production within six months that has no tester, at least three have paid or would pay an outsider for one, and at least one signs an order with a written acceptance test (at cost counts) | Fewer than three firms with a live need, or every answer is "in-house", "a student" or "our assembler does it" |
| CRA files | Of 60 contacted, at least eight take a call and at least two order a starter file at EUR 1,500 or more | No order and fewer than five calls, or the uniform answer is "we will wait for the standards" or "our suppliers' declarations are enough" |
| Inspection | Two or more real quotes or invoices in hand; at least two suppliers have a self-arranged sort that has run four weeks or keeps returning, with a monthly bill above the EUR 2,000–5,000 a trial would cost; one agrees to an image day | Jobs last days, the customer nominates the sorter, or nobody will let you photograph parts |

**Decision rule.** If test stations pass, that is the business: deliver the first station in December and January, and sell the compliance file to the same customers if it passed or drew calls. If only the compliance file passes, take the paid work for up to six months as a funded way in, call it what it is, and use those clients to repeat the test-station question in Germany and Austria. If only inspection passes, buy the bench rig and run one eight-week trial beside the manual check at one supplier, signal-only. If the electronics makers show need but no budget, spend two more weeks putting both offers to German and Austrian firms by video before dropping them, because the Czech list is short. If nothing passes, stop: the loss is four weeks and under EUR 2,000, and the next cheapest experiment is a pre-order page for a small wired tool sold to engineers, the parked product option. Where two options pass, the physical one leads.
