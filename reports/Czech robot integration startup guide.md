# Start narrow, partner early, verify everything

Start as a lean integrator that does one or two kit-based cobot applications (palletizing and CNC machine tending) for non-automotive small manufacturers in one Czech region, with projects of roughly CZK 0.7–3 million, and do not deliver a cell until an experienced electrical engineer and a machinery-safety consultant are contractually on your side. That size band is where Czech cobot-cell sellers already price and where the only live subsidy for a single robot cell tops out (50% of up to CZK 3 million, rural areas only). The opening is real but unproven: more than 60% of large Czech manufacturers use robots against 6–9% of small ones, and SMEs told the last robotization survey that they lack money and "anyone to consult", yet about 147 integrators already compete and the cobot distributors integrate in-house. The day you sign a declaration of conformity you are legally the machine's manufacturer, and every cell you will ever deliver falls under the new Machinery Regulation (from 20 January 2027) and the new Product Liability Directive (from 9 December 2026), which treats your software as a product. A software engineer reaches "can deliver a simple cell with partners covering electrical design and safety sign-off" in roughly 6–9 months, and independent delivery including CE documentation in 12–24 months. AI agents cut desk hours today (Structured Text PLC code, robot scripts tested in simulation, documentation, quoting), but engineering is only about 20–45% of a cell's price and commissioning stays physical, so an AI-first integrator wins on quote speed, fixed prices and pre-tested software for a standard cell family, not on a claim to automate integration. Integrator margins, compliance-consulting costs, insurance premiums, competitor sizes and one electrical-licensing question could not be sourced and must be confirmed with Czech professionals before money is committed.

**How far to trust the numbers.** All five research streams behind this report were unable to open full web pages and ran out of search budget, so every finding rests on search-result extracts rather than a full read of the source. Figures are therefore leads to verify, not facts to plan on; those that are most fragile are marked "verify" in the text, and many hardware prices are US reseller listings in USD rather than European quotes. Section 6 lists what must be confirmed with a Czech accountant, lawyer, insurer or safety consultant.

## 1. Two kit-based applications and CZK 0.7–3 million projects are the safest entry

### Palletizing and machine tending arrive as kits; welding and vision do not

Cobot palletizing and CNC machine tending are the two applications vendors have already turned into kits, which is why they suit a founder with little hardware experience: the kit supplies the frame, lift axis, sensors and application software, and the cobot's own controller handles motion and basic safety signals. A Robotiq palletizing kit for the UR20 lists at **USD 57,712 without the robot** at a US reseller ([Automation Distribution](https://automationdistribution.com/robotiq-palletizing-solution-pe-series-for-ur20-with-vacuum-gripper)), Vention palletizer frames at about USD 12,600–15,000 ([Vention](https://vention.io/designs/vertical-cobot-palletizer-ballscrew-84577)), a Robotiq machine-tending kit at USD 14,989–20,395 ([Automation Distribution](https://automationdistribution.com/robotiq-machine-tending-solution/)), and a complete Robotiq tending cell with a UR cobot at USD 74,399 ([Automation Distribution](https://automationdistribution.com/machine-tending-cell-mt-100-ur-cobot-robotiq-gripper-wago-control-package)). European marketplaces list complete cobot palletizers at about EUR 35,000–59,000 net ([Werktuigen](https://www.werktuigen.com/inno-way-m/wt-193-11671), [Machineseeker](https://www.machineseeker.ie/impuls+packaging-cobot+20kg+%2F+1200/i-21555163)). No EUR list prices for these kits were found, so get distributor quotes.

Palletizing standardizes best because boxes are regular and the same design repeats across customers. Machine tending is second: each job still needs part-specific gripper fingers, a way to present parts, and a signal interface to the particular CNC control, and Universal Robots itself warns that the more tightly the cobot is connected to the machine, the harder the job ([UR e-book](https://media.vanmeterinc.com/image/upload/v1588948929/marketing/blog/2020/UR_USA_Ebook_10steps_Cobots_final_B.pdf)). Welding is a poor first application: ABB, Fronius and Yaskawa already sell CE-marked turnkey cobot welding cells, and the process know-how sits with them ([ABB](https://new.abb.com/at/ueber-uns/geschaeftsfelder/robotik-und-fertigungsautomation/robotics/gofa-schweißzelle), [Fronius](https://www.fronius.com/de/schweisstechnik/produkt-informationen/welding-automation/cobot-welding)); Czech trade press gives buyers the same advice, to pick welding integrators for thermal-process expertise rather than robot programming ([Vše o průmyslu](https://www.vseoprumyslu.cz/robotizace/prumyslove-roboty/integrace-robotiky-a-automatizace-4-rady-pro-vyrobce.html)). Vision inspection is closer to your skills, but no kit pricing or evidence of easy entry was found; it tends to be bespoke per part.

The catch is that kits leave little money for the integrator. The kit vendor captures most of the engineering value, so what remains is resale margin plus installation, safety validation, training and support. Treat kit cells as the school and the reference-builder, not the long-term business.

### A first cell costs roughly three times the robot, and the Czech price is CZK 0.7–1.2 million

The common rule is that the robot is about a third of total system cost for a simple cell ([Automation World](https://www.automationworld.com/factory/robotics/blog/13315044/how-to-determine-robot-roi)); a Fraunhofer IPA analysis of its own projects puts typical industrial systems at **4–5 times the robot price** (verify date and sample) ([Fraunhofer Publica](https://publica-rest.fraunhofer.de/server/api/core/bitstreams/e67034b7-332b-4d87-aa2e-2bf26b3d84c7/content)). Vendor guides put a standard installed cobot application at USD 50,000–100,000, with integration and engineering at 50–100% of hardware cost on a first cell ([EVS](https://www.evsint.com/cobot-roi-calculator-sme-manufacturing/)). The Czech anchor is more useful: a Czech cobot-cell seller prices a palletizing cell (cobot, base, gripper, integration) at **CZK 700,000–1,200,000** and says a traditional caged cell easily costs CZK 2 million ([Svět robotů](https://www.svet-robotu.cz/blogs/novinky/dostupny-paletizacni-robot-konec-driny-i-pro-male-firmy)), and a grant consultancy says basic robotization starts just under CZK 1 million ([Grantex](https://grantex.cz/blog/kdy-se-vyplati-malym-a-strednim-podnikum-robotizovat-vyrobu/)).

Engineering effort per cell is quoted at 150–400 hours for a single cell and 200–800 hours for SME projects, at USD 125–250 per hour in US marketing guides ([GrabARobot](https://grabarobot.com/blog/robot-integration-cost-guide-2026/), [irecruit](https://www.irecruit.co/guides/robot-integration-cost-per-cell)). No Czech integrator billing rate was found. The nearest proxies are German freelance PLC and robot programmers asking EUR 70–110 per hour ([Freelancermap](https://www.freelancermap.at/freelancer-verzeichnis/sps-programmierung-38464)) and Czech robot-programmer job ads averaging about CZK 75,600 gross per month ([Jooble](https://cz.jooble.org/práce-programátor-robotů)).

**No reliable public margin benchmark for robot integrators exists.** The only figures found are a low-authority claim of 15–30% hardware resale margin ([Industrial Monitor Direct](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/plc-programming-cost-estimation-for-water-treatment-projects)) and members-only CSIA benchmarking ([Control Engineering](https://www.controleng.com/articles/great-engineering-includes-more-than-engineering/)). Combining the three-times rule with kit prices suggests the integrator's own value added on a kit-based cell is in the low tens of thousands of euros; this is an estimate derived from the cited figures, not a sourced number, and it means a two-person firm needs a steady flow of cells per year, not one or two, to live on kit work alone.

Quote payback conservatively. Vendors claim 6–18 months for cobots ([EVS](https://www.evsint.com/cobot-roi-calculator-sme-manufacturing/)), but those figures assume multi-shift use; the Czech grant consultancy says **2–4 years is common** ([Grantex](https://grantex.cz/blog/kdy-se-vyplati-malym-a-strednim-podnikum-robotizovat-vyrobu/)). One SME guide recommends showing a conservative case at 70% of projected benefits next to the base case ([EVS](https://www.evsint.com/cobot-roi-calculator-sme-manufacturing/)), and that is the honest default for a single-shift Czech shop.

### Contracts and cash sink new integrators more often than technology

The failures the sources describe are contractual. A typical turnkey payment schedule is 30% at signing, 50% at delivery, 10% at commissioning and 10% retained until 30 days of stable operation ([Industrial Monitor Direct](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/turnkey-automation-projects-contract-specifications-and-scope-management)); one US integrator takes 35% down ([CASI](https://www.casiusa.com/about/the-customer-sales-journey/)). On that schedule you have paid for robot and kit before the delivery payment arrives, and the last 20%, roughly your whole profit, depends on acceptance. The deposit must cover the hardware purchase.

Acceptance is where projects go wrong. In one documented case a factory acceptance test (FAT, the test at the integrator's site before shipping) covered only wiring and I/O, problems surfaced at the site acceptance test (SAT), and the contract's single week of SAT support turned into a string of change orders ([Control Design](https://www.controldesign.com/sensing/sensors/article/55366750/patrick-bunn-explains-why-functional-intent-is-critical-in-acceptance-testing)). Hazards found only at commissioning force late design changes and added guarding ([IBF Solutions](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/fachbeitraege/ce-kennzeichnung-kosten-reduzieren)). The countermeasures follow directly: a functional specification signed before design; acceptance criteria stated as numbers (cycle time, availability over a defined run, the part and packaging tolerances the cell is accepted against); a FAT that runs real customer parts; a stated number of on-site commissioning days with extra days billed; and a named in-house champion at the customer who learns the system ([IndustryWeek](https://industryweek.com/sponsored/article/22028362/how-to-make-your-first-robot-integration-a-success)). No failure-rate statistics exist; this evidence is anecdotal and mostly from US controls integration.

### First customers come through distributors, grant offices and one region

Small integrators that succeed are "lean integrators": they master **one or two applications in one regional market and often employ no more than 10 people** ([Automation World](https://automationworld.com/factory/robotics/article/21195925/cobots-and-the-rise-of-lean-integrators)). Robot makers certify integrators and route end customers to them, usually through the national distributor, because a distributor sells an arm without grippers, safety validation or programming ([Hesco](https://hesconet.com/blog/distributors-vs-system-integrators-in-industrial-automation/)). Universal Robots has kept adding small certified integrators in German-speaking countries to be close to small customers ([UR Germany](https://www.universal-robots.com/de/nachrichten-und-medien/news-center/universal-robots-erweitert-sein-vertriebsnetz-in-deutschland/)). How leads and discounts are actually shared is not public, so ask the distributor directly.

In Czechia three further channels fit a newcomer. First, the European Digital Innovation Hubs (EDIHs) give SMEs subsidised "test before invest" assessments, including design and testing of robotic concepts ([EDIH CTU](https://www.edihctu.eu/sluzby/)); becoming the implementation partner those assessments refer to is a low-cost lead source. Second, the local action groups (MAS) and grant consultants who administer the rural technology grant see every small firm that wants a subsidised robot (section 4). Third, the standard proof format in this market is a trade-press case study, such as the Stamit story of a UR10 tending machine tools ([MM Průmyslové spektrum](https://www.mmspektrum.com/clanek/kolaborativni-roboty-u-obrabecich-stroju)); a first reference written up there is a concrete early marketing goal. Aim first at non-automotive metalworking, plastics and food shops: automotive suppliers are cutting investment (section 4).

A defensible alternative to selling your own cells in year one is to sell programming and commissioning hours to existing integrators while you learn. Czech job ads do seek robot programmers for long-term contractor cooperation ([Jooble](https://cz.jooble.org/práce-programátor-robotů)), and at least one Czech firm sells contract robot programming across brands ([Ferrotherm](https://www.ferrotherm.cz/robot-programming-and-robotics/?lang=en)). No source documents this as an entry route, but it buys commissioning experience, which is the skill you most lack, and it carries no manufacturer liability.

### The minimum team is you plus three borrowed competences

A robot cell needs mechanical design, electrical and control-cabinet design, PLC and robot programming, safety engineering and commissioning. A kit-based cell removes most mechanical and electrical design; what stays in-house is application setup, gripper adaptation, the interface to the customer's machine, risk assessment, validation, documentation and training. The three competences to borrow from the start are these. An experienced controls and commissioning engineer, hired or partnered, because commissioning cannot be learned from a desk. A person holding the Czech electrical qualification the trade licence requires (section 4), which takes two to four years of documented practice to earn. And a machinery-safety consultant for the first cells: CE work is sold as fixed-scope packages by engineering offices ([Unchained Robotics](https://unchainedrobotics.de/en/products/service/ingenieurbüro-köckemann-ce-package-robot-up-to-80-kg-in-cell-with-conveyor)), though no prices were found. Buying cabinets from panel shops and fabricated parts from machine shops is common practice, but no source confirmed it or its cost.

## 2. Signing the declaration of conformity makes you the manufacturer

### The integrator, not the robot maker, CE-marks the cell

A robot is "partly completed machinery". It ships with a declaration of incorporation, not a CE mark under the machinery rules, and Universal Robots' own document says that when it is integrated into a complete machine "the integrator is responsible for the completed machine fulfilling all applicable Directives, applying the CE mark and providing the Declaration of Conformity" ([UR Declaration of Incorporation](https://universal-robots.com/download/certifications-and-declarations-e-series/declaration-of-incorporation-e-series)). Whoever builds the assembly, "or has it built", is the manufacturer ([Pilz](https://www.pilz.com/en-INT/support/faq/standards/articles/167659)). You therefore do the risk assessment, compile the technical file, write the instructions, sign the declaration and keep the documentation for at least ten years ([Computer & Automation](https://www.computer-automation.de/safety-und-security/acht-schritte-von-der-risikobeurteilung-bis-zur-ce-kennzeichnung.htm)). In Czechia the declaration and the instructions must be in Czech ([BOZPprofi](https://www.bozpprofi.cz/33/pruvodni-dokumentace-strojnich-zarizeni-uniqueidmRRWSbk196FNf8-jVUh4Egny2sdjJ1sRjWiM1G86sLqw9PBWuC7BmQ/)). A customer who buys a cobot and a kit and assembles them becomes the manufacturer itself, which is both a sales argument for you and a reason to state in every contract who signs.

### Every cell you deliver falls under the 2027 Regulation

Directive 2006/42/EC, transposed in Czechia as government regulation NV 176/2008 Sb., applies until 19 January 2027. **Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 applies from 20 January 2027 with no transition period** for newly placed machinery, and Czech Act 236/2024 Sb. repeals NV 176/2008 on that date ([EU-OSHA](https://osha.europa.eu/it/legislation/directive/regulation-20231230eu-machinery), [e-Pravo](https://www.epravo.cz/top/zakony/sbirka-zakonu/sb/2024/236)). Since that is ten weeks away, design and document to the Regulation from the first project.

Three points in the Regulation matter to a software-led integrator. Self-assessment stays the general rule, so a standard cell with conventional safety functions needs no notified body; third-party assessment is mandatory only for six high-risk categories ([Taylor Wessing](https://taylorwessing.com/-/media/taylor-wessing/files/germany/2025/tw24_eu_machinery_regulation.pdf)). One of those six is **safety functions that rely on machine learning**, so AI used for perception or path planning is unaffected, but AI vision that replaces a safety scanner puts the cell under a notified body ([UL Solutions](https://www.ul.com/resources/navigating-new-eu-machinery-regulation-eu-20231230)). Keep AI out of the safety chain and use certified safety devices. Finally, a "substantial modification" not foreseen by the manufacturer, including a software change, makes whoever performs it a manufacturer ([Fabrico](https://www.fabrico.io/blog/eu-machinery-regulation-2023-1230/)); if you sell remote updates, define in advance which changes are foreseen and documented. These readings come from law-firm and vendor summaries, not the Regulation's text, and need a safety consultant's confirmation.

The technical benchmark is ISO 10218-2:2025, the part of the revised robot-safety standard addressed to integrators, which now incorporates the collaborative-application requirements formerly in ISO/TS 15066 ([A3](https://www.automate.org/robotics/blogs/updated-iso-10218-faq)). One vendor source says the 2025 editions were listed in the Official Journal on 7 September 2026 and so carry presumption of conformity (verify) ([IBF Solutions](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/en/seminars-and-news/news/new-standards-for-industrial-robots-en-iso-10218-1-and-2)). "Collaborative" does not mean exempt: power-and-force-limited applications need contact forces and pressures measured, with equipment that is rented ([Automation Magazine](https://www.automationmagazine.co.uk/force-and-pressure-measurement-system-checks-cobot-safety/)) or bought, one measurement item being listed at EUR 5,811 plus VAT (scope of that price not verified) ([Unchained Robotics](https://unchainedrobotics.de/en/products/safety/cobosafe?limit=all)). Fenced or scanner-protected cells avoid that cost.

### Liability widens on 9 December 2026, and software becomes a product

The revised Product Liability Directive (EU) 2024/2853 applies to products placed on the market after **9 December 2026** ([Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2024/11/navigating-the-new-eu-product-liability-directive)). It treats software, including AI systems, as a product ([Keystone Law](https://keystonelaw.com/keynotes/how-will-the-new-product-liability-directive-eu-2024-2853-impact-businesses/)), liability is strict ([Zurich](https://www.nordic.zurich.com/en/news-and-risk-insights/articles/product-liability-directive)), and a law-firm note lists system integrators among those exposed ([Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/zh-hant/insights-and-events/insights/2026/05/new-eu-product-liability-directive-in-logistics-plant-engineering)). An integrator whose value is its software carries more exposure than under the old rules. Strict liability to injured people cannot be contracted away; liability to the customer can be limited, and the standard European tool is the Orgalim S 2022 model contract, which addresses defect liability and consequential loss ([VDMA](https://www.vdmashop.de/Orgalim-General-Condition-S-2022-Guide-on-their-use-and-interpretation/orgalime.112125)), with warranties of 12–24 months in practice ([Kolmeks](https://kolmeks.com/en/company/terms-of-delivery-and-warranty-conditions/)). Have a Czech lawyer adapt such terms; the default cap and warranty period in Orgalim were not confirmed from the text.

### Insurance must cover product, planning error and pure financial loss

A German case shows the trap: an insurer first refused a claim over a machine-specification error, and planning-error damages were partly excluded ([Leinemann Partner](https://leinemann-partner.de/en/credentials/how-is-your-insurance-cover)). You need product liability and cover for design errors, and you must check three things in the wording: defective product and defective work after handover, "pure financial loss" (the customer's lost production) with its sub-limit, and territorial scope if you install abroad. Czech insurers sell these as extensions to business liability; ČPP advertises limits up to CZK 100 million ([ČPP](https://www.cpp.cz/pojisteni-podnikatelu/pojisteni-podnikatelu-komplex)), ČSOB caps pure financial loss at CZK 30 million at most ([ČSOB](https://www.csob.cz/documents/10710/1599957/vpp-pojisteni-odpovednosti.pdf)), and commentary suggests CZK 5–20 million limits for a small company ([Total Brokers](https://totalbrokers.cz/cs/magazin/pojisteni-odpovednosti-pro-firmy-online/)). **No premium for a machine builder or integrator was found anywhere**; a broker quote is the only way to get one. Incorporate before the first contract so that liability sits in the company.

## 3. Six to nine months to a supervised first cell

### Universal Robots is the cheapest ecosystem to learn and has a Czech branch

Universal Robots (UR) is the practical first brand. Its e-learning is free ([UR Academy](https://academy.universal-robots.com/free-e-learning/e-series-e-learning/e-series-core-track)), its simulator URSim runs free in Docker ([ROS docs](https://docs.ros.org/en/jazzy/p/ur_robot_driver/installation/ursim_docker.html)), it maintains its own ROS 2 driver for the whole range ([ROS docs](https://docs.ros.org/en/jazzy/p/ur_robot_driver)), and its Czech branch runs the two-day Core Training in Prague and, with Mendel University, in Brno. A Prague listing showed **EUR 250 plus VAT**, far below the USD 1,000–1,200 charged in the US, so confirm the price ([UR Academy Czech Republic](https://academy.universal-robots.com/czech-republic/our-training-courses/20250127-core-en-jne), [Air Hydro Power](https://www.airhydropower.com/training/01-universal-robots.html)). The trade-off is hardware price; low-cost Chinese cobots sell new at about EUR 15,000–17,000 (Dobot CR5A EUR 15,252, JAKA Zu 5 EUR 15,900) ([Machineseeker](https://www.machineseeker.cn/dobot-cr5a/i-21201705), [eBay.de](https://www.ebay.de/itm/134745794819)) and are easy to script through Python and ROS 2 SDKs ([Welectron](https://www.welectron.com/Fairino-FR5-Robot-Arm)), which makes one of them a sensible second brand for price-sensitive jobs once distributor support is checked.

Partner programs are relationships, not purchases. UR's Certified Systems Integrator status brings training, technical resources and priority support, and one UK firm said it was only the third in its country to qualify ([Labman](https://labmanautomation.com/news/certified-systems-integrator-ur/)); the entry criteria, discounts and tier structure sit behind UR's partner portal ([UR Partner Resource Center](https://www.universal-robots.com/landing-pages/partner/partner-resource-center/)). The realistic sequence is vendor training, one or two reference cells, then certification. FANUC, ABB and KUKA run "apply, get assessed, get trained" schemes with no public criteria ([Patti Engineering](https://www.pattiengineering.com/blog/what-is-a-fanuc-authorized-system-integrator/), [ABB](https://www.abb.com/global/en/areas/robotics/channel-partners)); they are a second step, usually after buying robots and paid training.

### A real UR costs about EUR 10,000 used; a hobby arm teaches less than it seems

Used UR arms of the previous CB3 generation ask roughly **EUR 9,900–16,200** for a UR5, depending on age, and about EUR 10,000–10,900 for a UR3, while recent UR10e units ask EUR 22,000–27,500 ([Exapro](https://www.exapro.com/universal-robots-ur5-p260724043), [Machineseeker](https://www.machineseeker.ie/universal+robots-ur5+%282021%29/i-21202455), [Machineseeker](https://www.machineseeker.com/universal+robots-ur3+cb3/i-16350327), [Machineseeker](https://www.machineseeker.ie/universal+robots-ur10e/i-21450472)). These are asking prices from a small sample, without checks on warranty or wear. A used CB3 runs the real controller software, safety configuration and fieldbus interfaces, which is what integration work requires. Hobby arms such as the myCobot 280 at EUR 735 teach kinematics, vision and Python control but nothing about industrial controllers ([Welectron](https://www.welectron.com/Elephant-Robotics-myCobot-280-Robot-Arm-M5-Stack)). A lab under about EUR 2,000 is therefore a hobby arm, a Raspberry Pi with CODESYS, a Siemens S7-1200 G2, Factory I/O and a small 24 V bench of buttons, a sensor, a contactor and an emergency stop, with everything else in simulation. A lab near EUR 10,000–12,000 swaps the hobby arm for a used UR. A new Fairino cobot appears on one Polish listing at EUR 4,200 plus VAT, but the model is unclear, so do not budget on it ([Machineseeker](https://www.machineseeker.ie/fairino+cobot-fr5+fr10+fr20+fr30/i-22042388)). A Robotiq 2F-85 gripper at about EUR 4,900 is too expensive for either lab ([RS Online](https://it.rs-online.com/web/p/pinze-robotiche/0136163)); use a vacuum cup or a cheap gripper.

### Competence arrives in stages, and electrical work is the bottleneck

Training-vendor sources say a software developer gets PLC logic in 1–2 months and becomes useful in 3–6 ([plcprogramming.io](https://plcprogramming.io/blog/learn-plc-programming-complete-roadmap)), with 6–12 months to basic proficiency and 3–5 years to professional competence across platforms ([Industrial Monitor Direct](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/plc-engineer-career-path-skills-training-and-platform-guide)). A first simple cobot cell takes 4–12 weeks of project time, and the delay is "almost always on the process-engineering side" ([KGT Solutions](https://kgt.solutions/resources/blog/cobots-in-manufacturing-plant-manager-guide), [Productiv](https://getproductiv.com/blog/rise-of-cobots-what-new-automation-means-for-labor-intensive-operations)). Synthesizing these ranges gives **6–9 months to deliver a simple, low-risk cell with a partner covering electrical design and safety sign-off, and 12–24 months to independent delivery including CE documentation**. That is a synthesis, not a measured figure; no first-hand accounts of software engineers making this move were retrieved. The binding constraints are electrical design, mechanical fixturing and safety validation, and a panel builder and a mechanical designer shorten the path more than extra study does.

## 4. Czechia rewards small, grant-sized, non-automotive projects

### A manufacturing-heavy economy with a robot gap between large and small firms

Manufacturing produced **20% of Czech GDP in 2024** ([World Bank via Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/czech-republic/manufacturing-value-added-percent-of-gdp-wb-data.html)) and 30.9% of business-economy employment in 2023, the highest share in the EU ([Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Businesses_in_the_manufacturing_sector)). Automotive dominates: about 1.45 million cars were built in 2025 and cars are close to 20% of goods exports ([Central European Times](https://centraleuropeantimes.com/the-price-of-an-industrial-economy/)). By share of residents working in industry, the densest regions are Zlín, Vysočina and Pardubice at roughly 35–37%, with Liberec a long-term leader as well (the table extract was misaligned, verify) ([ČSÚ](https://www.czso.cz/documents/10180/189468424/33010123q4e02.pdf), [ČSÚ analysis](https://www.czso.cz/documents/10180/151683470/320329-20a.pdf)). A count of manufacturing SMEs by size band was not retrieved.

Robot density reached **216 per 10,000 manufacturing employees in 2024**, up 82% since 2017 and above the world average of 132, but below Western Europe's 267 ([BusinessInfo](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/cesko-patri-v-robotizaci-k-rychleji-rostoucim-zemim-spicka-mu-presto-unika/), [IFR](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/robot-density-surges-in-europe-asia-and-americas)). Czechia installed 2,298 robots in 2025 ([Systémy logistiky](https://www.systemylogistiky.cz/2026/09/29/ve-svetovych-tovarnach-pracuje-rekordnich-pet-milionu-prumyslovych-robotu/)), and an older article put 58% of installations in automotive ([Vše o průmyslu](https://www.vseoprumyslu.cz/inspirace/trendy/kvety-robotizace.html)). The clearest evidence of an under-served segment is the size gap: in 2020 **more than 60% of large manufacturers used industrial robots against 9% of small ones** ([BusinessInfo](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/robotu-by-se-ceskym-firmam-hodilo-dvakrat-vic-zjistil-pruzkum/)), and a 2022 Ipsos survey of 200 SMEs found 13% using industrial robots and 5% using cobots, with potential judged at about double current use ([AMSP/Ipsos](https://amsp.cz/media/amsp/3821/ipsos-pro-amsprobotizace-v-mspfinal.pdf)). The same survey named upfront cost and financing as the top barrier, and the association's commentary added that SMEs lack orientation and have no one to consult ([AMSP](https://www.amsp.cz/media/amsp/3823/ipsos-pro-amspautomatizace-52022-kom-amsp-cr.pdf)). No robotization-specific survey newer than 2022 was found.

### Sell on wage growth and skills mismatch, not on "no people"

The labour market has loosened: unemployment was 5.0% in September 2026 against 4.5% a year earlier, with 104,359 vacancies ([ČTK](https://www.ceskenoviny.cz/zpravy/nezamestnanost-v-cesku-zustala-v-zari-na-peti-procentech/2884375)). What persists is wage growth and mismatch. The average wage rose 6.4% nominally to CZK 51,966 in Q2 2026 ([ČSÚ](https://csu.gov.cz/rychle-informace/prumerne-mzdy-2-ctvrtleti-2026)), and specialised engineering and production trades show **0.1–0.3 applicants per vacancy** ([Labour Office](https://up.gov.cz/cms/documents/dd7ee2a6-e540-3384-6b3c-0c8b29fba013/SCK_TZ_nezamestnanost_ČERVEN_26_fin.pdf)). Foreign employees grew by 233,000 over 2021–2025 ([ČNB](https://www.cnb.cz/cs/o_cnb/cnblog/Zamestnanci-z-ciziny-stale-vyznamnejsi-prvek-na-tuzemskem-trhu-prace/)), and only 12% of employers are preparing for Ukrainian workers leaving after the war ([Newstream](https://www.newstream.cz/zpravy-z-firem/bez-ukrajincu-by-firmy-hledaly-nahradu-tezko-na-jejich-odchod-se-chysta-jen-zlomek-zamestnavatelu)). Temper expectations: in the Labour Office's 2026 employer survey only a minority, about 21% on one reading of the extract, expect automation to solve their staffing gaps ([MPSV](https://data.mpsv.cz/portal/assets/UP_Predikce_a_realita_pracovniho_trhu-2026-Dny6INnR.pdf)).

Automotive suppliers are a slow sale in 2026. Czech car output is growing, up 4.4% in the first half ([AutoSAP](https://autosap.cz/en/aktualita/pres-tri-ctvrte-milionu-vyrobenych-vozidel-v-prvnim-pololeti-cesky-autoprumysl-drzi-silnou-vykonnost/)), but large German suppliers are cutting plants and jobs and some Czech suppliers are reconsidering investments ([e15](https://www.e15.cz/byznys/prumysl-a-energetika/nemecti-dodavatele-v-hluboke-krizi-rusi-desetitisice-mist-a-desitky-zavodu-cesti-partneri-v-obavach-1426616), [BusinessInfo](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/cesky-autoland-brzdi-dodavatele-automobilek-zacinaji-propoustet-cekaji-ze-bude-jeste-hur/)). European automotive robot installations fell 25% in 2025 ([Tiscali](https://www.tiscali.cz/v-tovarnach-uz-pracuje-5-milionu-robotu-cina-ovladla-vic-nez-polovinu-trhu-evropa-ztraci-tempo-a-instalaci-ubyva-779860)), so automotive-dependent integrators have spare capacity and will move down-market. Against that, firms in Czechia report better investment intentions than firms in Germany, with an investment balance of +11 points against −8 ([GTAI on the AHK survey](https://www.gtai.de/de/trade/tschechische-republik/wirtschaftsumfeld/stimmung-unter-investoren-truebt-sich-ein-1760090)).

### About 147 integrators compete, and the distributors integrate too

A 2023 census counted **235 robot and automation suppliers in Czechia, including 147 integrators and 22 distributors**, with FANUC, KUKA and ABB the most-used brands, and 64 integrators in Slovakia ([HowToRobot Czechia](https://howtorobot.com/expert-insight/robot-and-automation-companies-czech-republic-0), [HowToRobot Slovakia](https://howtorobot.com/expert-insight/robot-and-automation-companies-slovakia)). With about 2,300 robots installed a year, the market is already long-tail; a newcomer differentiates by application, sector, region and project size. Turnover and headcount could not be retrieved for any competitor, and nothing was found on integrators' minimum project sizes or lead times, so **the "too small for established integrators" gap remains a plausible inference, not a demonstrated fact**.

The players closest to small shops are the cobot channel partners, most of which sell and integrate. Exactec in Liberec describes itself as UR's authorized distributor since 2010 ([Živéfirmy](https://www.zivefirmy.cz/exactec_f420967?cz=898)). AMTECH in Brno, which says it delivered over 500 UR robots and more than half of all UR units in Czechia and Slovakia, has announced the end of its UR distribution (date not shown) ([AMTECH](https://amtech.cz/ukonceni-distributorske-spoluprace-s-universal-robots/)), which suggests UR's Czech channel is being rebuilt. Roboticall is the exclusive Doosan distributor for both countries ([Roboticall](https://roboticall.cz/)), Controltech distributes Dobot ([Dobot CZ](https://www.dobotmagician.cz/cr.htm)), and Fairino Europe s.r.o. in Olomouc runs a "Buy & Try" programme ([Fairino](https://www.fairino.info/cz/buy-try-program)). Small process specialists include ARC-Robotics for FANUC-based machine tending and palletizing ([ARC-Robotics](https://www.arc-robotics.cz/)) and welding firms such as KSK and Goldmax ([KSK](https://kskct.cz/cs/robotizace), [Goldmax](https://goldmaxservices.cz/roboticke-svarovani-a-automatizace/)). Distributors are therefore supplier and competitor at once; the workable relationship is as the partner who takes the small, regional or process-specific jobs they do not want. Single-distributor brands and price-led entrants are likelier to welcome a new integrator than the established UR channel. The most relevant unread source is a 2025 feasibility study for the Danish Embassy in Prague on Czech manufacturers' automation barriers ([HowToRobot](https://howtorobot.com/expert-insight/danish-robotics-technology-finds-eu31-55m-export-opportunity-czech-manufacturing)); read it first.

### MSV Brno in October is the main fair; the hubs and trade press do the rest

MSV Brno drew 1,356 exhibitors and 55,160 visitors in 2025 ([Hospodářské noviny](https://img.ihned.cz/attachment.php/160/80851160/swGuChUqdlpyzN9tP26QrK0J3obIESBD/HN_250929_Strojirensky_veletrh.pdf)) and hosts the "Kontakt-Kontrakt" matchmaking event ([b2match](https://www.b2match.com/e/contact-contract-msv-2026/components/70118/8UOyYFE3Y7MU)); the 2026 edition ran 6–9 October, so the next one is eleven months off. Amper in March had 468 exhibitors and over 27,000 visitors in 2026, but only 7% of visitors named automation and robotics as their interest ([Amper report](https://www.bvv.cz/veletrhy/amper/2026/pdf/ZZ_Amper_26.pdf)). For a small integrator, matchmaking and a place on a distributor's stand beat an own stand. The institutional channels are the SME association AMSP ČR, the Confederation of Industry, the National Centre for Industry 4.0 and the testbeds at the Czech Technical University in Prague and in Brno ([CIIRC](https://www.ciirc.cvut.cz/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/CIIRC-10_VMarik_FINAL.pdf), [CEITEC](https://www.ceitec.eu/testbed-brno-for-industry-4-0-half-a-billion-crown-project-will-help-smaller-and-medium-sized-companies/t11022)). The main trade monthly, MM Průmyslové spektrum, reports about 22,000 readers per issue ([MM media data](https://www.mmspektrum.com:443/download/scv4pQ/mediadata_mmps_2024.pdf?locale=cs)).

### One live subsidy fits a single cell; loans are suspended

No national call aimed at production robotization is open in November 2026. The large "Technologie 4.0" call closed in early 2024 after 297 applications sought CZK 3.49 billion ([OP TAK](https://optak.gov.cz/digitalni-podnik-technologie-4-0-vyzva-i-op-tak/a-142/)), and the 2025–26 digital-technology call penalised production technology by up to 30 points ([eNovation](https://www.enovation.cz/aktuality/jak-uspet-ve-vyzve-digitalni-podnik-digitalni-technologie)). The live route is **"Technologie pro MAS (CLLD) – výzva II"**: a 50% grant on eligible costs of CZK 250,000 to 2,999,999, for SMEs in the territory of a local action group, excluding Prague and municipalities above 25,000 inhabitants, with national intake from 1 September 2026 to 1 September 2027 ([API](https://apiagentura.gov.cz/vyhlaseni-vyzvy-ii-technologie-pro-mas-clld/), [Manta IT](https://www.mantait.cz/clanky/dotace-technologie-pro-mas-2026)). Each local group runs its own short pre-selection round with a small budget, for example CZK 4.65 million at MAS Stolové hory ([MAS Stolové hory](https://www.mas-stolovehory.cz/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/OP-TAK_Vyzva-Mistni-akcni-skupiny-Stolove-hory-1.pdf)), and at least one requires two years of company history from the applicant ([BusinessInfo](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/male-a-stredni-podniky-na-venkove-mohou-zadat-o-dotace-na-digitalizaci-a-robotizaci-z-op-tak/)). A consultancy calls this the last big round ([Manta IT](https://www.mantait.cz/clanky/dotace-technologie-pro-mas-2026)). A cell priced just under CZK 3 million of eligible cost fits this instrument, and that ceiling sits below the usual project size of established line builders. Tell customers not to plan around a hoped-for new call.

On financing, the National Development Bank's zero-interest Expanze loans (CZK 1–100 million, up to 50% of eligible costs) were **suspended from 10 August 2026** when the call was fully subscribed ([BusinessInfo](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/rozvojova-banka-pozastavila-prijem-zadosti-o-zvyhodnene-uvery-expanze/)). A new portfolio guarantee, Garant InvestEU, covers up to 70% of loan principal through the customer's own bank and is planned to reach borrowers in Q4 2026 ([NRB](https://www.nrb.cz/produkt/zaruka-invest-eu/)). Otherwise customers use bank loans and leasing. Robot rental exists but is thin: one ABB partner advertises rental from CZK 29,500 per month on a 60-month contract (page date unclear) ([SMAR](https://erp.smar.cz/en_US/page/operativni-leasing)), and one article says Czech firms still do not offer robots-as-a-service to a significant extent ([Innovation Anywhere](https://innovationanywhere.cz/robot-as-a-service/)). Offering rental through a leasing partner is an opening, but residual-value risk would sit with you.

### Support for the startup itself favours R&D, not services

A pure services integrator fits few programmes. CzechInvest's Technology Incubation held its seventh call until 12 January 2026 and is near its 250-firm target, so do not count on a new one ([BusinessInfo](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/czechinvest-otevira-sedmou-vyzvu-technologicke-inkubace/), [ČTK](https://www.ceskenoviny.cz/tiskove/zpravy/projekt-technologicka-inkubace-vrcholi-brzy-dosahne-sveho-cile-podporit-250-inovativnich-firem-z-celeho-ceska/2633318)). If you develop your own technology, such as a reusable cell platform or an engineering harness, three tools apply. The Technology Agency's TREND "Nováčci" competition targets firms new to R&D ([TA ČR](https://tacr.gov.cz/soutez/program-trend/)). Its SIGMA DC1 competition is listed by one aggregator as open from 3 December 2026 to 27 January 2027, for SME-led projects up to CZK 15 million at up to 70% intensity (verify) ([dotacni.info](https://www.dotacni.info/tacr-program-sigma-20-verejna-soutez-dilci-cil-1-z-vyzkumu-do-byznysu/)). And from 1 January 2026 the R&D tax deduction is **150% of eligible costs up to CZK 50 million** ([Ministry of Finance](https://mf.gov.cz/cs/ministerstvo/media/tiskove-zpravy/2026/prehledne-ktere-zmeny-pripravilo-ministerstvo-fina-62343)), though courts demand stricter evidence than the statute suggests ([dReport](https://www.dreport.cz/blog/novela-odpoctu-na-vyzkum-a-vyvoj-v-kontextu-aktualni-judikatury/)). EDIH CTU offers most of its services with up to 85% financial support ([EDIH CTU](https://www.edihctu.eu/2026/01/20/edih-ctu-2-0-zahajen-rok-2026-se-stava-rokem-ai/)), and Innovation Vouchers call IV pays for testing, measurement and certification services, with intake to 30 April 2027 on one source and 30 November 2026 on another ([API](https://apiagentura.gov.cz/aktualizace-navyseni-alokace-pro-vyzvu-iv-inovacni-vouchery/)).

### An s.r.o. is quick; the electrical qualification is the gating item

A limited company (s.r.o.) can be founded with CZK 1 of capital in about one to three weeks for roughly CZK 5,500–11,000 all-in, with a notarial deed still required ([Notary Fiánová](https://www.notarfianova.cz/zalozeni-sro/), [Firma pro vás](https://firmaprovas.cz/cenik/)). The harder part is electrical. The sources place two activities among the craft trades of the Trade Licensing Act: production, installation and repair of electrical machines and devices, and installation, repair, inspection and testing of electrical equipment ([Firmin](https://www.firmin.cz/zivnosti/vyroba-instalace-opravy-elektro), [Firmin](https://www.firmin.cz/zivnosti/montaz-opravy-revize-elektro)). A craft trade needs a responsible representative with proven qualification, such as relevant education or six years of practice ([odpovednyzastupce.cz](https://www.odpovednyzastupce.cz/montaze-opravy-revize-a-zkousky-elektrickych-zarizeni/)).

Three layers must be kept apart: the trade licence with its responsible representative; a company-level authorisation from the technical inspection body TIČR for work on "reserved electrical equipment" under Act 250/2021 Sb. ([TIČR](https://www.ticr.eu/soubor/ez-informace-ticr-2024-pdf/)); and each worker's personal competence under government regulation NV 194/2022 Sb., which requires electrotechnical education and, for the "leading electrician" level of section 7, about two to four years of practice depending on scope ([ceskezakony.cz](https://ceskezakony.cz/zakon/194-2022), [odbornazpusobilost.cz](https://odbornazpusobilost.cz/zkouska-odborne-zpusobilosti-v-elektrotechnice-dle-nv-c-194-2022-sb/)). A founder without that background cannot self-qualify quickly and must employ or contract such a person. **Whether building a machine's own control cabinet in a workshop counts as work on reserved electrical equipment, as opposed to connecting the machine to the plant's installation, is not answered by any source found**; confirm it with TIČR or an inspection technician before you wire anything for a customer. Which trade covers PLC programming and machine building was also not confirmed from the statute.

### Register for VAT at once and use contractors carefully

VAT registration becomes mandatory after turnover passes CZK 2,000,000 in a calendar year, or immediately above CZK 2,536,500 ([Dokvero](https://dokvero.cz/navody/limit-obratu-dph-2026)); an integrator buying robots will register voluntarily at formation to recover input VAT. Goods supplied with installation are taxed where they are installed, so a cell installed in another EU state carries no Czech VAT and is usually reverse-charged to a VAT-registered customer ([Money.cz](https://money.cz/novinky-a-tipy/dane/dodani-zbozi-s-montazi-ci-instalaci-z-pohledu-dph/)). Using sole-trader contractors in an employee-like way (the "švarcsystém") risks fines of CZK 50,000 to 10 million ([AK Šmarda](https://www.aksmarda.cz/rady-a-tipy/kontrola-svarcsystemu-pokuty-prubeh-obrana/)), and one source says a new definition of dependent work applies from 1 December 2026, though sources disagree on its content ([dokladbot](https://dokladbot.cz/blog/svarcsystem-znaky-sankce-jak-se-vyhnout)). For hiring, 80% of Czech PLC programmers earn CZK 46,800–88,800 gross per month ([Platy.cz](https://www.platy.cz/en/salaryinfo/electrical-power-engineering/plc-programmer)). The corporate tax rate, employer contribution rates and depreciation rules were not confirmed and belong to your accountant.

### Slovakia first, then Czech plants' German and Austrian parents

Hourly labour cost in 2025 was **EUR 19.8 in Czechia and Slovakia, EUR 19.1 in Poland and EUR 46.3 in Austria** ([Eurostat via Symsite](https://www.symsite.sk/single-post/slovak-labor-costs-are-11th-lowest-in-the-eu)). Slovakia is the lowest-friction export market: several distributors treat the two countries as one territory, and robot density is similar at 210 ([Visual Capitalist](https://www.visualcapitalist.com/ranked-countries-most-industrial-robots-per-worker-2024-v2/)), but there is no price advantage, so you sell on capability. Germany and Austria offer the price advantage but weak investment appetite, with only 23% of firms in Germany planning higher investment in 2026 ([GTAI](https://www.gtai.de/de/trade/tschechische-republik/wirtschaftsumfeld/stimmung-unter-investoren-truebt-sich-ein-1760090)); the realistic entry is through the German or Austrian parents of Czech plants you already serve, or by selling engineering hours to German integrators. Poland installs more robots than Czechia but has its own distributors and equal labour costs, so a Czech integrator has no obvious edge there.

Posting your employees abroad requires an A1 social-security certificate for each worker and compliance with host-country minimum pay ([Labour Office guide](https://up.gov.cz/cms/documents/f5d30a1b-344e-e6ef-833e-f283683bdb35/Poskytovani_preshranicnich_sluzeb_vysilani_pracovniku.pdf)). Germany requires advance online notification through its minimum-wage portal only for listed sectors ([HWK Niederbayern-Oberpfalz](https://www.hwkno.de/downloads/cz-vykonavani-remeslnickych-sluzeb-v-nemecku-76,18084.pdf)); Austria requires an online posting notification and applies collective-agreement wages ([Czech MFA guide](https://mzv.gov.cz/file/4668948/Legislativni_nalezitosti_vysilani_pracovniku_a_pusobeni_ceskych_OSVC_a_firem_v_Rakousku_verze_listopad_2022.pdf)). Whether machine installation and commissioning fall into Germany's notifiable sectors, whether German craft rules require a further notification, and how Czech electrical qualifications are recognised abroad were not established. Under the machinery rules, instructions must also be supplied in the official language of the country of use ([BOZPprofi](https://www.bozpprofi.cz/33/pruvodni-dokumentace-strojnich-zarizeni-uniqueidmRRWSbk196FNf8-jVUh4Egny2sdjJ1sRjWiM1G86sLqw9PBWuC7BmQ/)).

## 5. AI cuts desk hours today, not commissioning

### Engineering is a fifth to a half of the price, so AI moves cost by single digits and schedule by weeks

No authoritative breakdown of hours by phase exists. Integrator guides put hardware at 40–70% of a turnkey budget, engineering, software and integration at 20–45%, and installation, commissioning and training at 10–25%, with the engineering share shrinking for standardised cells ([Unchained Robotics](https://blog.unchainedrobotics.de/en/how-much-does-a-turnkey-robotics-solution-cost)). One integrator timeline runs 6–10 weeks of engineering, 8–14 of build and 4–6 of on-site installation and commissioning ([LANPDT](https://lanpdt.com/tech-talk-episode-19/)). On those figures, cutting desk engineering by 30–50% lowers total project cost by perhaps 5–15%; this is an inference from marketing-grade data. The larger lever is schedule and risk: software that arrives on site already tested against a simulation shortens commissioning, where vendor case studies of virtual commissioning report reductions from 21% to 75% ([machineering](https://www.machineering.com/en/virtual-commissioning-why-the-investment-often-pays-for-itself-in-just-three-months/), [Visual Components](https://www.visualcomponents.com/blog/increasing-control-software-quality-with-virtual-commissioning/)).

### Where agents work: text artefacts with an executable check

**PLC code in Structured Text is the strongest case, and the harness matters more than the model.** Research from 2024 showed that wrapping generation in compilers and model checkers raised success from 47% to 72% ([LLM4PLC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05443v1)). The October 2026 PLCWorld benchmark, which runs generated code against simulated plant behaviour, found a frontier model used directly reaching **82.7% task success on easy cases but 25.1% on hard ones**, and names the gap between code that passes checks and code that later fails the task or violates safety ([PLCWorld](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02982)). The research frontier is now "verification-gated" agent harnesses that declare a task done only when logged external checks confirm it ([SemaPLC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.18565)), which is the kind of system your skills build.

The tooling is open to outsiders. CODESYS sells a server that lets any compatible model read, create and edit Structured Text and read compile errors, and says Claude models gave the best results in its internal tests ([CODESYS](https://www.codesys.com/ecosystem/release-lifecycle/releases-updates/development-system-mcp-server/), [CODESYS](https://www.codesys.com/products/engineering/ai-supported-engineering/)). Siemens made its Eigen Engineering Agent generally available in April 2026 to TIA Portal's 600,000-plus users ([Siemens](https://press.siemens.com/global/en/pressrelease/siemens-launches-eigen-engineering-agent-bringing-purpose-built-ai-industrial)); Beckhoff has announced an equivalent ([Beckhoff](https://www.beckhoff.com/en-au/company/news/multimedia-twincat-coagent.html)); and open-source bridges connect general coding agents to TIA Portal with dry-run gating before anything touches a controller ([tia-portal-mcp](https://github.com/Czarnak/tia-portal-mcp)). Siemens' claims of two to five times faster task execution come from its own pilots; **no independent productivity measurement of any PLC assistant was found** ([Automation.com](https://www.automation.com/article/siemens-eigen-engineering-agent-purpose-built-ai-industrial-automation-engineering)).

Robot programs follow the same pattern with a simulator as the verifier. RoboDK publishes agent skills that teach coding agents to build cells and generate controller programs for ABB, FANUC, KUKA and UR ([RoboDK](https://github.com/RoboDK)), and academic systems pair generation with a digital-twin check because syntactically correct code can be kinematically invalid ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/16/8/3883)). Vendor assistants are still guidance tools, as with ABB's ([Robotics Tomorrow](https://www.roboticstomorrow.com/news/2025/09/26/abb-robotics-adds-generative-ai-assistant-to-robotstudio/25605)). No measured production results exist.

Documentation and quoting are the other strong fits. Hazard lists, draft risk-assessment tables, manuals, translations and the technical-file index are text assembled from the project's own bill of materials, schematics and code, and AI-first tools already target this in Germany ([CE-Copilot](https://www.ce-copilot.de/en/guides/risk-assessment-software)). Specialists counter that AI cannot deliver a complete, compliant risk assessment alone, partly because harmonised standards are paywalled and models do not reliably know them ([highdoc](https://www.highdoc.de/ce-kennzeichnung-und-risikobeurteilung-mit-ki/)), and test houses stress that responsibility stays with the manufacturer ([Intertek](https://www.intertek.com/industrial-equipment/machinery-regulation)). Your agents therefore need licensed standards text and your own validated hazard library in context. Quoting is unpaid engineering, so automating concept, feasibility and costing has a direct margin effect; products exist ([ReshapeX](https://roboticsandautomationnews.com/2025/06/24/reshape-launches-ai%e2%80%91powered-sales-platform-for-industrial-automation-sector/92534), [Botnes](https://www.automate.org/companies/botnes)) but none reports measured accuracy, and estimate accuracy depends on a database of your own past projects that you do not yet have. Electrical design is "configure, then generate" even without language models: EPLAN generates schematics from configuration and estimates up to 40% time savings, a vendor figure ([EPLAN](https://eplan-software.com/company/innovations)).

AI perception is the most production-proven use in the cell itself, and you buy it. Vendors that remove per-part vision teaching report hundreds of deployed systems, such as Inbolt on 200-plus robots in over 100 factories ([EU-Startups](https://www.eu-startups.com/2026/09/paris-based-inbolt-raises-e11-million-to-give-industrial-robots-real-time-vision-and-intelligence)) and Sereact with 200-plus systems ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/04/27/german-robotics-startup-sereact-raises-110m/)), mostly at large automotive and logistics customers. At least one such vendor sells explicitly through integrators ([Technobezz](https://www.technobezz.com/news/trener-robotics-raises-32-million-dollars-for-its-robot-agnostic-ai-platform)).

### Where agents do not work: geometry, ladder, safety and the shop floor

Mechanical design is the least AI-ready desk discipline: on a May 2026 benchmark of parametric CAD, the best frontier model scored about **0.40**, recovering coarse geometry but not faithful parametric programs ([BenchCAD](https://benchcad.github.io/BenchCAD_webpage/)), and no production-proven generative tool for fixtures or grippers was found. Ladder and other graphical PLC languages remain weak: the CODESYS server cannot create or modify graphical objects ([CODESYS](https://www.codesys.com/ecosystem/release-lifecycle/releases-updates/development-system-mcp-server/)). Safety code is fenced off by design; Rockwell's assistant "can explain, but cannot modify, safety-related PLC code" ([Rockwell](https://www.rockwellautomation.com/en-us/docs/factorytalk-design-studio/current/contents-ditamap/factorytalk-design-studio-help/before-you-begin/factorytalk-design-studio-copilot.html)). Exception handling, the failed sensor, the operator intervention and the restart condition, is most of real PLC code and the part generated code misses ([ManufacturingTomorrow](https://www.manufacturingtomorrow.com/article/2026/09/what-if-plc-programming-started-with-a-sentence/27993)).

Robot foundation models are not a tool for a project-based integrator yet. A Siemens factory study needed **2,535 on-site episodes and about two weeks on the floor** to adapt one model to a single pick-and-insert task ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27461)), and researchers warn that such models achieve high single-trial success yet deteriorate under repetition ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.18813)). For a fixed-price cell with uptime commitments, that trades programming hours for data-collection hours and statistical reliability. Everything physical stays manual: installation, I/O checkout, commissioning against real parts, gripper iteration, safety validation and sign-off, cycle-time tuning and customer change requests. Trust is also low: in a 2026 survey of 107 control engineers, 61% reported low or no trust in AI-driven controls ([Control Engineering](https://www.controleng.com/research-how-industrial-ai-is-impacting-the-plant-floor/)).

### Position as the fast, fixed-price, well-documented integrator of a standard cell family

Five implications follow. First, "we use AI to write PLC code" is not a differentiator when Siemens ships an agent to every TIA Portal user; the durable assets are a library of verified modules, simulation test benches and project context the generic agent lacks. Second, leverage is highest on a constrained, repeatable cell family, so standardise and choose text-first stacks: Structured Text on CODESYS or Beckhoff, Siemens through its text-based and API routes, and UR with a simulator. Third, sell outcomes the customer can check: a quote with a reach and cycle-time study in days, a fixed price, fewer on-site days, and complete Czech documentation at handover. The survey finding that SMEs have "no one to consult" makes a fast, concrete feasibility answer the natural front door. Fourth, keep AI out of the safety function and make delivered code readable by the customer's maintenance staff, which answers both the Regulation and the trust problem. Fifth, be clear about what kind of company this is. Investors in 2026 funded product and robots-as-a-service companies, with USD 100–200 million rounds at RobCo, Vention and Standard Bots, which sell standard cells directly ([The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/robco-raises-100m-scale-industrial-automation/), [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/06/09/standard-bots-raises-200m-1b-valuation-revolutionize-ai-native-industrial-robotics/)); no well-funded "AI-native integrator" of custom cells was found. Those platforms compete with you on the simplest palletizing and tending cells. The less contested ground is the middle: customer-specific cells with a PLC, peripherals, safety and process know-how, which is where agent leverage on code, documents, quoting and electrical generation applies. A project-based AI-first integrator is a services firm with better margins unless it turns a cell family or its engineering software into a product.

## Conclusion

The research changes the shape of the problem in three ways. The binding constraints for a software founder are not the ones a software founder expects: they are a person with a multi-year electrical qualification, a deposit schedule that covers hardware, and acceptance criteria written as numbers. Those are solved by partnering and contracting, not by learning faster. Timing is an advantage rather than a burden: you enter exactly as the machinery and product-liability rules change, so you build one set of templates for the new regime while incumbents revise old ones, and a harness that assembles compliant documentation from project data is worth more in 2027 than it was in 2025. And the market thesis is plausible but untested: the adoption gap and the "no one to consult" finding point to under-served small shops, yet no source shows established integrators refusing small jobs, and the cobot distributors already sell cells at CZK 0.7–1.2 million.

The first 90 days should therefore test the thesis and the partnerships, not build the company. Kit-based cells are the way to earn references and commissioning experience; the business worth owning sits one step up, in customer-specific cells built from a standard family with software that arrives on site already tested. Decide after the first handful of cells whether you are building a services firm with unusually good margins or a product, because the funding, the hiring and the liability profile differ.

## The 90-day starting plan (9 November 2026 to early February 2027)

The plan has one goal: by day 90, hold evidence on demand, a partner set that makes delivery legal and safe, and a demo cell with its own documentation, so that the decision to take a first paid cell rests on facts. Delivering a customer cell inside 90 days is not the goal; the competence timeline does not support it. Four dated events fall inside the window: the reported new dependent-work definition on 1 December 2026, the SIGMA DC1 window reported as 3 December 2026 to 27 January 2027, the Product Liability Directive on 9 December 2026 and the Machinery Regulation on 20 January 2027.

| Days | Actions | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| 1–14: set up and book | Start the free UR e-learning and run URSim in Docker; install CODESYS and write a first Structured Text sequence. Book UR Core Training in Prague or Brno and confirm its price. Book first meetings with an accountant (company form, VAT, contributions), a commercial lawyer (contract terms, trade licences), an insurance broker (quote request) and a machinery-safety consultant (fixed-scope quote for one cobot cell). Read the Danish Embassy feasibility study on Czech automation barriers. Pick a working hypothesis: one application (palletizing or machine tending), one or two regions, non-automotive shops. | E-learning track complete; four professional meetings in the calendar; written hypothesis of application, region and customer type |
| 15–30: legal shell and channel | Found the s.r.o. and register for VAT on the accountant's advice. Put the control-cabinet question to TIČR or an inspection technician in writing. Identify a candidate responsible representative and a panel builder. Meet at least three channel partners (the UR branch or Exactec, plus two of Roboticall, Controltech, Fairino Europe) and ask each about partner terms, demo units, and which jobs they turn away. Order the PLC bench (Siemens S7-1200 G2, Raspberry Pi with CODESYS, 24 V parts, emergency stop) and decide whether to buy a used UR. | Company registered or filed; written answer or scheduled answer on the electrical question; three distributor conversations logged; bench ordered |
| 31–60: customer discovery and harness | Visit or call 20 small manufacturers in the chosen regions with one question set: which manual task hurts most, batch sizes, shifts, who they asked before, what they were quoted. Build the engineering harness: an agent that edits a CODESYS project, compiles, runs it against a simulated plant and logs results; a URSim cell with a robot-to-PLC handshake; a first quoting template with a reach and cycle-time check. Attend UR Core Training. Decide with the accountant whether a SIGMA DC1 or TREND application is worth preparing. | 20 conversations written up with a count of real candidate tasks; at least two referral partners; harness passes its own test on one palletizing or tending sequence; training certificate |
| 61–90: demo cell and first commitment | Assemble a demo cell on the bench or with a borrowed or used cobot: gripper, sensors, emergency stop, PLC handshake. Produce its full document set to the 2027 Regulation and EN ISO 10218-2:2025 with agent assistance, and pay the safety consultant to review it. Draft standard contract terms with the lawyer: functional specification, numeric acceptance criteria, deposit covering hardware, stated commissioning days, liability cap. Bind insurance or hold a firm quote. Offer three fixed-price feasibility studies to the best candidates from discovery, each with layout, cycle-time estimate, price range, payback at 70% of projected benefit and grant fit. Book the safety certificate course. | Demo cell running a repeatable cycle; consultant's written review of the risk assessment; contract template; insurance quote; at least one paid feasibility study or signed letter of intent |

Budget the 90 days from the prices found, all of which need confirming: company formation at roughly CZK 5,500–11,000, UR Core Training at EUR 250 plus VAT if the Czech listing holds, a bench lab under about EUR 2,000 or about EUR 10,000–12,000 with a used UR, and a safety course at roughly GBP 1,950–2,300 on UK prices. Professional fees, the safety consultant's review and insurance are unknown until quoted.

Set the decision rule now. Proceed to a first paid cell if at least five of the 20 shops have a task that fits the standard cell, at least one will pay for a feasibility study, an electrical-qualified partner and a safety consultant are signed up, and the control-cabinet question has a clear answer. If the shops are interested but the partners are missing, sell programming and commissioning hours to existing integrators for six months while keeping the harness work going. If fewer than five shops have a fitting task, change the application or the region before spending on hardware.
